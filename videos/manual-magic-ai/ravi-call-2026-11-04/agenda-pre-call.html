1Live demo: how to show Manual Magic to a client

About 12 minutes, using one SOP the whole way through. Run it in this order with a client and the platform tells its own story.

  1. Open on the problem. Ask the client how many versions of their store-opening routine exist across their outlets today."If it isn't written down, it isn't usable."
  2. Dashboard. Progress for each of the six categories, Manual Health and Coverage. This is how the owner sees how far along the manual is.
  3. Settings: teach it the business. Business Details, Target Reader, then Brand Voice: paste a piece of the client's own writing and press Analyse. Finish with Preferences: logo and five theme colours."It sounds like you, not like a generic AI."
  4. Upload IP. Drag in a document and watch it go from ANALYSING to READY. It takes up to 200 files at a time.File: Demo files → Patisserie Penelope - Coffee Standards Flat White.pdf. This is the document the coffee SOP is built from in Q5.
  5. SOP Board. The Draft column fills with SOPs written from the client's own IP. Open "To open the store safely and on time".
  6. The SOP tabs. Editor, then Revise Draft with one line of feedback. Then Checklist, FAQ, Storyboard, Assets (the infographic) and the QR Code tab.
  7. Publish. Drag the card from Review to Approved to Published. Click MANUAL in the top bar to see what the team sees, then scan the QR code with your phone."Publish once and every outlet has the latest version straight away."
  8. Ask Your Wizard. Run the franchise-agreement gap analysis: "Compare the obligations in my uploaded franchise agreement with my published SOPs. List the franchisee obligations not yet covered."
  9. Close. Show the PDF export, then the next step: the free trial (up to 5 SOPs from one substantial document) at manualmagic.ai/try, the scorecard at scorecard.manualmagic.ai, and White Glove for clients who want it done properly from day one.

Habits that make a demo go smoothly

  • Set the account up the day before. Generate images in advance, because they take several minutes.
  • Use an example from the client's own sector: a café gets coffee, a QSR gets opening checks.
  • Lead with data safety before they ask: the platform runs on a private model, nothing uploaded is used to train any AI, and the data is hosted in London.
  • Never show another client's account or documents.

2White Glove: what you are offering

White Glove is expert-led consultancy that makes sure the manual the client builds is sound, franchise-ready and scalable from the start. It is the route for franchisors, enterprise customers and multi-site businesses that want extra assurance.

Phase 1 · 2 hoursPre-Subscription Evaluation

Reviews the client's existing content and IP and maps the business against what replication needs. Ends in a written Readiness Assessment.

Phase 2 · 2 hoursOnboarding and Architecture

Sets up the platform, brand voice and glossary, and designs the category structure and the order of the SOPs.

Phase 3 · 3 hoursPost-Onboarding Review

An expert review of the client's first 10 SOPs, with a written SOP Review Report that sets the standard for the rest of the build.

What it is not

  • It is not a manual-writing service. The client's team edits and approves every SOP.
  • It is not a design or print service. Covers, separators and a bilingual print layout are separate work (see below).

Q2How do we combine multiple chapters into an SOP?

Workaround today

The platform is built the other way round from a printed manual. One SOP covers one task. A "manual" in your samples, such as the 72-page Customer Service manual, becomes a set of SOPs in one category, grouped with a tag such as Customer Service. Its numbered sections (1.1, 1.2, …) become the SOPs.

  • To build one larger SOP from several processes: Editor → Revise Draft, and under Feedback type "Combine the opening, handover and close-down routines into one SOP, with a section for each."
  • To merge two existing SOPs: copy the text of one into the other's Editor, then delete the spare one from its Overview tab.
  • Export is one SOP at a time. There is no export of a whole category or the whole manual yet. We have asked for it.

Show: Revise Draft with the "combine" feedback on the store-opening SOP.

Q4How are spacing and alignment fixed?

Works today, by design

The platform sets spacing, alignment and fonts itself, so every SOP looks the same in every outlet. The toolbar controls structure, not layout. If spacing looks wrong, it is nearly always formatting pasted in from Word or email:

  • Select the text and press the Eraser (clear formatting).
  • Use H2 and H3 for headings, not bold text.
  • Use a numbered list for steps, bullets for everything else, and a table for items side by side.
  • Check the result in Preview.

Q5How do we bring visuals and pictures into an SOP, for example a coffee recipe?

Works today

  3. Film the trickiest stage and upload the clip the same way. Videos play on the page in Resources.
  4. Generate Image with the feedback "Step-by-step infographic of the six stages of making a flat white, numbered, one picture per stage."
  5. Storyboard → Generate plans a short training video; export it for whoever films it.
  6. QR Code → Download PNG and fix it to the coffee machine.

The pictures sit under the method in Resources, not between the steps (see Q1).

Q8How do we know whether content came from the client or was machine-generated?

Workaround today

Every draft is written from the client's own uploaded IP, shaped by their Business Details, Target Reader and Brand Voice. The platform does not mark individual sentences as written by AI or by a person. Control comes from four things:

  • Created by on the Overview tab shows who created the SOP.
  • History in the Editor shows previous editions, so you can see what changed.
  • Nothing reaches readers until a person moves it through Review and Approved. AI helps, humans decide.
  • Agree a tag such as Client-verified and add it once the client's expert has checked the SOP line by line. It shows on the card and in the manual.
Wesley only

Ravi's question is a fair one for the builders: a marker showing which uploaded document each section came from. Note it for Penny.

Q9How do I give a command to create a process, an infographic or a page, for example hand washing in a café?

Works today
  1. Add SOP on the Dashboard, then the orange + on the SOP Board.
  2. Objective: "To make sure every Team Member washes their hands correctly." Keep it short, because the Objective becomes the title.
  3. Category: Day-to-Day Operating Requirements. Steps: the ones you already follow. Tags: Hygiene, Food Safety. Create.
  4. Editor → Generate Draft, with Feedback: "Write a hand-washing SOP for café Team Members. Include when to wash, each step in order, how long, how to dry hands and what to do if supplies run out."
  5. Checklist → Generate. Assets → Generate Image: "Simple numbered infographic of each hand-washing step, one picture per step, for display above a sink."
  6. QR Code → Download PNG for the wall above every sink.

Quicker start: ask the Wizard "Draft a hand-washing procedure for café Team Members based on our hygiene documents" and paste the answer into a new SOP. Check any generated picture closely, because words and small details can come out wrong.

Show: the hand-washing SOP prepared before the call, with its finished infographic.

Q10How do we add country-specific food safety guidelines from a government authority?

Works today, if you upload first
  1. Download the current guidance from the authority's own website. For your markets that might be FSSAI (India), Dubai Municipality or ADAFSA (UAE), or the SFDA (Saudi Arabia).
  2. Upload it as IP.
  3. Ask the Wizard: "Compare our food safety SOPs with the uploaded [authority] guidance. List every gap as a prioritised action plan."
  4. Open each SOP that needs changes: Revise Draft, "Align this SOP with the uploaded [authority] guidance."
  5. Tag each SOP by country (IN, UAE, KSA) so every region finds its own version, and add the title and date of the guidance it follows.
  6. Have a qualified person review it before approval, and set a date to check the authority's site for updates.

Always upload the regulation first. Without it, the Wizard will still answer from general knowledge, and it doesn't say where that knowledge came from.

4Your library across the six categories

Your list is thorough. One shift makes it work on the platform: Manual Magic builds the Operations Manual your client's franchisees run the business from. Your library also holds the franchisor's development and sales documents. Those are valuable, but they are a different job. Here is a suggested three-way split to go through together.

Becomes SOPs

Upload as IP. The platform drafts SOPs from them.

  • SOP manuals: Food Safety, Cleaning, Customer Service, Product, Barista, Store Ops, BOH, FOH, Central Kitchen, Ops Checklist (38–47) → Day-to-Day
  • Supply Chain, Accounting, Training guidelines (48–51) → Day-to-Day
  • HR Handbook and HR Manual (36–37) → Your Team
  • New Store Opening, Pre-Opening Checklist (34–35) and the Design Manuals (1–2) → New Business Set-Up
  • Brandbook (13) → Marketing & Promotion

Franchise development and sales work, which is your consultancy's own product.
