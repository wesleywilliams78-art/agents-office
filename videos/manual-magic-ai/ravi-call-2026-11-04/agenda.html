1Set the business up before any SOP

The most common mistake is jumping straight into SOPs. Without the set-up, the SOPs come out generic. Ask every client to do this first, or do it with them on the first call. Allow about 30 minutes.

Open it from Improve SOP generation on the Dashboard, or from Settings (the cog).

PanelWhat it asksWhy it matters
Business DetailsWhat the business does, its model and sector, how customers engage, the standard roles in a unit, the regulations and brand standards that apply, the critical equipment.The platform is global, so it needs to know which rules and roles apply to this client.
Target ReaderWho reads the SOPs, the jobs and KPIs they must hit, where they read them (phone, laptop, kitchen screen), and their baseline capability.Baseline capability is the most important answer. It makes the SOPs make sense to the person doing the job.
Brand VoiceLanguage, learning style, an example of the brand's writing (press Analyse), personality sliders, three words, and tones to avoid.It tells the platform how to write, not only what to write. A law firm is formal, a hospitality brand is relaxed.
PreferencesFive theme colours and the client's logo.It removes the Manual Magic branding, so the manual is the client's own.

Arabic is on the Brand Voice language list.

2The Dashboard

  • Manual Health is a score for the manual as it stands, across everything uploaded. It is a quick read on how usable the manual is today.
  • Coverage is how much of a complete operations manual's structure already has content, based on Penny's forty years of building manuals. Coverage of 35% means 35% is covered and 65% is still to write. Check it after each upload: if it doesn't move, the new document probably repeats what is already there.
  • Click any of the six categories to open the SOP Board filtered to that category.

Your point that "Coverage" reads as "already covered" when people are looking for the gap has been passed to Manual Magic.

3Building an SOP: hand-washing

  1. New card. On the SOP Board, the orange + at the top right. Give the objective, a high-level description, the category, the steps if you have them, and tags (for example store manager, franchise owner, finance, legal). Create.
  2. Generate Draft. Editor → Generate Draft. The SOP is written in seconds from the set-up and the uploaded IP.
  3. Review it as the owner. Minimum standards, roles and responsibilities, the step-by-step procedure, risk controls, how it is measured and improved, and the tools and resources needed.
  4. Add your own photos. Assets → Upload asset, for example the hand-wash sinks in your own store.
  5. Generate a visual. Assets → Generate Image, and describe it under Feedback ("Produce a flowchart of the process"). It takes 5 to 7 minutes, so start it early.
  6. Preview. The full SOP, with the photos and the generated flowchart in Resources at the foot of the page.
  7. Checklist and FAQ. Their own tabs. Generate them only where they help (a closing checklist, for example). Not every SOP needs one.
  8. Design and Export. One SOP as a PDF, with or without the checklist and FAQ. You get an email when it's ready, and the file is also saved in the SOP's Assets. Export only the SOPs you want as wall posters. Everything else lives digitally.

4The SOP Board and the live manual

  • Every new SOP starts in Draft. Drag the card through Review, Approved and Published. A business with layers might have the L&D manager draft, the operations manager review and the operations director approve.
  • Once Published, the SOP is visible to everyone with access. Click MANUAL in the top bar to see the six categories and the SOPs in each, as the team sees them on a phone or laptop.
  • Drag SOPs into the order you want. With hundreds of SOPs, readers use the search bar.
  • Only the admin login can edit SOPs.

5Ask Your Wizard and Upload IP

  • Upload IP (the cloud icon): drag and drop the client's material. That includes loosely structured documents, the franchise agreement and the local government's food safety standards. PDF, Word, .txt or .md, up to 100 MB a file and 200 files a batch. READY means it has been read, and the manual builds itself around it.
  • Ask Your Wizard finds the gaps. In the demo we asked how protected the business is on food safety legislation. It confirmed the strengths for the UK, Dubai and India from the uploaded standards, and named the gaps: bring in a HACCP policy, and add a pest control procedure.
  • Upload the regulations for each country the client trades in, or do it for them as part of your service. Every gap the Wizard finds is work a client may ask you to help with.

6Worked example: making coffee in the store

  1. Load the standard as IP. Upload the brand's coffee standard (we used a flat white standards card) through Upload IP. If the client doesn't have one written down, someone has to write it first. That is the brand's IP.
  2. Create the SOP. Orange + → "Coffee production", Day-to-Day Operating Requirements, tags Barista and Coffee. No need to type the steps, because they are already in the uploaded standard.
  3. Generate Draft. It produced the steps in order: prepare the equipment, select and weigh the beans, set the grind and grind to order, and so on.
  4. Add the pictures. Assets → Upload asset → one photo per stage: grind, dose, tamp, extract, steam, serve.
  5. Preview. The photos sit in Resources under the method.

Tips from the call

  • Have someone in the business take the photos, and put the key numbers on the picture before it is uploaded: "22 g", "froth 5 seconds for a cappuccino, 7 for a latte". Images are edited outside the platform.
  • A barista making coffee won't read a manual, so the visual does the work.
  • Video: a short clip uploaded to Assets plays on the page in Resources. For video broken into captioned step-by-step guides, Manual Magic partners with Manual.to, a separate platform that suits networks of 20 or more outlets.

Q1Clients want to benchmark against best practice, for example Starbucks or Costa. Can the tool help?

Use the client's own research

Starbucks' and Costa's manuals are their intellectual property, so they can't be loaded. The client's own research can: notes from visiting those stores and watching the steps belong to the client, and can be uploaded as IP. The Wizard will then compare the manual against them, and against any regulations or sector standards uploaded. And the goal is the manual that serves this business, not the biggest one. A 2,000-page manual that the team uses a quarter of is wasted work.

Q2How is the final manual sequenced, chapter one, chapter two?

Works today

The six categories are the chapters, and they never change. Every SOP falls into one of the six. Inside each, drag the SOPs into the order you want readers to follow, for example site selection, then design, then CAD drawings, then equipment. Readers either scroll the ordered list or search.

Q3Can several sections (introduction, hand-washing, COVID protocol, pest control) become one hygiene SOP? Can two files be merged?

Works today

Yes. Editor → Revise Draft, and under Feedback: "Merge these SOPs [names] into one, with a section for each task and role." Then delete the spare copies. The platform also splits content sensibly by itself. In one manual, the profit and loss process went into Business Development, Growth & Profit, while stock counting became its own SOP in Day-to-Day, tagged to management accounts. Hand-washing becomes a team procedure and also a line on the manager's shift checklist. If it suggests an SOP you don't need, delete it. It advises, you decide.

Q4A barista needs the steps, the grams, when to add milk and how long to froth, with a picture for each step. How?

Works today

See the worked example in section 6. Load the standard, generate the SOP, add one photo per stage with the numbers written on it, and repeat for each drink (cappuccino, macchiato, latte). Each drink is its own SOP, ordered in the category.

Q5Ingredient quantities, sometimes a secret formula (masala, biryani). Can they go in a table, or must they be uploaded first?

Workaround today

The SOP is about process. Nobody reads a whole SOP to find the amount of coriander. Put the ingredients and quantities in the SOP's Checklist tab, where they can be edited freely, so the team has a quick list for, say, masala chai. Load the recipe as IP first if you want the platform to draft it. A truly secret formula can stay with the owner, outside the manual.

Where the chef holds the recipes in their head, they have to be part of building the manual. They should be driving that part of it.

Q6Can the whole manual be downloaded as one printed, chaptered book for the owner, an investor or an audit?

Not offered yet

Not today. Export is one SOP at a time, because the platform was built digital-first. We have passed on the need for a whole-manual export. For a client who wants the bound manual on the shelf, or something tangible to show an investor or an auditor, produce a print edition from the approved SOPs: your designer working from your master files (cover, inner cover, back cover, separators), or Manual Writers International Consultancy Services.

Q7Numbering, headings, sub-numbering and appendices, so a design manual's chapters link up?

Workaround today
  • Put the number at the start of the SOP title (for example "1.12 Bathroom tile specification"), then drag the SOPs into that order in the manual.
  • Inside an SOP, H1, H2 and H3 headings give the heading and subheading structure.
  • Tag related SOPs (for example Design Manual) so they filter together on the SOP Board.
  • An appendix is its own SOP, placed last in the category.

We are asking Manual Magic whether a numbering convention can be set once for a whole manual.

Q9The POS and other software?

Works today

Manual Magic doesn't connect to the POS or hold its data. The SOPs cover how to use the system and the flow it drives: the order comes through, it goes to the barista, and takeaway and delivery each follow their own process.

Q10Does it work for retail, such as clothing with visual merchandising?

Works today

Yes. It works for any multi-site business. Visual merchandising standards suit photos in Assets: the reference display, shown beneath the procedure.

Q11What does History show? Can we see who changed what, and spot the difference?

Partly today

Editor → History keeps every saved edition with its date and time, including individual changes. With more than one licence, it shows who made each change. It does not yet highlight what changed between two editions. That would save a lot of back and forth with clients, so we have passed it to the tech team.

Q12Is there a final client approval, or a lock so they must approve before moving on?

Workaround · confirming

There is no lock today. The working method: the client moves an SOP to Approved, and you move it to Published, so the final sign-off is yours. In practice, have the client upload all of their IP and build the first SOPs themselves. You review those (the White Glove review covers the first 10), and once they've shown they can do it, step back. Building the whole manual for a client who hasn't handed over their IP leads to friction. We are asking whether an alert on Approved, or a Published lock, is possible.

Q13If retail audit scores (GoAudit, iAuditor) are fed in each quarter, will it suggest SOP improvements?

Confirming with Penny

Not inside this portal. Manual Magic AI works inside the Claromentis Franchise Hub, which adds audits and training alongside the manual. An audit recorded there flags the SOPs that need updating, so the brand keeps improving. Cost and how it is bought: we are confirming with Penny.

Q14Is there a limit, for example 35 manuals?

Confirming with Penny

There is no limit on SOPs. Two thousand is fine. The numbers on each category on the Dashboard (Draft 0 · Review 0 · Approved 0 / 6) are the suggested core SOPs for that category, not a cap. We are confirming with Penny that there is no limit on manuals either.

Q15Translation, for example Arabic?

Works today

Arabic is on the Brand Voice language list. You already have in-house translators, and most manuals will be in English. If a client needs translation, Manual Writers International can quote, so you can compare costs.
