# The Operator's Ledger: series brief for motion

Read this before making any Operator's Ledger episode. It condenses the series bible (`videos/operators-ledger/DESIGN.md`) and the script skill (`operators-ledger-episode`), adapted to the Motion Studio. Where it disagrees with `motion.md`, **this wins** for this series. Ask `motion_series` with `episode: N` for that week's plan row.

## What it is
The Monday explainer from WRW Hospitality, 95 to 120 seconds: one MBA concept made useful to owners, operators and founders scaling a multi-site hospitality business. Narrated in Wesley's voice. **The test:** if the episode could run on any business channel unchanged, it is not finished. The textbook takes one sentence; the operator angle takes the rest.

## The story: a puzzle, solved by the framework, verdict last (25 Oct 2026)
Never open by defining the model. **60 to 90 seconds** for the feed (about 140 to 200 words), one line per scene.
1. **The hook** (first 3 s, on screen as text too, since most watch muted): an operator's dilemma with a stake the viewer could get wrong in their own business. Frame 0 shows it, finished and still.
2. **The stakes**, in pounds or sites.
3. **The clues:** the framework walked as clues, one per scene, each shifting the odds; the framework named once, as the tool, with its author and year.
4. **The verdict, last:** an operator's call, never bending the framework.
5. **The lesson and the question for Monday** (red ground).
6. **The ledger page:** one new ledger line writes itself in: `Case NN · Framework: … · Verdict: … · Lesson: …`.
7. **End card** (ink, below).
The season question runs underneath every episode: "Will Athena take her café from 1 site to 10?" **Hooks and retainers:** every scene ends on a line that opens a small loop the next scene closes; concrete specifics; the answer withheld until earned.

Script rules: sentences of 6 to 19 words, calm and plain, like explaining to one owner across a table; no hype, no "game-changer", no lists of three. British English, £ with comma thousands, a true minus sign on screen (−£300); numbers spelled out in the narration. The series world compounds: SITE 1 (thriving, £20,000 a week, £4,800 four-wall profit), SITE 10 (struggling, £13,000 a week, −£300, past break-even after Episode 04), Athena the Operator. Every number reconciles. Never attribute a claim you are not sure of; if a source is uncertain, say so to Wesley instead of guessing. If Wesley gives a script, use it word for word.

**The promise (series device, 25 Oct 2026):** the opening ends on "This is the Operator's Ledger. One puzzle, one framework, and one verdict." — "one puzzle" lifting, "one framework" lifting again, "one verdict" coming down. Record it fresh in the cloned voice and choose the take by measured pitch (several seeds; the small "and" is what gives the natural up, up, down). **Never pitch-shift or reshape the voice**: Wesley heard a Rubber Band-shaped take as butchered (25 Oct 2026).

**The title slot (Wesley, 25 Oct 2026):** the opening card's eyebrow carries the episode's topic every week, `THE OPERATOR'S LEDGER · <TOPIC>` (e.g. UNIT ECONOMICS), never a case number; the ledger page at the end keeps the case number. Use "the verdict" consistently, never "the call".

## Format and layout
- **1080 × 1350 (4:5), 30 fps.** Safe area: the centred 1080 square, y 135 to 1215, margins about 86 px. All text, numbers, characters and captions stay inside it; only grounds and mountains reach the full frame.
- **Text is flush left,** never centred, except the captions. No more than about 8 words on screen at once outside the captions.
- **Type:** Newsreader Light for headlines and big numerals (sentence case, never bold); Archivo for labels, chart numbers and captions with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on every counting number; kickers in Archivo caps, tracking .22em.

## Apple clarity (decided 25 Oct 2026): sharper, not different
Wesley's direction: make the episodes look sharper using Apple's design philosophy (apple.com is the reference), **without changing the colours, fonts or brand rules**. What that means on screen:
- **One idea, one hero per frame.** Every frame has a single hero (the chart, the number, Athena, the shop) shown large and given room. Anything that is not helping the line being spoken right now leaves the frame. On screen at once: at most the hero, one headline or label group, and the caption.
- **Confident scale contrast, three sizes only.** A big, calm display size (Newsreader Light, 96 to 160 px for the key number or line), a small precise label size (Archivo caps 20 to 24 px), and the caption. Nothing in between. Key figures are heroes in Apple's stat pattern: the number huge, its descriptor small beneath it ("£4,800" over "FOUR-WALL PROFIT, WEEKLY").
- **Two-tone headlines.** The statement in ink, the continuation in warm grey #4A4644, in one line: "Unit economics. What one site actually keeps." The full stop does the work; no colons, no exclamation marks.
- **Eyebrows.** A tiny Archivo caps kicker above each headline (deep red on bone, bone on red): "SITE 10", "THE TRAP", "BREAK-EVEN".
- **Space and a strict grid.** One left edge for every piece of text in a scene (x 86 inside the safe area), a vertical rhythm in multiples of 8 px, generous empty space around the hero, nothing crowding anything else. Fill the frame by scaling the hero up, never by adding more things. The lower third above the caption band is not left as a dead strip: the hero or the baseline lives there.
- **Precision.** Hairline rules 2 to 3 px, labels aligned exactly to the bar segments they name, tabular figures, equal gaps, consistent stroke weights; no element touching or overlapping another unless it is meant to.
- **Progressive disclosure, a still hero.** Like Apple's product pages, the hero stays put while the explanation builds around it: the chart holds its place and its parts light up and label themselves as the voice names them, rather than new charts replacing old ones.
- **Calm, purposeful motion.** Elements arrive with a short rise (24 to 40 px) and fade in on a critically damped spring over about half a second, one at a time, on the word. A slow push (2 to 4% over a scene) can bring the eye to the hero. Nothing spins, bounces or flies across the frame.
- **Few words on screen.** Labels of one to four words, headlines under seven; the narration and captions carry the rest.

## Colour
Bone #F3F2F2 default ground; ink #201E1D for line art, numbers, text, the caption band and the profit segment; red #EC3013 for statement grounds (cold open, the question), emphasis and loss; deep red #B6240E for red text under 32 px on bone. **Profit is ink (bone on red), loss is red, never green.** Character palette for characters, buildings, props and chart cost segments only: sand #E9D8BD, stone #C8BFB3, ochre #F2C14E, sage #7E9C82, blush #F4A28C, mustard #E3A23B / #B9781F. Athena never wears red. No gradients, shadows, glows or textures.

## Motion: calm and precise
The numbers must be readable. Springs, critically damped: `snap` for entrances and labels, `calm` for bars and stacks growing from the baseline, never `pop`, `drop` or `wobble`. Numbers count up and land with no overshoot (`M.tween` with `power3.out` for the count, tabular figures). One main movement per beat; secondary moves (a blink, a bob, a sign settling) may run alongside. Hold every key line a second or more. **The one allowed overshoot:** the WRW logo stamping on the end card (scale 1.15 to 1 and a 3° rotation settling, spring `weight`). Cue everything from the narration's word timings (`M.cue`) so the picture changes on the word.

**Colour changes are crafted transitions, never a bang** (the studio-wide rule; it replaces DESIGN.md's old "hard colour wipe or simple cut"). In this world: the red ground drains down into the baseline as the chart rises out of it; a column of the chart grows until its colour fills the frame; the ledger's page turns to the next ground; Athena's ledger ribbon pulls the red across; the shop awnings' stripes widen into the ground. Half a second to a second, on the word, with a soft whoosh or page sound.

## Captions (Wesley's choice, 25 Oct 2026)
No box. Word-synced from the narration (`M.caption`, at most six words a chunk), Archivo Medium about 48 px set straight onto the frame, centred, low in the safe area: ink on bone grounds and bone on ink grounds (read the ground beneath them each frame, so they follow the colour transitions). The word being spoken is brand red #EC3013 and lifts into place on a `snap` spring as it is said; words still to come wait at about 26% opacity. No bouncing marker over the words: a square with a trail was tried and removed as too busy (Wesley, 25 Oct 2026). They never sit over other text (the engine checks). The engine writes the `.srt`.

## End card (every episode, 3 s hold)
Ink ground, `mountains-bone-on-ink.svg` drawing in along the bottom. Flush left: kicker "THE OPERATOR'S LEDGER", the episode title in Newsreader, the WRW logo stamping in, "WRW HOSPITALITY" as an Archivo kicker, then "Wesley Williams". No job title. YSF Advisory never appears.

## Sound
- **Narration is the one audio file allowed in this series,** and only through the studio: make it with `motion_narrate` (Wesley's cloned voice, VoiceStudio VoxCPM2, de-plosived and word-timed with Whisper), or from Wesley's own recording. Place it with `voice: [{ line, at }]` in `M.scene`; the engine mixes it, ducks the music under it and finishes the loudness at −15 LUFS. Never an `<audio>` tag. No scene's narration may run more than 1 s past its slot.
- **Music and effects are synthesised** (`score(S)`): a subtle, low bed with no melody fighting the voice (`S.pad`, `S.sub`, a soft `S.pluck` motif, brushed `S.hat` at most), a soft `S.tick` when a number lands, a low `S.taiko` or `S.sub` thud when a loss appears, a whoosh into each crafted transition. Tempo that divides 30 fps (90 or 120 bpm).
- A stand-in voice may be used for drafts while VoiceStudio is off, but **a final needs Wesley's voice**: the studio refuses a final with a stand-in.
