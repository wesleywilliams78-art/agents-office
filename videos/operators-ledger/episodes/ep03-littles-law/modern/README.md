- **What it is:** pays off Episode 02's "Next case: why the lunch queue walks out." Athena's site ten has a queue out of the door every lunchtime and still loses £300 a week (Episode 01). Clues on one live queue: the till serves two a minute; ten guests a day see the line and walk on; each would have spent £8.50. Little's Law in three parts (the queue, the rate, the wait), each lit on the queue as it is named; then on ink: 12 in the queue ÷ 2 a minute = a 6-minute wait, and 10 × £8.50 × 6 days = £510 a week walking out. Verdict: don't add staff all day; move one person from prep to a second till, twelve to one; three a minute and the same queue waits four minutes; site ten turns a profit. The Monday card (the blank 12:10 queue count, the Wednesday QUEUE tool) holds through the sign-off. End card: "Next case: who is site ten for?" (Episode 04, positioning).
- **Ledger line (off screen):** `Case 03 · Framework: Little's Law · Verdict: A second till, twelve to one · Lesson: The queue isn't the problem; the wait is.`
- **Numbers (illustrative, weekly):** wait = queue ÷ rate: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 minutes; at 3 a minute, 12 ÷ 3 = 4 minutes. Walk-aways: 10 a day × £8.50 × 6 days = £510 a week. At 70p of each pound left after food (Episode 01), £510 back is £357, against site ten's £300 weekly loss: +£57 a week. The second till moves an existing person from prep (no added labour). Little's Law holds for a stable queue's long-run averages; the episode keeps to one lunchtime queue with the till as the bottleneck.
