# Episode 03 Brief — Little's Law
**Series:** The Operator's Ledger · **Plan row:** 03 · **Monday:** 9 Nov 2026 · **Working title:** "The money walking out of your lunch queue"
**Module:** Operations · **Source:** John D. C. Little, "A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW", *Operations Research* 9(3), 383–387, 1961 (DOI 10.1287/opre.9.3.383).

## The angle
Little's Law says the number in a queue equals the arrival rate times the time spent waiting. Turned round, two numbers an owner can count at lunch (the queue, and how fast the till serves) give the wait. The episode links the wait to guests who give up, and those guests almost exactly fill SITE 10's £520 break-even gap from Episode 01.

## Numbers check
- **The wait:** 12 ÷ 2 a minute = 6 minutes. At 3 a minute, 12 ÷ 3 = 4 minutes.
- **Walk-aways:** 10 a day × £8.50 × 6 days = £510 a week.
- **SITE 10's gap:** break-even £13,520 (Episode 01) − sales £13,000 = £520. £510 ÷ £520 = 98%.
- **Labelling:** the walk-away count and the £8.50 spend are illustrative and labelled on screen.

## Sources and caveats
- **Little (1961):** the law holds for long-run averages in a stable queue. "Wait = queue ÷ the rate you serve" uses the serving rate as the throughput, which is fair while the till is busy and the queue isn't growing. The script keeps to that case: one moment, one queue.
- **Walking away:** the script says "long enough for some guests to give up". It doesn't claim a researched abandonment rate.
