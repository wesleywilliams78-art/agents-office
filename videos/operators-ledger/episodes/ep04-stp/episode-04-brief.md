# Episode 04 Brief — STP: segment, target, position
**Series:** The Operator's Ledger · **Plan row:** 04 · **Monday:** 16 Nov 2026 · **Working title:** "Who is each of your sites actually for?"
**Module:** Marketing · **Source:** Wendell R. Smith, "Product Differentiation and Market Segmentation as Alternative Marketing Strategies", *Journal of Marketing*, July 1956, pp. 3–8. Kotler credits Smith with introducing the term. STP (segmentation, targeting, positioning) is the standard sequence in marketing textbooks such as Kotler & Keller, *Marketing Management*.

## The angle
Multi-site owners do STP once, for the brand, then run the same menu, hours and rota everywhere. But each site's street is its own market. SITE 1 sits by a station; SITE 10 sits on a residential street and is running SITE 1's menu. Positioning SITE 10 for its own guests takes it past the break-even line from Episode 01.

## Numbers check
- **Saturday sales:** 200 × £7 = £1,400. 200 × £11 = £2,200. The difference is £800, from one Saturday a week.
- **SITE 10's weekly sales:** £13,000 + £800 = £13,800, which is above the £13,520 break-even (Episode 01).
- **Profit at £13,800,** using Episode 01's cost structure: 13,800 × (1 − 5,500/13,000) − 7,800 ≈ +£162 a week. That assumes brunch keeps the same food-cost percentage, so the brief says "past break-even" rather than quoting a profit.
