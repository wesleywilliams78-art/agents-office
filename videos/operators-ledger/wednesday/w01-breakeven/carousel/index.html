What does one site need to break even?
A four-step worksheet. One site, ten minutes, your own numbers.
SWIPE FOR THE STEPS1 / 8
STEP 1
Add up the fixed costs
Fixed costs stay the same in a quiet week. Use weekly figures.
SITE 10, THE EXAMPLE FROM MONDAY
Core labour£5,200
Rent and service charge£2,600
Fixed costs a week£7,800
The test: would this cost fall if next week were quiet? If not, it’s fixed.

STEP 2
Find the share that moves with sales
Variable costs as a % of sales: food and drink, packaging, card fees, delivery commission.
SITE 10: SALES £13,000 A WEEK
Food and drink, £3,90030.0%
Other variable, £1,60012.3%
Variable share (£5,500 ÷ £13,000)42.3%
Use this site’s own food cost, after waste. Not the brand average.

STEP 3
What each £1 keeps
100% minus the variable share. This is what pays the fixed costs.
100% − 42.3%
= 57.7p
Of every £1 a guest spends at site 10, 57.7p is left to cover rent and the core team. Only after they’re covered is anything profit.

STEP 4
Divide the fixed costs by what each £1 keeps
SITE 10
£7,800 ÷ 0.577£13,520 a week
£13,520 ÷ 7 trading days£1,931 a day
Actual sales £13,000: short by£520
Give each manager the daily number. It turns a P&L into a target they can hit today.
Exact: £7,800 ÷ (1 − £5,500/£13,000) = £13,520.

THREE MISTAKES THAT HIDE A LOSS
1
Treating labour as variable
Most of the rota is there whatever you take. Put the core team in fixed costs.
2
Using the brand’s average food cost
One site’s waste hides inside a group average. Use each site’s own number.
3
Leaving out card fees and commission
They rise with every sale. They belong in the variable share.
