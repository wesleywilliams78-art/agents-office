# Franchise Entry Report: how to build an issue

One country per week, published every Friday on the WRW Hospitality LinkedIn page and reposted by Wesley Williams. It is a free 7-page A4 PDF for brands deciding whether to enter a market. It belongs to WRW Hospitality alone: never label it Scaling Without Borders, which is Wesley's personal newsletter. The reader should finish knowing where the opportunity is, what blocks it, and what to do in the first 90 days.

Issue 01 (Norway, 23 Oct 2026) is the reference build. Every issue uses the same template so the series compounds. The value is the method: sourced facts, honest gaps, and one clear position. Polish without sourcing is worthless here.

## Non-negotiables

1. **Public sources only.** Every figure carries a date and a source code ([S4]) that resolves to a full entry in the source list.
2. **Never invent a number.** Anything derived is labelled **Estimate**, with the method stated. Anything missing is written **Not found**. A tile that says "Not found" is more credible than a guess.
3. **Two signals for a rise/fall call.** Two independent signals are required. With only one, mark the row **Thin**.
4. **Unit counts come with a date and method.** For example, "counted 21 entries on brand locator, 23 Oct 2026". A brand's own "over 60" claim is a claim, not a count. Say so.
5. **Content governance.** Insight tier only:
   - No client names and no private details.
   - Nothing from client calls.
   - No partner named in the present tense.
   - If the country, or a category in it, is a live client or employer interest, keep the report neutral and general. Do not recommend for or against that interest. Flag it to Wes before publishing.
6. **No prices or service pitch in the report.** Page 7 carries one CTA line only. Services and prices live on the WRW Hospitality LinkedIn Services page.
7. **Not legal, tax or investment advice.** Say so on the regulation page and in the method note.
8. **Numbers about our own work** come from `00-Meta/numbers-ledger.md` only; otherwise write "(assumed)".

## Inputs to confirm before starting

- **Country, issue number, issue date.** The date is the Friday of publication, written "23 Oct 2026".
- **Conflict check.** Is this country a live client or employer market? If yes, apply the neutral rule above and tell Wes.
- **Category set.** Use the fixed set below unless Wes says otherwise.

If you are working unattended, take these defaults and state them at the top of your notes.

### Fixed category set (so issues compare)

- **8 core:** chicken, burger, pizza, kebab/grill, coffee, bakery/dessert, bubble tea, fast casual.
- **Plus:** plant-based.
- **Plus:** 1 local category the data supports. Norway's was roadside/forecourt QSR.

The same categories appear in the same order in every issue.

## Workflow

Set up a task list with these stages. The final stage is checking.

### 1. Load the brand system

Read the `wesley-linkedin-visuals` skill and its `visual-system.md` before designing anything. This report extends that system to an A4 format, using the palette and type below. Do not add new colours, fonts or decorative devices without flagging them. The one sanctioned exception is the issue's country colour (see Palette).

### 2. Research in two parallel streams

Run two research agents at once, each with a precise brief. Search budgets run out: each Issue 01 stream hit a 200-call cap. Scope them tightly and tell them to list the gaps they couldn't close rather than guess.

**Stream A: market, regulation and costs.** Prefer primary sources in this order: national statistics office, central bank, government and legislation sites, regulators, then reputable law-firm trackers.

| Item | What to get | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| Population | Latest quarterly figure, plus trend | National statistics office |
| GDP per head | Official figure and year. A USD conversion only as a labelled Estimate | Statistics office; IMF/World Bank |
| Food-service spend | Household spend on restaurants (COICOP 11 / 11.1) and/or NACE 56 turnover. Note which, and the year | Statistics office; industry body |
| Franchise count | Systems, outlets, turnover. Often no official count exists: say so | Statistics office; franchise association |
| Spending trend | Real household consumption, consumer confidence, CPI, and the restaurant CPI line | Statistics office; central bank |
| Currency | Rate vs GBP, EUR and USD on a stated date; policy rate and its direction | Central bank |
| How people eat out | 3 facts: frequency, price level vs EU (Eurostat PLI), regional concentration | Surveys; Eurostat; industry studies |
| Franchise law | Specific law? Mandatory disclosure? Registration? Any live proposals? | Legislation site; law-firm tracker |
| Trade mark | National office fee, timing, Madrid route. Whether an EU mark covers it | Trade mark office |
| Food safety | Registration before opening; inspection or rating scheme | Food regulator |
| Alcohol | Licence types, who grants them, fees | Health ministry; municipality |
| Halal and supply | Certification bodies; slaughter rules; import quotas | Agriculture ministry |
| Employment | Minimum wage or sector floor; employer social charges; holiday pay; pension; sick-pay period; average sector wage | Labour authority; tax authority; statistics office |
| Tax | VAT on takeaway vs eat-in; corporate tax; withholding tax on royalties (related vs unrelated franchisor) | Tax authority; PwC Tax Summaries |
| Company set-up | Entity types, share capital, registration fee, director residency | Company registry |
| Other blockers | Trading-hours law, sugar/packaging duties, plastics bans, supply-chain disclosure laws | Government sites |

**Stream B: category movement.** For each category:

- **Current unit counts** for the main chains, from their own store locators. Count them and give the date and method.
- **An earlier comparable count** (ideally 2–4 years back) from annual reports, regulator filings (competition-authority merger notices are excellent), company accounts, press or archived pages.
- **Entries, exits, bankruptcies and master-franchise appointments** in the last 2 years.
- **Sector context:** official bankruptcies in food service, margins, operator sentiment surveys, and delivery-platform data.
- **Which international brands are absent.** Absence is a signal, so record it.

Record each signal separately, with a confidence level:
- **High:** the brand's own locator, annual report or regulatory filing.
- **Medium:** reputable press.
- **Thin:** a single weak source.

### 3. Verify the load-bearing facts yourself

Agent reports are leads, not facts. Before writing, open the primary source yourself for every number used in:
- the cover verdict;
- any page headline;
- the chart;
- the "If I had a brand" position.

Issue 01 lessons:
- **Press releases contradict themselves.** McDonald's "restaurant no. 100" release still said 87 in its boilerplate. Use the specific, dated claim and note the conflict.
- **Timing claims often have one weak source.** KFC's "first openings early 2027" traced back to a founder quote in a secondary article. The acquirer's own release gave no date. Write "expected… per the founder; deal subject to approvals".
- **The same source can say two things.** One survey said both "half" and "6 in 10" eat out less than monthly. Use the more conservative figure.
- **Statistics get suspended.** Norway's goods consumption index stopped in mid-2025. Use the nearest live substitute and say which.
- **Some figures are out of date or contested.** Franchise counts may be 15 years old, and associations may be disputed. Show the age or the conflict rather than hiding it.

### 4. Analyse

**Rise/fall table.** Columns:
- Direction: ↑ Rising, → Flat, ↓ Falling, or "Not found". Always arrows plus words, never colour alone.
- Evidence
- Source codes
- Date window
- Confidence: High, Medium or Thin. Thin is shown in bold in the country colour, always with the word.
- Saturation: Low, Medium or High.
- Entry difficulty: Low, Medium or High.

Definitions:
- **Saturation:** how many branded national players already hold the category, and how much convenience retail substitutes for it.
- **Entry difficulty:** capital intensity, labour intensity, licensing, supply-chain friction and incumbent strength.

**White space: 3–5 gaps.** A gap is a need with rising demand and no clear branded owner. For each gap give:
- the gap;
- the evidence (with codes);
- who it suits;
- the likely entry route;
- a size of S, M or L (our judgement of the prize for a new entrant).

Mark any gap resting on thin evidence. Plot every category on an **opportunity-vs-saturation 2x2**:
- quadrants labelled Move now, Out-execute, Test small, Avoid;
- one highlight in the country colour;
- a note that positions are judgement, not measurement.

**The position ("If I had a brand").** One sentence in Wes's voice. Pick the category with:
- a rising signal;
- low saturation;
- a time-bound trigger, such as an incumbent's arrival or a master-franchise appointment.

Then add the counterpoint: which categories to avoid and why. Issue 01's position: "If I ran an international chicken brand right now, I'd be in a Norwegian master-franchise conversation this quarter." The position must follow from the table. If no category earns it, say so plainly; a "no clear gap" issue is still valuable.

**Entry routes.** Compare master franchise, area developer, direct franchise and company-owned launch on four measures:
- control;
- capital;
- speed to scale;
- what has been seen in this country, with dated examples.

Give a best fit for an emerging brand, a mid-size brand and a global brand.

**Wage engine.** Show what every 100 of pay costs the employer (holiday pay, pension, employer social charges). Convert the sector wage floor to an all-in hourly cost in GBP, labelled Estimate with its method.

### 5. Write the copy

Rules: British English, plain language, no jargon, no emoji, no exclamation marks. Each page headline is a claim, not a label. For example: "No franchise law, a firm wage floor, and a trade mark you must file separately".

Headline, table and chart must agree. Issue 01 had to change "pizza and coffee are standing still" because the table said legacy pizza is falling.

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1. Cover | Country-colour field, the WRW logo tile, issue number and date. "Franchise Entry Report" with the country name in display serif. A one-line verdict. Three headline stats. Mountain line along the bottom. |
| 2. Market snapshot | Six stat tiles: population, GDP per head, food-service spend, franchise count, spending trend, currency. "How [country] eats out" with 3 bullets. A "So what" box in ink. Two simple bar charts. |
| 3. Rise and fall | Category table. Diverging bar chart of unit-count change, noting that windows differ so readers compare direction, not size. A "Reading the table" box. |
| 4. White space | 3–5 gap cards. The 2x2. The "If I had a brand" box in the country colour, signed by Wesley Williams, WRW Hospitality. |
| 5. Regulation and cost to operate | Regulation table (area, current position "verified [month year]", source). Three key tiles. Wage-engine chart and two wage tiles. Not-advice line. |
| 6. Entry route and risks | Entry-route comparison table. Three best-fit cards. Six risk tiles: costs and demand, labour, supply chain, seasonality, regulatory, currency and rates. |
| 7. First 90 days and sources | "Your first 90 days" 10-point checklist. "What we'd check next" box (the open gaps). Method note. Full source list. Closing band in the country colour with the mountain line and CTA: "Which country should we cover next?" The © line. |

### 6. Build the PDF

Build as HTML/CSS and render to A4 PDF with headless Chromium (Playwright `page.pdf`, `print_background=True`, zero margins, `@page{size:A4;margin:0}`). Each page is a fixed 210 × 297 mm section. Canva is the fallback if code rendering isn't available.

**Palette.** Bone, one country colour and black is the WRW report palette. It deliberately extends the LinkedIn system's no-black rule.
- **the country colour, the only accent.** Take one colour from the country's flag. It replaces WRW red on the cover field, the closing band, the "If I had a brand" box, section labels, rules, hero numerals and the one highlight in each chart. A second flag colour may be used for thin rules only.
  - If the flag's main colour is red, use WRW red `#FF3131`.
  - The cover field must hold white text at a contrast of 3.6:1 or better (WRW red is 3.66). If it can't, use the flag's darker colour or set the cover text in ink.
  - Colours only: never the flag graphic, emblem, coat of arms or text from a flag.
  - Check the colour against the country's official flag specification. Record it and its contrast in the issue log.
  - Reference colours: Norway and Denmark `#FF3131`, Sweden `#006AA7`, Finland `#002F6C`. The palette table is in the repo at `videos/franchise-entry-report/README.md`.
- WRW red `#FF3131`, in the WRW logo tile only (and as the country colour for red flags);
- bone `#F1ECE2` page background;
- bone-2 `#E6DFD2` for hairlines;
- ink `#111111`;
- secondary text `#4A4743`;
- muted `#8A857D`;
- white `#FFFFFF` for panels.

**Type.**
- Headlines, page titles and hero numerals: Playfair Display, the closest match to the book cover serif. Self-host the font files from `@fontsource/playfair-display`.
- Body: Inter.
- Numerals: lining figures.
- No italics.

**Mark.** The WRW seal: a red rounded square with white serif "WRW". It stays white on red on every cover, whatever the country colour; on a red field, use white with red text. Use the real logo file when available; never redraw it differently per issue.

**Mountain line.** Cover and closing band only, always the same silhouette.

**Footer on every page:** "WRW Hospitality · Franchise Entry Report · Issue NN" and "Page X of 7". Never "Scaling Without Borders".

**Copyright:** "© 2026 WRW Hospitality (Wesley Williams). Not to be resold, rebranded or redistributed." on the last page, and the same line in the PDF's file properties.

**Charts.**
- Flat; one highlight per chart in the country colour; everything else ink. Colour never carries meaning: rise and fall keep their arrows and words.
- Direct labels; no 3D.
- A source line under every chart.
- Never dual axes.
- Size labels so nothing clips. Issue 01 clipped "29,000" on the first render.

Use the `dataviz` skill for chart rules.

**Source list.** Keep sources in one array and render them into the page-7 list, so codes and the list never drift. Small text is acceptable in the PDF because the links work there.

### 7. Check, then check again

This stage is required. Render every page to PNG and look at each one.

- [ ] Nothing clipped, overlapping or orphaned; tiles aligned; footer on all 7 pages.
- [ ] Every [S#] on a page exists in the source list, and every source is used.
- [ ] Each figure on the cover, page headlines and the position matches its page and its primary source.
- [ ] Headlines agree with the table and chart.
- [ ] Every Estimate is labelled with its method. Every gap reads "Not found". Every one-signal row reads "Thin".
- [ ] No client names, private details, prices or present-tense partners.
- [ ] The accent is exactly this issue's country colour, and `#FF3131` appears only in the logo tile (unless the flag is red). Grep the HTML/CSS for any other hex values.
- [ ] The cover's white text has a contrast of 3.6:1 or better against the country colour.
- [ ] No flag graphic or emblem anywhere. No "Scaling Without Borders" anywhere. The © line is on the last page.
- [ ] Spell-check in British English; proofread headlines by eye.
- [ ] PDF under 5 MB (Issue 01 was 0.31 MB) and exactly 7 pages.
- [ ] For high-stakes issues, have a separate agent that didn't write the report check the figures against sources.

### 8. Package and log

Deliver:
1. **The PDF:** `Franchise-Entry-Report-[Country]-Issue-NN.pdf`.
2. **The LinkedIn caption**, from this template. No emoji, no exclamation marks; PDF as a document post or the link in the first comment.
   > Thinking about franchising into [Country]? This week's free report covers which food categories are rising, which are saturated, and where the gaps are. [One surprising finding.] Download it below. Which country should we cover next?
3. **The issue log entry:** verdict, caption, category set used, the full dated source list, and the open gaps for the next refresh. Append it to the running Franchise Entry Report issue log.
4. **Optional explainer video** for the Services page or the feed. 1920×1080, about 45–50 seconds, silent with on-screen captions. Seven scenes:
   - cover;
   - the market (3 count-up stats);
   - the rules;
   - what's moving (animated bar chart);
   - the gap;
   - "If I had a brand";
   - CTA: "Want this for your brand and your country? Request services on this page."

   Build it as one HTML page with a deterministic `setT(t)` timeline. Screenshot frames at 30 fps with Playwright and encode with ffmpeg (H.264, yuv420p, faststart). Make frame 0 a complete cover, because it is the thumbnail. Use the issue's country colour for the fields. Use quick cuts rather than long crossfades between the colour and bone fields, which muddy. Export frame 0 as the thumbnail image.

## Repeat checklist for each new country

- [ ] Copy the template; change the country, issue number and date only.
- [ ] Re-pull every statistic and date it.
- [ ] Source every row of the rise/fall table; mark Thin where needed.
- [ ] Verify the legal page against a primary source.
- [ ] Run the conflict check; remove client names and private details.
- [ ] Set the country colour, check its contrast, and log it.
- [ ] Spell-check, and check that no Scaling Without Borders label remains.
- [ ] Export the PDF under 5 MB; write the caption.
- [ ] Log sources and the issue number.

## Common failure points

- Letting an agent's summary stand in for the primary source on a headline number.
- Picking the position first and finding evidence after. The position must fall out of the rise/fall table.
- Treating a brand's "over X" claim as a count.
- Comparing unit changes across different time windows without saying so.
- Filling white-space slots with thin ideas to reach five. Three strong gaps beat five weak ones.
- Adding a price table or service pitch to the report.
- Overloading tiles. One number, one short line of context, one source line.
