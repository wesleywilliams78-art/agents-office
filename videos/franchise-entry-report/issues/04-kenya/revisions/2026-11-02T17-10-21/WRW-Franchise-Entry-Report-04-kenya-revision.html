So what?

Food prices rose 9.5% in a year and bank loans cost 14.4%, so the margin is squeezed from both sides. We found no published restaurant margin. The largest operator grew visits 14% by letting average spend fall 3%. Kenya rewards a lean, value-led unit in the five cities, paid for by phone and sold through delivery. [S9, S3, S5]

04WHITE-SPACE OPPORTUNITIES

Four gaps, and the way in matters more than the category

A gap is a need with growing demand and no clear owner. The size of each one is our judgement of the prize for a new entrant: S, M or L.

1

Forecourt-first QSR

GAP
Fuel retailers are turning stations into food stops, and only a few brands sit on them yet. A partner brings the site, the lease and the traffic.
EVIDENCE
Vivo’s 350th Shell station opened in Jul 2026 as a convenience hub. KFC, Java and The Coffee Club all entered or grew through fuel retailers. [S34, S15, S35, S27]
SUITS
Compact chicken, coffee and grab-and-go formats with a short menu.
ROUTE
Master franchise held by a fuel retailer, or a joint venture.
LLarge prize
2

The five-city build-out

GAP
The buyers sit in five cities, and the delivery apps already serve towns beyond Nairobi. Counts outside the capital are thin, but openings there are recent.
EVIDENCE
Glovo runs in 12 towns, and Uber Eats in Nakuru and Eldoret. Java opened in Eldoret in Dec 2025. International arrivals reached 2.76m in 2025/26, up 9.8%. [S13, S7, S36, S37]
SUITS
Brands with a proven Nairobi unit that can run on a regional supply chain.
ROUTE
Area developer, or a master partner with a regional plan.
MMedium prize

Value-led, delivery-ready menus

IF I HAD A BRAND
“
If I had a brand going into Kenya, I’d pick the partner before I picked the site.

The brands that scaled came in on someone else’s footprint: KFC on Vivo’s forecourts, Java with Rubis and TotalEnergies, The Coffee Club with a 14-station fuel retailer. Labour is cheap. Money, food and rent are not: 14.4% to borrow and 9.5% food inflation.

The partnerships that ended lost the partner, not the customer. Famous Brands took its stores back from its franchisee of 23 years, and Subway’s franchisee kept the shops as Subz. I’d write credit checks, step-in rights and site assignment into the deal first, then choose the category.

Wesley Williams, WRW Hospitality

No franchise law, cheap labour, and costs that sit in money, food and tax

We found no franchise statute, so the contract does the work. Labour is a small cost line. Borrowing, food and withholding tax are the lines to model first.

Emerging brand

A fuel-retailer partner

Under c. 25 units. A fuel retailer brings sites, leases and traffic you can’t buy from abroad. KFC, Java and The Coffee Club all came in this way.

Mid-size brand

A local master, credit-checked

Proven in 2 countries. Check the partner’s balance sheet: Famous Brands’ master of 23 years owed banks over KES 67m when it lost the licence.

Global brand

A joint venture with a retailer

KFC’s franchisee and Vivo Energy set up a 50:50 venture across Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda in 2019. KFC now has 48 Kenyan restaurants.

Price the risk before you price the franchise

Six risks, each sourced
Currency and remittance

The shilling has held near 130 to the dollar for about 18 months, but royalties are earned in KES. We found no primary source on remittance rules. The treaty withholds 15% on royalties. [S3, S4, S44]

Cost of borrowing

Bank loans averaged 14.4% in Sep 2026, against a policy rate of 8.75% after ten cuts. A franchisee’s fit-out is funded at the higher rate. [S3, S4]

Food and supply

Food prices rose 9.5% in the year to Sep 2026, against 6.8% overall, after drought and the Middle East conflict. Cold-chain and local supply data for chicken, potatoes, dairy and oil: not found. [S9]

Partner credit

Famous Brands’ master partner owed KES 6.1m in royalties and over KES 67m to banks before the licence was reclaimed. Subway’s franchisee kept the shops as Subz (blog, Thin). [S21, S31]

Delivery economics

Uber agreed to buy Delivery Hero, which would put Glovo and Uber Eats under one owner, subject to Competition Authority review, with completion expected in H2 2027. Jumia Food left in Dec 2023. Commission rates: not found. [S51, S13]

Protest, security and rent

Protests damaged some of Simbisa’s city-centre stores in FY2025, and new taxes cut its customer count 6%. A Nairobi venue faces a KES 16.5m rent claim, with KES 10.8m in arrears. [S16, S52]

07YOUR FIRST 90 DAYS

Ten moves, in order, before you sign anything

1

File the trade mark with KIPI, and record each franchisee as a registered user.

2

Shortlist 3 to 5 partners among fuel retailers and multi-brand operators.

3

Credit-check the partner and write step-in rights, site assignment and a non-compete into the master agreement.

4

Rebuild the unit P&L on Kenyan costs: rent, 14.4% money and 9.5% food inflation, not only the wage floor.

5

Model the royalty net of 15% withholding, and get a ruling on how the initial fee is taxed.

6

Confirm VAT on restaurant meals with the Kenya Revenue Authority before you price the menu.

7

Count the competition yourself: dated locator counts for KFC, Simbisa’s brands and Java House, city by city.

8

Price delivery at more than one commission rate, and plan for one owner of Glovo and Uber Eats.

9

Plan the licence path for the first site: county permit, food hygiene and halal certification.

10

Agree how royalties are paid in KES and converted to sterling.
