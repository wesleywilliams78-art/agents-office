## 1. Franchise law

- Position: Sweden has a franchise-specific disclosure law, Lag (2006:484) om franchisegivares informationsskyldighet. In force 1 Oct 2006. Amended 2008 (SFS 2008:511) and 2016 (SFS 2016:221, in force 1 Sep 2016, which changed sections 5 and 6). It is a pre-contract disclosure duty only. There is no registration, no filing, no cooling-off period and no franchise-specific regulator.
- What must be disclosed (section 3): written information, "i god tid" (in good time) before the franchise agreement is concluded, clear and comprehensible, covering at least: (1) description of the franchise business; (2) other franchisees in the system and the scope of their operations; (3) fees and other financial terms; (4) IP rights granted to the franchisee; (5) goods or services the franchisee must buy or lease; (6) non-compete obligations during and after the contract; (7) term, rules for changing, extending and ending the contract and the financial consequences of ending it; (8) how disputes are resolved and who bears the costs. The same duty applies when an existing agreement is transferred to a new franchisee with the franchisor's consent. Scope (sections 1-2): the franchisee pays the franchisor to use its business concept, uses its trade marks or other IP, and is subject to recurring compliance checks.
- Penalties and remedy (sections 4-6): if a franchisor signs without giving the information, the Patent and Market Court (Patent- och marknadsdomstolen) can order it to provide the information, for that agreement and future ones. Franchisees, trade associations and others with a legitimate interest can sue. The order must normally carry a fine (vite) unless special reasons make that unnecessary. No fixed fine amount is set in the Act. Procedure follows the Marketing Act (2008:486). The Act does not itself void the contract or set damages.
- Live proposals to change it: Not found. Tried: lagen.nu change history (last change SFS 2016:221), riksdagen.se and regeringen.se searches for "franchise" 2026, a general web search for a new franchise law inquiry. Nothing current surfaced.
- As at: 24 Oct 2026 (lagen.nu consolidation current to SFS 2016:221 for this Act; riksdagen.se text same).

## 2. Trade mark

- PRV (Patent- och registreringsverket) fee, Swedish national mark: SEK 2,700 for the first class if filed through the e-service; SEK 3,900 if filed any other way (paper, email, or goods/services not on the e-service pre-approved list). Each extra class SEK 1,000 either way. Fee is payable whether or not the mark is registered. As at 24 Sep 2026 (PRV page last updated that date). Fee level traced to the 2025 fee change (Trade Marks Ordinance, changed March 2025).
- Timing: PRV states about 6 weeks to a first answer (registration notice or office action) on a new application, as at 22 Sep 2026. After registration there is a 3-month opposition period; the registration is not final until it ends with no opposition. Registration lasts 10 years, renewable. Estimate: roughly 4.5 months to a final, unopposed registration if there is no office action (6 weeks + 3 months). PRV does not state this total itself.
- EU trade mark (EUIPO): an EU trade mark has unitary effect across all EU member states, so it covers Sweden (EUIPO Guidelines, territorial aspects; Regulation (EU) 2017/1001). Confirmed, with the caveat that the EUIPO pages do not name Sweden; coverage follows from Sweden's EU membership. A UK registration (UKIPO) does not cover Sweden or the EU; the UK-based franchisor needs an EUTM or a Swedish mark.
- Madrid route: the EU is a Madrid Protocol member, so an international registration can designate the EU (reaches Sweden) or Sweden individually. PRV states designated countries have 12-18 months to examine an international registration. If an EU designation is refused it can be converted into national applications including Sweden (EUIPO FAQ).
- EUIPO fees: Not found in this stream (not needed for the Swedish position; Stream owner may add).

## 3. Food safety

- Registration: a food business must be registered with (or approved by) its control authority, normally the municipal environment and health unit, BEFORE it opens. A business may start when the registration decision arrives, or if two weeks have passed since a complete notification even without a decision. Starting early can bring a sanction fee. Risk classification and the first control visit follow after opening (Livsmedelsverket, via search extract; municipal forms: Linkoping, Mjolby). As at 24 Oct 2026.
- Fee: set by each municipality (not national). Examples read on the primary tariffs: Koping, 2026 tariff, hourly rate SEK 1,435, registration and risk classification charged as a fixed fee equal to 1.5 hours (section 12); Estimate: 1.5 x 1,435 = SEK 2,152.50. Vargarda 2026 tariff also charges 1.5 hours of control time for registration (section 7). Mjolby's form lists a handling fee of SEK 1,170 (older form, undated). So expect roughly SEK 1,200 to 2,200 per outlet to register, plus an annual control fee based on risk class and hours. Larger cities (Stockholm, Goteborg, Malmo) not found.
- Inspection regime: municipalities carry out risk-based official control; frequency set by risk class; a control fee is charged after each inspection (hourly tariff).
- Public rating scheme: there is NO national Smiley-type scheme. A national scheme was investigated (SOU 2005:44) and the bill was withdrawn on 9 Nov 2006. Some municipalities run their own: a southern Swedish group around Malmo since 2005; Stockholm publishes inspection results for restaurants. Stockholm's page was seen only via search result summary. Confidence on "no national scheme" is Medium: based on a government document title and a Riksdag written answer found in search; no 2026 statement found saying so explicitly.

## 4. Alcohol

- Who grants it: the municipality (kommun), under Alkohollagen (2010:1622), chapter 8. Application is in writing to the municipality. The police give an opinion for a permanent licence open to the public. The county board (Lansstyrelsen) supports and supervises the municipalities. Folkhalsomyndigheten (Public Health Agency) issues guidance and the knowledge tests. Consolidated text read on lagen.nu, current to SFS 2026:511.
- Requirements: the applicant must be personally and financially suitable; a knowledge test in the alcohol law is part of the assessment (pass mark 75% per section); the person in charge of serving must be at least 20; staff must be employed by the permit holder or hired from a staffing agency. The municipality can refuse even where other conditions are met (e.g. location near homes, schools).
- NEW 2026: the rule that a licensed venue must serve food and have its own kitchen was abolished on 1 June 2026 (Riksdag decided 7 May 2026). Existing permits unaffected. This makes bar-led formats easier. Knowledge test still needed; a new permit for the public needs a new test.
- Hours: default is 11.00 to 01.00 unless the municipality decides otherwise (section 19); premises cleared within 30 minutes of the close.
- Timing: the municipality must decide within 4 months of a complete application, extendable by up to 4 months where the investigation requires it (Folkhalsomyndigheten guidance, via search extract; as the extension period was not seen on the primary page, treat the 8-month ceiling as Medium).
- Fees (set per municipality under chapter 8, section 10): Atvidaberg 2026, new permanent permit for the public including one knowledge test SEK 11,850; fixed annual supervision fee SEK 2,950 plus a turnover-based variable fee (read on the municipal tariff). Soderkoping 2026: SEK 11,330 for the same category (seen via search snippet only). So the working range found is about SEK 11,000 to 11,850 to apply, with annual supervision from about SEK 3,000 upward. Big-city fees (Stockholm, Goteborg, Malmo) not found; they are likely higher.

## 5. Halal and supply

- Religious slaughter: all animals slaughtered in Sweden must be stunned before bleeding (Djurskyddslagen 2018:1192, chapter 5, section 1). Sweden does not use the religious-slaughter derogation, so slaughter without stunning (kosher; strict halal without pre-stunning) is prohibited. Halal is allowed in Sweden where the animal is stunned first (Jordbruksverket). Practical effect: a halal Swedish supply chain relies on pre-stun-compliant halal certification, or on imported product.
- Import of halal meat: Not found as a Swedish rule. Tried: Jordbruksverket and Livsmedelsverket pages, Riksdag motions. Meat from EU member states moves freely; the only item found is a Riksdag motion (SD, 2022/23:2149) asking the government to seek an EU exemption to ban imports of unstunned-slaughter meat. Outcome not found, so treat no ban as the current position pending legal check. Quotas: none found. Thin for imports.
- Packaging producer responsibility: Forordning (2022:1274) om producentansvar for forpackningar applies; businesses that put packaging on the Swedish market must report quantities for the previous year and join/pay to a producer responsibility organisation (Naturvardsverket). EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) applies from 12 Aug 2026, and Sweden amended 2022:1274 in response (Naturvardsverket).
- Single-use plastics: Engangsproduktforordningen (2021:996) sections 17-22 require sellers of drinks in single-use cups or fast food in single-use boxes to be able to offer a reusable alternative (in force since 1 Jan 2024). The exemption threshold was tightened from 1 Jan 2026 (a municipal page says fewer than 75 single-use cups or boxes per day on average; search extract only). Environmental sanction fee of SEK 10,000 for failing to offer (2023 information sheet from Hall Sverige Rent; verify before print). Medium to Thin.
- Deposit scheme: Sweden has a national deposit scheme for cans and PET bottles run by Returpack (Pantamera). Rules for who must join are in the packaging ordinance; a Returpack technical specification dated 10 Apr 2026 points to 2022:1274. Detailed obligations for restaurants not found. Thin.
- EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR): applies from 30 Dec 2026 for large and medium operators, 30 Jun 2027 for micro and small operators (after the Dec 2025 targeted revision). Affects cocoa, coffee, palm oil, soy, cattle products, rubber, wood in the supply chain; operators and traders must hold due diligence statements. Commission page dated Jul 2026; Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102 adds products from 30 Dec 2027.

## 6. Tax

- VAT, exact scope (as at 26 Feb 2026 Skatteverket news; Prop. 2025/26:55 dated 20 Nov 2025; Riksdag approved Feb 2026):
  - Food (livsmedel) sold from 1 Apr 2026 to 31 Dec 2027 inclusive: 6% (was 12%). Takeaway food from a restaurant is generally food and takes 6%. Bottled and packaged water takes 6%.
  - Restaurant and catering SERVICES (meal eaten on site with service, tables, cutlery; catering with support services): stay at 12%. The government expressly declined to cut these.
  - Still 25%: spirits, wine, strong beer (and tap water and some conference-package items are outside the 6% rate).
  - The relevant date is when the taxable event happens: inside the window 6%, before or after 12% for food.
  - Practical effect for F&B: a dine-in plus takeaway outlet runs two rates (12% / 6%); the prop. itself notes extra administration. Estimate: price pressure to push takeaway share if the gap persists; end date 31 Dec 2027 means the food rate reverts to 12% on 1 Jan 2028 unless extended (no extension found).
- Corporate income tax: 20.6% flat. PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Sweden, last reviewed 22 Sep 2026.
- Withholding tax on royalties to a non-resident franchisor: Sweden does not levy a withholding tax on royalties as such; Swedish-source royalties are treated as business income taxable at the corporate rate, subject to treaty reduction or waiver (PwC note). The non-treaty column shows 20.6% (that corporate-rate tax). UK-Sweden treaty: royalties 0% (PwC treaty table, United Kingdom row: dividends 0/5/15, royalties 0). So a UK franchisor receiving a continuing fee or royalty from a Swedish franchisee should face no Swedish tax on it under the treaty, provided it is the beneficial owner and treaty formalities are met. Not tested: whether a given franchise fee (as opposed to royalty) is treated as a royalty or business profits; get tax advice. As at 22 Sep 2026.
- Employer taxes: arbetsgivaravgifter 31.42% of gross pay in 2026 (sjukforsakring 3.55, foraldraforsakring 2.00, alderspension 10.21, efterlevandepension 0.30, arbetsmarknadsavgift 2.64, arbetsskadeavgift 0.10, allman loneavgift 12.62; these sum to 31.42, checked). Youth reduction: for employees born 2003-2007 (aged 19-23), employer pays 20.81% (old-age pension charge plus half the other charges) on pay up to SEK 25,000 a month; pay above that at the full 31.42%. Applies to pay from 1 Apr 2026 to 30 Sep 2027. NOTE: the brief suggested other end dates; Skatteverket says 30 Sep 2027. Occupational pension also attracts special payroll tax (sarskild loneskatt) of 24.26% on the pension cost (Skatteverket).

## 7. Company set-up

- Aktiebolag (private AB): minimum share capital SEK 25,000 (Aktiebolagslagen 2005:551, chapter 1, section 5). Can be set in euro at the equivalent.
- Registration fee (Bolagsverket) for a new AB: SEK 2,700, or SEK 2,400 if filed electronically with an electronic signature (Aktiebolagsforordningen 2005:559, chapter 3, section 1, as changed by SFS 2025:343; in force from 19 Jun 2025 per FAR Online).
- Residency: at least half of the board members must be resident in the EEA (ABL 8:9); the managing director, if there is one, must be resident in the EEA (ABL 8:30); at least one special signatory must be EEA-resident (8:37). Bolagsverket can grant exemption for special reasons (fee SEK 1,000 under 3:3 of the ordinance). A UK resident therefore cannot be the sole director without exemption. A legal person cannot be a board member (8:10).
- Time to register: Not found on the Bolagsverket page (the site blocked automated reading). Tried: bolagsverket.se company pages, search. Treat as unknown; do not print a number.
- Alternatives: branch (filial) of a foreign company registered with Bolagsverket under the Foreign Branches Act (1992:160); the page was fetched but processing detail and fees not extracted, so Not found for the branch fee. Handelsbolag (general partnership): not researched beyond existence. Note: a franchisee will normally be a Swedish AB owned by the master or sub-franchisee; the franchisor itself needs no Swedish entity merely to receive royalties (see item 6).

## 8. Employment cost (the wage engine)

Agreement: Gröna Riksavtalet (Visita and Hotell- och Restaurangfacket, HRF), 1 Apr 2025 to 31 Mar 2027. Read on the agreement text (HRF PDF). There is also a Hängavtal Visita (the version for employers not members of Visita; it carries an extra 0.51% administration charge on the premium base and higher minimum wages). Sweden has no statutory minimum wage (HRF folder); the collective agreement sets minimums. Whether a franchisee must sign is a legal question: not researched; a franchisee that does not sign an agreement is not bound by these minimums by law, but the sector norm and union pressure make it likely.

### Working: what every 100 of pay costs the employer (ESTIMATE)

Method (adult employee, age 22 or over, standard day shift, no OB, no overtime, no sickness):
1. Base pay: 100.00
2. Holiday pay at the agreement rate 12.72% of pay: 12.72. Pay subject to charges = 100 + 12.72 = 112.72.
3. Employer contributions 31.42% x 112.72 = 35.42.
4. Pension and insurance premiums 4.85% x 112.72 = 5.47. (Assumes the premium base includes holiday pay; premiums are a percentage of gross pay.)
5. Total = 112.72 + 35.42 + 5.47 = 153.60 per 100 of pay. Multiplier 1.5360.

Sensitivity A, with special payroll tax: add 24.26% x the 4.50% pension premium on 112.72 = 5.07 x 0.2426 = 1.23 per 100, giving about 154.83. (Assumes the SAF-LO premium carries the tax; not checked on a page that says so for this scheme.)
Sensitivity B, aged 19-22 on the youth rate: contributions 20.81% instead of 31.42%, and no pension premium before age 22 (other premiums of about 0.45% remain). 112.72 x (1 + 0.2081 + 0.0045) = about 136.7 per 100.

- 153.64 x 1.5360 = SEK 236.00 per hour (Estimate). With sensitivity A: about SEK 237.9.

## 9. Other blockers

- Trading hours: no general statute on shop or restaurant opening hours found. The binding limit for licensed venues is the alcohol serving time (item 4: default 11.00-01.00 unless the municipality sets otherwise). Not found: a primary source stating "no trading-hours law". Treat as an absence-of-evidence finding. Thin.
- Cash register law: businesses taking cash or card must register sales in a cash register that is manufacturer-declared and linked to a certified control unit (kontrollenhet); a receipt must be offered on every sale (Skatteverket kassaregister pages; current rule SKVFS 2021:17). Small-business exemption from the manufacturer-declared register if cash and card sales stay at or below four price base amounts a year (2024 threshold quoted as SEK 229,200; 2026 figure not checked). Even then a certified control unit is needed if a register is used. Restaurants are subject to unannounced Skatteverket checks. Medium (search extracts of Skatteverket pages; the pages were not read in full).
- Staff ID ledger (personalliggare): required in restaurants. Record the business name and number, and for each working day the full name and personal or co-ordination number of everyone working, including unpaid and agency staff, with start and end times. Exempt only if just the owner, spouse and children under 16 work. Control fee SEK 12,500 plus SEK 2,500 for each active person not recorded. Keep for two years after the end of the calendar year in which the tax year ended. As at 24 Oct 2026. High.
- New in 2026 that affect an F&B entrant: (a) VAT on food 6% from 1 Apr 2026 (item 6); (b) food-and-kitchen requirement for alcohol licences abolished 1 Jun 2026 (item 4); (c) reduced employer contributions for ages 19-23 from 1 Apr 2026 (item 6); (d) EU PPWR applies from 12 Aug 2026 (item 5); (e) the reusable-cup threshold tightened 1 Jan 2026 (item 5, Thin); (f) EUDR from 30 Dec 2026 (item 5); (g) Gröna Riksavtalet second pay step on 1 Apr 2026 (item 8).
