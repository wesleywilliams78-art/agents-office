02MARKET SNAPSHOT

A small, rich market with flat volumes: a new brand has to take share, not ride the tide

Finland has 5.66 million people and a GDP per head 19% above the EU average. Restaurant prices are high and demand is cautious. The market rewards brands that arrive with a local operator, not brands that arrive alone.

S20
So what?

So what: Finland is a market of doors, not demand. The doors are held by McDonald's franchisees, S-Group, Restel, Kotipizza and a few funded domestic brands. A new brand wins by getting one of those operators, or an operator like them, to carry it. Growth in demand will not do it: volume rose about 1% in Jun 2026 and restaurant prices already sit 27% above the EU average.

READING THE TABLE

What it means

1

The rows that grow are not the biggest categories. Burger and pizza are large and held. The growth is at the edges: KFC's slow climb, a domestic burger brand with drive-through lanes, and quick service at fuel stations. That points to sites and operators, not tastes.

2

Two rows lean on one source type: pizza is only claims, and bubble tea and plant-based are searched and thin. We call no rise or fall on them. Coffee is flat in value, and the Fazer rise is in sales, not in a counted network.

3

The pattern repeats the Nordic lesson. Whatever the format, the brands that grew had an operator behind them: Apollo for KFC, Restel for Burger King and Taco Bell, S-Group for Hesburger, and a funded owner for Friends & Brgrs. Find that partner first.

05REGULATION AND COST TO OPERATE

No franchise law, a 20% company tax that may fall to 18%, and pay set by collective agreement: cheap to set up, steady to run

Finland has no franchise-specific law and no disclosure statute. Costs are set by tax, payroll and a collective pay table, not by licences. This page is information, not legal, tax or investment advice.

06ENTRY ROUTE AND RISKS

The proven routes run through a local operator: master franchise, a retail or fuel group, or direct franchise, with company stores the slowest

Four routes, scored 1 to 3. For control and capital, 3 is the most control kept and the most capital put in. For speed, 3 is fastest.

Licensed operator inside a retail or fuel groupMediumLowFastS-Group runs 106 Hesburgers, renewed 16 Jun 2026. Kotipizza is at over 90 fuel stations. Fazer Cafe opened at the airport through SSP on 27 Feb 2026 (S41, S24, S48).
Direct franchising, unit by unitMediumMediumSlowMcDonald's has 12 franchisees on 69 sites. Kotipizza is all franchised. Typical fees: joining a few thousand euro, service 5 to 10% of turnover, marketing 1 to 5% (S51, S22, S33).
Company-owned flagshipHighHighSlowFazer Ravintolat runs its own cafes and lost €0.3m on €44.7m sales in 2025. Hesburger's FoodStation is a company test (S49, S21).
Fit 1

A chicken or grill brand with a drive-through

Take the licensed-operator route with a fuel or retail group. It fits the sites being signed now, and it avoids KFC's one-chain pace.

Fit 2

A bakery-cafe brand

Start with a licensed flagship in a station or airport, then offer area rights. Fazer's loss shows that owned cafes alone do not pay.

Fit 3

A premium kebab or fast casual brand

Use a joint venture with a food group. Babista closed two sites in 13 months, while funded groups kept testing.

Six risks

Six risks, each sourced
Partner failure

KFC reached 9 sites in about five years, and a 2018 plan for 50 sites stalled for want of a franchisee. The wrong partner costs years. [S4, S6, S17]

Franchisee exit

Subway's master franchisee bought nine Finnish restaurants in Mar 2026. The buyer's release gives no reason the sellers left, so we draw no conclusion. [S2]

Cautious demand

Restaurant demand was subdued in 2026. Eepee's restaurant revenue fell 5.6% in Jan to Jun, partly from the Babista closure. [S52, S26]

Dear to run

Restaurant prices are 27% above the EU average, and pay is set by the PAM table with on-costs of about 20%. [S43, S10, S7]

Tax change

The 18% corporate rate from 2027 is a draft. Plan on 20% until it passes. [S8]

Thin data

No official franchise count, no bubble tea chain count and no total food-service spend in euros. Our unit counts come from company statements and our own locator reads. [S31, S37]

07YOUR FIRST 90 DAYS

Ninety days to a signed operator: spend the first 30 on the partner list, not on the menu

1

Days 1 to 7: write the partner list. Start with Restel, S-Group, Apollo, Nordic Bites Group, NoHo Partners and the Kotipizza and McDonald's franchisees (S19, S41, S4, S2, S16, S51).

2

Days 1 to 14: count your own sites in Finland by hand from the locators and apps. Record the date and method for each count.

3

Days 7 to 21: file the national trade mark at €250 for one class, and check EU coverage (S28).

4

Days 14 to 30: build the unit model on the all-in wage of £12.26 an hour, then test it at about £16.22 with supplements (S10, S7, S51).

5

Days 21 to 35: take the draft 18% corporate rate out of the base case and run both 20% and 18% (S8).

6

Days 30 to 45: meet three operators and ask each for its open sites at fuel stations and malls (S24, S19, S44).

7

Days 40 to 55: check each operator's own record. Ask how long its last new brand took to reach 20 sites, as KFC's 9 sites in about five years suggests it can be slow (S17).

8

Days 50 to 65: set the royalty and withholding position with a Finnish adviser, treaty first (S3).

9

Days 60 to 80: agree heads of terms with one operator, with a site list and a pace per year.

10

Days 80 to 90: register the food premises four weeks ahead for the first site and start hiring on the PAM table (S29, S10).

WHAT WE’D CHECK NEXT
  • Does the operator already hold fuel-station or mall sites for your format?
  • Can you name three openings in the past 12 months that the operator delivered?
  • Does your unit model still work at £16.22 an hour all-in?
  • Does it still work at 20% corporate tax and a 20% royalty withholding?
  • Do you have a plan if the operator reaches only nine sites in five years?
