Minimum Operating Standards

Local customisation: franchisees may propose additional seasonal campaigns relevant to their local market (e.g. local festival, university freshers' week), subject to Head Office approval of messaging and branding.

5.1 Opening/closing
Opening checklist
Introduction
This chapter provides the standard opening checklist for Imran’s Diner units. A disciplined opening routine ensures the unit is clean, safe, fully stocked, and ready to serve before the first customer walks in. Every shift that starts poorly cascades through the day.
Minimum Operating Standards
The opening team member (typically Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) or Unit Manager) must arrive at least 45 minutes before the unit's published opening time. Kitchen and prep team arrival is set by the prep schedule (see Chapter 5.2 — Prep schedules) and will usually be earlier. 45 minutes is the minimum needed to complete the sequence below properly, not a target to beat. If opening routinely requires significantly longer than 45 minutes, the cause is almost always an incomplete close the night before rather than a slow opening team — treat a lengthening open as a closing-checklist problem and address it there.
Opening checklist sequence: (1) Secure entry — check for any signs of break-in or damage before entering; report anything suspicious before proceeding. (2) Deactivate alarm system. (3) Lights and HVAC — turn on all lighting, heating/cooling, and ventilation/extraction systems. (4) Temperature checks — record fridge and freezer temperatures on the daily log; if any reading is outside the critical limit (fridges 0-4°C, freezers -18°C or below), follow the HACCP corrective action procedure (see Chapter 3.6). (5) Equipment power-on — switch on EPOS, coffee machine, blenders, display units, and any other equipment in the sequence set out in the equipment start-up guide. The start-up guide is derived from the equipment register (Chapter 2.2); until that register exists, the opening lead powers on to the unit's own documented equipment list, and the sequence is confirmed at the Sheffield baseline visit. (6) Visual cleanliness check — front of house, customer seating, counter, menu boards, windows, and entrance must be clean and presentable. (7) Restock — check and replenish front-of-house supplies (napkins, cups, lids, straws, retail display items). (8) Prep check — confirm with kitchen/prep that all prep for the day's first period is complete or in progress (see Chapter 5.2 — Prep schedules). (9) Cash float — verify the till float matches the expected amount; log the count on the daily cash sheet (see Chapter 5.1 — Cash handling). (10) Staff check — confirm all scheduled team members have arrived and are in uniform. (11) Final walk-through — the opening lead walks the entire unit (front to back) to confirm readiness. (12) Open doors at the published time.
The completed opening checklist must be signed by the opening lead and retained for 12 months, matching the retention period for temperature and cleaning records in Chapter 3.6 — the opening checklist records fridge and freezer readings, so a shorter period would break the evidence trail an Environmental Health officer follows.
If any critical item cannot be completed (e.g. equipment failure, staffing shortfall), the opening lead must notify the Unit Manager (currently Owner) / Franchisee before opening and document the issue.
Roles & Responsibilities
Opening Lead (Shift Supervisor or Unit Manager): Completes the checklist; signs off; escalates issues.
Team Members: Assist with restocking, cleaning, and prep tasks as directed.
Unit Manager / Franchisee: Available by phone during opening; addresses escalated issues.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) checklist completion rate (percentage of days with a fully completed, signed checklist), (b) late openings (unit opened after published time), (c) issues logged at opening and resolution times. Review weekly.
Tools & Resources
Printed opening checklist (daily, one per unit). Temperature log sheet. Daily cash sheet. Equipment start-up guide.
Closing checklist
Introduction
This chapter provides the standard closing checklist for Imran’s Diner units. A thorough closing routine ensures food safety standards are maintained overnight, the unit is secure, cash is reconciled, and the opening team inherits a clean and ready environment the next day.
Minimum Operating Standards
The closing team may begin close-down preparation up to 30 minutes before the published closing time, restricted to back-of-house tasks that are invisible to the customer and that do not reduce what can be served: deep-clean of unused equipment, waste consolidation, dry store tidy, stock counts, and preparation of banking paperwork. Until the published closing time the unit serves the full menu to the full standard. Cooking equipment serving live menu items is not switched off, the customer area is not cleaned down, chairs are not stacked, and no customer is turned away or discouraged. The last thirty minutes of trade are among the most profitable of the day for a late-night operation, and an early shutdown is the most common way a unit loses them.
Closing checklist sequence: (1) Last customer served and premises cleared. (2) Front of house — wipe down all surfaces, sweep and mop floors, clean customer seating and tables, empty bins, clean windows and glass if needed. (3) Kitchen/prep — clean all equipment, sanitise preparation surfaces, cover and date-label any open ingredients for storage, check use-by dates and discard expired items, sweep and mop kitchen floor. (4) Stock — move perishable items to appropriate cold storage, check fridge/freezer temperatures and log, secure dry stores. (5) Cash — complete till reconciliation per the cash handling procedure (see Chapter 5.1 — Cash handling); count cash, reconcile to EPOS, prepare banking if applicable, secure cash in safe. (6) EPOS — run the Presto end-of-day report, file it in the unit's daily records, and submit it with the daily report to the Unit Manager (currently Owner) for review the following morning (see Chapter 6.3 — Reporting cadence & ownership). Head Office receives trading data through the monthly report, not daily; the daily report is a unit-level control. (7) Equipment — switch off all non-essential equipment per the equipment shut-down guide; leave fridges and freezers running. (8) Waste — bag and remove waste to the designated waste area; separate recyclables per local authority requirements. (9) Security — check all windows and doors are locked, arm the alarm system. (10) Final sign-off — the closing lead completes and signs the checklist.
The completed closing checklist must be retained for 12 months, matching the opening checklist and the temperature and cleaning record retention in Chapter 3.6.
No team member should leave until the closing lead has completed the final walk-through and dismissed the team.
Roles & Responsibilities
Closing Lead (Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) or Unit Manager): Completes the checklist; signs off; ensures the unit is secure before leaving.
Team Members: Carry out cleaning, restocking, and close-down tasks as directed.
Unit Manager (currently Owner) / Franchisee: Reviews closing checklists periodically; addresses any recurring issues.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) checklist completion rate, (b) issues found by the opening team that should have been caught at close (e.g. cleaning not done, stock not stored correctly), (c) cash discrepancies identified at close. Review weekly.
Tools & Resources
Printed closing checklist (daily). Till reconciliation sheet. End-of-day EPOS report. Equipment shut-down guide. Waste disposal guide (local-authority-specific).
Cash handling & till reconciliation
Introduction
This chapter defines Imran’s Diner's cash handling and till reconciliation procedures. Rigorous cash handling protects the business from theft, fraud, and accounting errors, and ensures accurate financial reporting. The UK is increasingly cashless, but many QSR takeaway operations still handle meaningful volumes of cash. Note: HMRC's Making Tax Digital requirements mean EPOS/cash register systems must produce reliable digital records, and daily sales data must be retained (a minimum of 6 years is the common standard for VAT and company records).
Minimum Operating Standards
The till float must be a fixed, agreed amount of £100 per till. The float must be counted and verified at the start of every trading day (opening checklist) and at the end of every trading day (closing checklist).
All sales must be rung through the EPOS system. No sales may be conducted off-register. Every sale must produce a customer receipt, and the till system must meet HMRC's Making Tax Digital record-keeping requirements.
Voids and refunds on the EPOS must be authorised by the Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) or Unit Manager (currently Owner) and logged with a reason. Excessive voids or refunds must be investigated.
Cash drops: where the till holds more than £300, the excess must be removed and placed in the safe (a 'cash drop'). The £300 figure is an interim working threshold: cash drop and banking thresholds must be set at or below the cash-on-premises limit in the Head Office insurance schedule (Chapter 2.4) once that schedule is issued, because cash held above the insured limit is an uninsured loss and the unit will not discover that until it is robbed. Cash drops must be recorded on the daily cash sheet.
End-of-day reconciliation: count the physical cash in the till, subtract the opening float, and compare the result to the EPOS cash sales total. Any variance, over or short, must be recorded on the daily cash sheet with the name of the person who counted. A variance over £10 on a single day must be investigated and reported to the Franchisee. A variance of any size must also be investigated where the same team member records three or more variances in a rolling four-week period, or where variances run consistently in one direction — a persistent small shortage is a stronger signal than a single large one, and a persistent overage means the till is being worked incorrectly, not that the unit is making money.
Banking: cash must be banked at least twice per week, and in all cases before cash held on premises approaches the insured cash-on-premises limit. Banking should be varied in timing and route for security. Two people should handle banking where possible.
No personal money (IOUs, personal change) may be taken from or added to the till. No staff purchases may be processed outside normal EPOS procedures.
Card payment reconciliation: card terminal settlement reports must be reconciled against EPOS card sales daily.
All cash handling records (daily cash sheets, banking records, variance logs) must be retained for 6 years. The binding requirement is VAT record-keeping, which is 6 years; Companies Act 2006 s.388(4) requires only 3 years for a private company, so the VAT period is the one to work to.
Roles & Responsibilities
Opening Lead: Counts and verifies the opening float.
Shift Supervisor: Authorises voids and refunds; conducts cash drops; monitors till activity.
Closing Lead: Conducts end-of-day reconciliation; secures cash; completes daily cash sheet.
Unit Manager / Franchisee: Reviews daily cash sheets; investigates variances; arranges banking.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) daily cash variance (over/short), (b) number and value of voids/refunds, (c) banking frequency vs. target. Persistent variances or unusual void patterns must trigger an investigation. Review weekly.
Tools & Resources
Daily cash sheet (template). EPOS end-of-day cash report. Void/refund log. Banking log. Safe log (recording cash in/out of the safe).

Stock rotation (FIFO)
Introduction
This chapter defines Imran’s Diner's stock rotation procedures based on the FIFO (First In, First Out) principle. Proper stock rotation is a fundamental food safety requirement, minimises waste, and ensures customers always receive product made with the freshest ingredients. FIFO compliance is a standard Environmental Health inspection point.
Minimum Operating Standards
All stock must be rotated on a FIFO basis: the oldest stock (earliest delivery date or earliest production/prep date) must always be used first.
When new deliveries are received, existing stock must be moved to the front/top and new stock placed at the back/bottom of shelves and storage areas.
All items in cold storage, dry storage, and prep areas must be clearly date-labelled. For items transferred from original packaging, the label must include: product name, date of transfer/opening, and use-by date.
Use-by dates must never be extended or altered. 'Best before' dates are advisory but Imran’s Diner policy is to follow them unless Head Office guidance states otherwise for specific products.
Daily checks: as part of the opening procedure, a designated team member must check all cold and dry storage areas for items past their use-by date. Expired items must be removed, logged on the waste sheet, and disposed of.
Deliveries: upon goods receiving, check that delivered items have sufficient remaining shelf life for expected usage. Reject any delivery where items are damaged, at incorrect temperature (chilled goods above 4°C, frozen goods above -18°C), or have insufficient remaining shelf life. Sufficient remaining shelf life means the item will be fully used before its use-by date at current usage rates, with a minimum of 3 days remaining on any fresh item regardless of usage. An item delivered with less life than the unit will take to use it is waste that has already been paid for, and accepting it transfers the supplier's problem onto the unit's food cost. Short-dated deliveries must be recorded, not just rejected — a supplier delivering short-dated stock repeatedly is a supplier risk matter (Chapter 2.4 — Supplier risk & contingency), not a one-off.
Prep items (anything made in-house): must be labelled with the prep date and a use-within period. That period is set per item through the food safety management system and recorded against the item in the recipe book and portion specification directory (see Chapter 5.2 — Prep schedules); it is not set generically here, because a use-within period is a validated control measure specific to the product, its process and its storage temperature. Where an item's use-within period has not yet been set, it is prepped to order until it has. Prep items past their use-within date must be discarded.
Roles & Responsibilities
All Team Members: Apply FIFO when accessing and restocking products; date-label items correctly; report any stock concerns.
Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader): Conducts daily date checks; manages deliveries and goods receiving; ensures FIFO compliance during the shift.
Unit Manager (currently Owner): Monitors waste logs; investigates FIFO failures; includes FIFO compliance in training and performance management.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) waste due to expired stock (weight or cost per week), (b) FIFO compliance observed during audits (spot-check: are oldest items at the front?), (c) delivery rejections due to short shelf life or temperature issues. Excessive waste may indicate over-ordering or poor rotation discipline.
Tools & Resources
Date labels and label gun (or adhesive labels). Waste log/waste sheet. Delivery goods-receiving checklist. Temperature probe (for delivery checks).
Waste tracking & reporting
Introduction
This chapter defines Imran’s Diner's waste tracking and reporting procedures. Food waste is a direct cost to the business, an environmental concern, and a potential indicator of operational issues (over-ordering, poor stock rotation, inaccurate prep planning). Tracking waste systematically enables data-driven decisions to reduce it.
Minimum Operating Standards
All waste must be recorded on the daily waste log at the point of disposal. The log must capture: date, item, quantity (weight or units), reason for waste (expired, over-production, dropped/damaged, customer return, quality issue), and the team member recording it.
Waste categories: (1) Date-related waste (expired or past use-within), (2) Prep waste (over-production, trimmings, prep errors), (3) Service waste (customer returns, incorrect orders), (4) Quality waste (items not meeting standard on inspection), (5) Damaged/spillage.
Waste cost must be calculated and reported weekly, expressed as a percentage of food purchases (cost of waste / cost of food purchased). The weekly figure is used to spot patterns, not to judge performance: purchases move with ordering timing rather than with trade, so a week of stock-building reads artificially low and the week it is used reads artificially high. Performance is judged on a rolling four-week average, which removes that distortion. Target waste percentage: set from the four-week baseline established under Chapter 5.2 (Portioning & waste standards), with a stated improvement on it, and reviewed three months after it is set. This chapter holds the target; Chapter 5.2 refers to it rather than restating it. [Tawheed to confirm: target waste percentage, once the four-week baseline is complete] For context when the baseline lands: a well-run QSR kitchen typically runs food waste in the low single digits as a percentage of food cost. A baseline materially above that points at over-prep or over-ordering rather than at carelessness, and both are fixed by the prep quantity and par level rules in this chapter rather than by asking the team to be more careful.
Weekly waste review: the Unit Manager (currently Owner) must review the waste log weekly to identify patterns and take corrective action. Common patterns include: specific products consistently wasted (adjust order quantities or prep volumes), waste concentrated at specific times (adjust prep schedules), and waste concentrated around specific team members (training issue).
UK food waste context: the UK has national targets for food waste reduction (via WRAP's Courtauld Commitment 2030 and the Government's food waste reduction roadmap). While not directly binding on individual small businesses, waste reduction is a reputational positive and a cost saving, and larger businesses face mandatory food waste reporting under measures being phased in.
Waste disposal must comply with local authority waste regulations (Simpler Recycling / Environmental Protection Act 1990 duty of care). Food waste, general waste, paper/cardboard, glass, and plastics must be separated per local requirements, and businesses must use a registered waste carrier.
Roles & Responsibilities
All Team Members: Record waste accurately and immediately at the point of disposal.
Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader): Monitors waste during the shift; checks that the waste log is being completed.
Unit Manager: Conducts weekly waste review; calculates waste cost percentage; implements corrective actions; reports waste data to the Franchisee.
Franchisee: Reviews waste data monthly; addresses systemic issues (e.g. supplier quality, menu design).
Measure Analyse Improve
Track weekly: (a) total waste cost and waste cost as % of food purchases, (b) waste by category (to identify dominant causes), (c) trend over time (improving, stable, deteriorating). Set a waste reduction target and review progress quarterly.
Tools & Resources
Daily waste log (printed, one per day). Weekly waste summary template. Waste cost calculator (linked to product cost data). Local authority waste sorting guide.

5.5 Customer service standards
Service standards & scripts
The service sequence
1. Greet. Every customer is acknowledged within seconds of walking in, by a team member facing the door (Chapter 2.2 — Front-of-house layout standards). [Tawheed to provide: greeting wording — already answered on WhatsApp, to be transferred in verbatim] The wording is the franchisor's, not a template: it is the one line every customer hears first, and it is the cheapest piece of brand consistency in the whole manual.
2. Take the order and serve. Take the order clearly and confirm it back to the customer.
3. Receipt and wait time. Issue the receipt and tell the customer how long their order will be — “Thank you for ordering, it’ll be about X minutes.”
4. Keep the customer updated. If the wait is running longer than usual, update the customer before they have to ask, so no one is left wondering where their order is.
Common situations
Indecisive customers. Help them choose by pointing to what Imran’s Diner is known for — the Imran’s Special, the wedges and the fillet burger (Chapter 1.1 — Positioning vs. category).
Consistency. Every customer is greeted and treated the same way, whoever they are and however busy the unit is.
Large groups and children. A group of six or more is taken as a single order wherever possible, with one person ordering and one order number issued — splitting a large group across multiple tickets is the most common cause of items arriving at different times and of order errors at peak. The group is told the realistic wait before the order is placed, not after. Large collection or catering orders placed in advance are handled through the catering process rather than at the counter during service. Children are addressed directly and served with the same courtesy as any customer, and any allergen question involving a child is escalated to the Shift Supervisor without exception — the standard allergen process applies in full and is never shortened because the unit is busy (see Chapter 5.5 — Handling special requests/allergens at point of sale).
Development areas
Upselling and suggestive selling: approved approaches are to suggest a specific item, once, at the natural point in the order — a side with a main, a drink with a meal, or the known pairing for a signature item. Suggestive selling means naming one thing the customer may genuinely want, not reading a list. Not approved: asking more than once; suggesting a larger size by default; suggesting items the kitchen is backed up on; and scripted multi-item upsells at peak, which slow the queue and cost more in throughput than they earn in attachment. Any suggestion that delays an order at peak is a net loss, because the constraint at peak is throughput, not basket size. Because Imran's Diner does not discount (Chapter 1.1), attachment is the primary lever on average transaction value, so it is measured rather than assumed: attachment rate is reported from Presto as the percentage of main-item orders carrying a side or a drink, and reviewed monthly. Order accuracy & speed standards
Introduction
This chapter defines Imran’s Diner's standards for order accuracy and speed of service. In a QSR environment, getting the order right and delivering it quickly are the two most fundamental drivers of customer satisfaction. Consistently hitting these standards builds repeat custom; consistently missing them destroys it.
Minimum Operating Standards
Order accuracy target: 98% of orders delivered exactly as ordered, with the correct items, correct quantities, correct modifications, and correct allergen accommodations.
Speed of service target: 10–12 minutes average from order placement to the customer receiving their complete order, for dine-in and collection. This is a cook-to-order kitchen running peri grill, flat grill, fryer and pizza oven; the target reflects that, and is not benchmarked against assembly-line QSR. For delivery orders: 10–12 minutes from order acceptance to handoff to the driver. The unit is measured on prep-to-handoff only, because driver allocation and travel time are controlled by the delivery platform, not by the unit. Total delivery time is still monitored, because it drives the platform rating and therefore order volume (Chapter 4.3), but a unit is not held to account for a figure it cannot influence; where total delivery time is consistently poor while prep-to-handoff is on target, that is a platform matter for Head Office, not a unit performance matter.
Order accuracy process: (1) The team member taking the order must read it back to the customer before confirming on the EPOS. (2) The EPOS ticket/screen is the single source of truth for the make station. (3) Before handoff, the person assembling the order must verify each item against the ticket. (4) For allergen-flagged orders, a second check by the Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) or senior team member is required (see Chapter 3.6 — Allergen management).
Speed of service process: monitor ticket times through the EPOS where the system supports it. If the EPOS does not track ticket times, conduct manual time-and-motion observations at least weekly during peak periods.
When an order error occurs: apologise to the customer, remake the order immediately at no charge, log the error (item, cause, time), and brief the team. Do not blame specific team members publicly.
Common causes of order errors and mitigations: mishearing the customer (read back the order), unclear EPOS modifiers (standardise button labels), kitchen misreads (clear ticket formatting), and distraction during assembly (dedicated make station, no multitasking during peak).
Roles & Responsibilities
Team Members (order taking): Confirm orders with customers; enter accurately into EPOS.
Team Members (make station): Prepare orders exactly as per EPOS ticket; verify before handoff.
Shift Supervisor: Monitors ticket times; conducts second checks on allergen orders; addresses errors in real time.
Unit Manager (currently Owner): Reviews order accuracy and speed data weekly; identifies trends and implements training or process improvements.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track weekly: (a) order accuracy rate (correct orders / total orders), (b) average ticket time (order to handoff), (c) number and nature of order errors, (d) customer complaints related to order accuracy. Review weekly and set improvement targets where standards are not being met.
Tools & Resources
EPOS ticket time reports (if available). Order error log. Customer feedback/complaint log. Time-and-motion observation template (for manual speed checks).
Handling special requests/allergens at point of sale
Introduction
This chapter covers the specific procedures for handling customer special requests and allergen declarations at the point of sale. This supplements the allergen management chapter (Chapter 3.6) with the customer-facing process — what the team member at the counter or taking the order must do when a customer mentions an allergy, intolerance, or dietary requirement.
Minimum Operating Standards
When a customer declares an allergy or intolerance: the team member must (1) take it seriously — never minimise or dismiss, (2) check the allergen matrix for the requested item(s), (3) if confident the item is safe, confirm to the customer and flag the order on the EPOS as an allergen order, (4) if unsure, call the Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) or Unit Manager (currently Owner) — never guess, (5) if the allergen cannot be guaranteed absent (due to cross-contamination risk in a shared kitchen), inform the customer clearly and let them decide whether to proceed.
EPOS allergen flagging: all allergen orders must be flagged in Presto so that the make station and the handoff team both see it. The flag must be a structured field — a dedicated button or modifier that prints prominently on the kitchen ticket and at handoff — not a free-text note. A free-text note is not acceptable as the sole mechanism: notes are truncated on ticket printers, are easy to miss on a busy ticket, and cannot be reported on, so a unit using notes cannot evidence how many allergen orders it handled or whether the protocol was followed. [Tawheed to confirm, as part of the Presto feature audit: how Presto flags allergen orders, and whether a structured allergen modifier is available] Until a structured flag is configured, every allergen order is verbally confirmed between the order taker, the make station and the handoff, and that verbal handover is a named step the Shift Supervisor is accountable for.
Common special requests (non-allergen): dietary preferences (e.g. vegan, halal), portion modifications, ingredient swaps. The team member should accommodate where possible within the menu and operational constraints. Any request that requires deviation from the standard recipe must be approved by the Shift Supervisor.
Halal: Imran's Diner is not halal-certified as a business. All meat and meat-derived products sold in any unit must nonetheless be halal, sourced from suppliers who hold current halal certification of their own. This is a product specification, not a marketing claim, and it is not optional at unit level. Evidence: each unit holds on file the current halal certificate of every meat supplier it buys from, together with delivery records tying its purchases to those suppliers; certificates are checked at expiry and replaced before they lapse. Without business-level certification this supplier evidence chain is the only thing that substantiates the specification, so a unit that cannot produce it cannot demonstrate compliance. No substitution: meat may only be bought from suppliers on the approved list (Chapter 2.4). The approved-product rule — any wholesaler branch is acceptable provided the approved specification is bought — does not extend to meat, because halal status attaches to the supplier and not to the product description. No substitution without written Head Office approval, in any circumstances, including supply shortages. Scope beyond meat: the specification covers all products, so it reaches ingredients a meat certificate does not — alcohol-containing sauces and flavourings, gelatine in desserts, and animal rennet in cheese — and these must be checked against the approved specification in the recipe book and portion specification directory (Chapter 5.2) before any new product or supplier is introduced. What is said to customers: team members state only what is written on the menu and refer anything further to the Shift Supervisor. Any public statement about halal status — menu, website, delivery platforms, social media — is issued by Head Office, not written at unit level, because an unevidenced or overstated claim is a misleading commercial practice under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008. Refusal: if a request cannot be safely or practically fulfilled, the team member must explain why politely and suggest alternatives. The customer's safety always takes priority over making the sale.
Documentation: any allergen-related incident (customer reaction, near-miss, or complaint about allergen handling) must be reported immediately per Chapter 3.6 — H&S incident reporting.
Roles & Responsibilities
Team Members (order taking): First point of contact for allergen and special request declarations; follow the procedure above; never guess.
Shift Supervisor: Second-line support for allergen queries; verifies allergen-flagged orders before handoff.
Unit Manager: Ensures the allergen matrix is current and accessible; ensures all team members are trained; investigates any allergen incidents.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of allergen-flagged orders per week (indicates team is correctly identifying and flagging), (b) allergen-related complaints or incidents (target: zero), (c) allergen knowledge spot-check results (during audits, ask team members scenario-based questions). Review monthly.
