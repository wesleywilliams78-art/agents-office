5.6 Cleaning & maintenance
Daily/weekly/deep-clean schedules
Introduction
This chapter defines the cleaning schedules for Imran’s Diner units across three tiers: daily, weekly, and deep-clean. A clean unit is non-negotiable.
Minimum Operating Standards

Reports are retained with the equipment maintenance log and must be produced for insurers after any fire. The contractor is appointed from the Head Office approved contractor list (Chapter 2.6). 5.7 Complaint handling
Complaint resolution process
Introduction
This chapter defines Imran’s Diner's customer complaint resolution process. Every complaint is both a risk and an opportunity — handled well, a complaint can convert a dissatisfied customer into a loyal advocate. Handled poorly, it creates negative word-of-mouth and, in serious cases, legal or regulatory exposure. The goal is to resolve complaints quickly, fairly, and consistently.
Minimum Operating Standards
Every complaint — verbal, written, in-person, or online — must be acknowledged, taken seriously, and resolved. No complaint should be dismissed or ignored.
In-person complaints: the team member receiving the complaint must (1) listen without interrupting, (2) apologise sincerely (an apology is not an admission of liability), (3) offer an immediate resolution where within their authority (e.g. remake the order, offer a replacement), (4) if the issue is beyond their authority, escalate to the Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) or Unit Manager (currently Owner) immediately — the customer should not have to wait or re-explain.
Resolution authority levels: Team Members may offer an immediate product replacement or remake at no charge. Shift Supervisors may additionally offer a complimentary item or a modest goodwill gesture. Refund and compensation authority is reserved for the Unit Manager or Franchisee (see Chapter 5.7 — Refund/comp authority levels).
Written/online complaints (email, social media, review platform): acknowledge within 24 hours, investigate, and provide a substantive response within 72 hours. Where a complaint arrives on a public channel, the public response and the complaint resolution run in parallel and are not the same thing: the public reply follows the response times in Chapter 4.3 (48 hours, or 24 hours for a one- or two-star review) and its job is to acknowledge and move the conversation offline, not to resolve, while the substantive investigation and resolution follow the 72-hour standard here and happen privately. Resolving a complaint in public invites a negotiation in front of an audience, and the audience is the part that costs money. Where the 72 hours cannot be met because the investigation is genuinely incomplete — a supplier batch check, or a food safety matter — the customer is told so within the 72 hours, with a date: a holding response given on time is a response, silence is not. See Chapter 4.3 — Review/reputation management for online-specific guidance.
Logging: every complaint must be logged on the complaint register with: date, customer name (if given), nature of complaint, how it was received (in-person, phone, email, review), resolution offered, and outcome (customer satisfied/not satisfied). The register must be reviewed weekly by the Unit Manager.
Escalation triggers: complaints involving alleged food safety issues (illness, allergen reaction, foreign body), discrimination or harassment by staff, threatening or abusive customers, or potential legal claims must be escalated immediately to the Franchisee and Head Office (see Chapter 5.7 — Escalation thresholds).
UK consumer law note: the Consumer Rights Act 2015 gives consumers rights to remedy for goods (and services) that are not as described, not of satisfactory quality, or not fit for purpose. For food service, this entitles the customer to a replacement or refund. Repeated refusal to honour legitimate complaints could attract Trading Standards attention.
Learning: complaints are data. Recurring complaint themes must trigger process or training changes, not just individual resolutions.
Roles & Responsibilities
Team Members: First responders for in-person complaints; resolve within authority; escalate where needed.
Shift Supervisor: Handles escalated in-person complaints during the shift; logs complaints; provides higher-authority resolutions.
Unit Manager: Reviews complaint register weekly; investigates trends; handles written complaints; escalates serious matters.
Franchisee: Handles complaints involving legal risk, significant compensation, or reputational impact.
Head Office: Handles complaints with brand-wide implications; provides guidance on complex or sensitive cases.

Escalation thresholds
Introduction
This chapter defines the escalation thresholds for customer complaints and operational incidents — the criteria that determine when an issue must be escalated beyond the person handling it. Clear thresholds prevent under-escalation (where serious issues are resolved at too low a level) and over-escalation (where minor issues unnecessarily consume senior management time).
Minimum Operating Standards
Level 1 — Team Member resolves: minor product quality issues (e.g. wrong sauce, cold food), simple service delays, and general customer feedback. Resolution: remake, replace, or apologise.
Level 2 — Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) resolves: repeated or more significant product issues, minor customer dissatisfaction not resolved by the team member, team member conduct complaints (minor), and any situation where the team member is uncertain how to respond.
Level 3 — Unit Manager (currently Owner) resolves: requests for refunds, complaints involving multiple issues or persistent dissatisfaction, staff conduct complaints (serious or repeated), any complaint received in writing (email, letter), online review complaints, supplier quality issues, and equipment failures affecting service.
Level 4 — Franchisee resolves: complaints alleging food safety failure (illness, allergen incident, foreign body), any complaint threatening legal action, staff grievances or disciplinary matters, complaints alleging discrimination or harassment, complaints involving potential media or social media escalation, refund or compensation requests above the Unit Manager's authority (the thresholds are set in Chapter 5.7 — Refund/comp authority levels, and are not restated here), and any interaction with regulatory authorities (the local authority Environmental Health team, the HSE, Trading Standards, or the FSA).
Level 5 — Head Office involvement: any complaint with potential brand-wide implications, any media enquiry about a complaint, any legal claim or threat of legal proceedings, any incident requiring public communication, and any regulatory enforcement action.
When in doubt, escalate up. It is always better to over-escalate than to under-escalate a serious issue.
Every escalation must be documented: who escalated, to whom, when, the issue, and the outcome.

Head Office: Handles Level 5; provides guidance on borderline cases.

5.8 Business continuity & contingency procedures
Equipment failure protocol (fridge/freezer/oven down)
Introduction
This chapter defines what to do when a critical piece of equipment fails during trading hours or is discovered as failed at opening. Equipment failures in a food service environment are not just inconveniences — a fridge or freezer failure is a food safety emergency that can result in total product loss, Environmental Health enforcement action, or customer illness. A clear protocol ensures the team acts immediately to protect product, customers, and the business.
Minimum Operating Standards
Critical equipment is defined as any equipment whose failure directly affects food safety or the ability to trade: refrigeration (fridge, freezer, display chiller), cooking equipment (oven, hob, fryer, grill — as applicable to menu), hot and cold holding units, and the EPOS. The identified equipment bottleneck — the single item that caps the unit's throughput (Chapter 3.4) — is always critical equipment, whatever its type, because its failure stops trade even when nothing else has failed. Each unit holds its own named critical equipment list, drawn from the equipment register (Chapter 2.2) and issued with the opening pack. [Tawheed to provide, from the Sheffield equipment register: the named critical equipment list for the current unit]
Refrigeration failure — immediate actions: (1) Do not open the unit door unless absolutely necessary. A closed fridge maintains safe temperature for approximately 4 hours; a closed chest freezer for approximately 24 hours (these are guidelines — actual times vary). (2) Check whether the failure is the unit itself or the power supply (tripped breaker, power cut). Reset breakers if safe to do so. (3) Begin temperature monitoring — record the internal temperature every 30 minutes using a calibrated probe thermometer. (4) If internal temperature rises above 5°C (fridge) or -15°C (freezer), begin emergency product transfer to alternative cold storage if available. (5) If no alternative storage is available and temperatures exceed safe limits (above 8°C for fridge, above -12°C for freezer), all affected perishable product must be disposed of. Record all waste on the waste log with 'equipment failure' as the reason.
Cooking equipment failure — immediate actions: (1) Assess whether a reduced menu can be offered using remaining working equipment. (2) If the failed equipment is essential to the core menu and no workaround exists, the Unit Manager (currently Owner) must decide whether to continue trading with a reduced offering or to close temporarily. (3) Notify the Franchise Owner immediately if closure is being considered.
In all cases: (1) Contact the equipment maintenance provider or manufacturer's service line immediately. Record the call reference number. (2) Log the failure in the equipment maintenance log (see Chapter 5.6 — Equipment maintenance log) with: equipment name, time of failure, symptoms, actions taken, and service provider response. (3) If the failure affects food safety, complete a HACCP corrective action record (see Chapter 3.6).
Temporary closure: if the unit cannot safely trade due to equipment failure, the Unit Manager must (1) inform all scheduled staff, (2) post a notice at the entrance and update any active online ordering channels, (3) notify the Franchise Owner and Head Office, and (4) document the closure period and estimated reopening time.
Product loss: all product disposed of due to equipment failure must be recorded with quantities and estimated cost. This feeds into insurance claims (business interruption / stock loss) and the monthly waste report.
Post-repair: before returning the equipment to service, verify that it reaches the correct operating temperature and holds it stably, with the temperature probed and recorded before restocking with high-risk food. An empty unit reaches temperature quickly, so the check is that it holds, not that it gets there. For cooking equipment, the unit must reach and hold its required temperature across a full cook cycle before it is used for service. The legal position: the Food Safety (Temperature Control) Regulations 1995 require food likely to support pathogenic micro-organisms to be held at or below 8°C, and hot held food at or above 63°C; Imran's Diner operates to the stricter internal standard in Chapter 3.6 (fridges 0-4°C, freezers -18°C or below). Those regulations allow a temporary departure during equipment breakdown only for a limited period consistent with food safety, and set no required post-repair holding period, so the judgement of when a unit is stable rests with the Unit Manager and must be recorded rather than assumed. Record the verification in the equipment maintenance log with the time, the readings and who verified it. Where the repair was to equipment that failed during trading, the verification is done before the next trading session, not during it.
Roles & Responsibilities
Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader) / Opening Lead: First responder — executes the immediate protocol, begins temperature monitoring, contacts the service provider.
Unit Manager: Decides on reduced menu or temporary closure; notifies the Franchise Owner and Head Office; manages the insurance/waste documentation.
Franchise Owner / Operator: Authorises expenditure for emergency repair or replacement; liaises with insurers; reviews the incident for prevention.
Head Office: Maintains the approved equipment service provider list; supports insurance claims; reviews failure patterns across the network.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of equipment failures per quarter by type, (b) response time from failure to service provider attendance, (c) product loss value per incident, (d) trading hours lost to equipment-related closure. Review equipment failure frequency during monthly business reviews — recurring failures on the same equipment may justify early replacement rather than continued repair.
Tools & Resources
Equipment maintenance log (see Chapter 5.6). HACCP corrective action record (see Chapter 3.6). Waste log (equipment failure entries). Systems outage procedure (EPOS/the rota/scheduling system/due diligence system down)
Introduction
This chapter defines fallback procedures when one or more of Imran’s Diner's operational systems — EPOS, the rota/scheduling system, or due diligence system — become unavailable during trading. A systems outage does not mean the unit stops trading. It means the unit switches to manual processes until the system is restored. The key risk during an outage is not lost sales — it is lost data: transactions not recorded, hours not logged, and reconciliation gaps that take days to unpick. This chapter should be read alongside Chapter 5.4 (EPOS & systems use) for normal operating procedures.
Minimum Operating Standards
EPOS outage — fallback procedure: (1) Switch to manual order-taking using a pre-printed duplicate order pad (kept in back-of-house at all times). Each order slip must record: order number (sequential), items ordered, price, payment method (cash/card), and time. (2) If card payments are unavailable, switch to cash-only and display a notice at the counter and entrance. If a standalone card terminal is available as backup, use it — record transaction references on the manual order slip. (3) All manual order slips must be entered into the EPOS retrospectively once the system is restored. The Unit Manager (currently Owner) is responsible for ensuring this is completed by end of the same trading day (or the following morning if the outage extends to close). (4) Reconcile: compare manual order slip total against the till cash count and any standalone card terminal records. Document any discrepancy.
Rota/scheduling system outage — fallback procedure: (1) Record all clock-in and clock-out times on a paper sign-in sheet (kept at the staff entrance). Each entry: employee name, date, clock-in time, clock-out time, break duration. (2) The Unit Manager must enter all manual time records into the rota/scheduling system once the system is restored, within 24 hours of restoration and in all cases before the payroll cut-off for the period. Paper sign-in sheets are retained after entry, not discarded, until the payroll run covering those hours has been completed and checked — the paper record is the only evidence if the restored system log and the manual record disagree. (3) Any discrepancies between the paper record and the restored system log must be investigated and resolved before payroll is processed.
Due diligence system outage — fallback procedure: revert to paper for every check the system would otherwise capture — temperature logs, opening and closing checklists, cleaning sign-offs — using the printed templates each unit holds for this purpose. The checks themselves are never skipped because the system is down: they are food safety records, and an outage is not a defence for a gap in them. Completed paper records are entered into the system once restored, and the paper originals are retained alongside the digital record rather than replaced by it. [Tawheed to confirm: the due diligence system's name and the specific checks it captures, so the paper fallback templates match it one-for-one]. The principle is the same: capture the data manually, re-enter it when the system is restored, and reconcile.
General outage rules: (1) Attempt a restart before escalating — close and reopen the application, restart the device, check the internet connection. (2) If the issue persists after restart, contact the system provider's support line. Record the support ticket number. (3) Notify the Unit Manager (if not on shift) and the Franchise Owner if the outage exceeds 1 hour for any system that stops or degrades trading — EPOS, card payment, or online ordering — and if it exceeds 4 hours for any other system. An EPOS outage is notified immediately on confirmation, not after an hour, because the decisions that follow (cash-only trading, pausing delivery platforms, closing) are not the Shift Supervisor's to take alone. (4) Do not attempt any technical fixes beyond restart unless trained to do so — escalate to the system provider.
Manual-process supplies: each unit must maintain a minimum stock of pre-printed order pads, paper sign-in sheets, and a calculator in back-of-house at all times. These supplies must be checked monthly as part of the equipment/stock audit.
Post-outage: once the system is restored, the Unit Manager must (1) re-enter all manual records, (2) reconcile manual totals against system records, (3) document the outage in the equipment/systems log (date, time, duration, system affected, cause if known, data re-entered, discrepancies), and (4) report the outage to Head Office if it exceeded 1 hour or affected data integrity.

Supply disruption contingency
Procedure
Supply disruptions are rare at Imran’s Diner. When one happens, the unit follows these steps.
1. Check before trading. The Team Leader (People & Stock) checks stock of the critical ingredients against their minimum trading quantity before service. Minimum trading quantity is calculated, not estimated: average daily usage × the lead time in days to the backup source for that ingredient. The test is not whether the unit can trade today — it is whether it can trade until replacement stock can arrive. These figures come from the same average daily usage data that sets par levels (Chapter 5.3 — Ordering cycle & par levels) and are not collected separately.
2. Use the backup supplier. If a supplier cannot deliver, order from the backup supplier for that category (Chapter 2.4 — Supplier risk & contingency). This step does not apply to meat. Meat has no approved backup supplier, and the halal specification means it cannot be substituted at unit level in any circumstances, including supply shortages (Chapter 5.5 — Handling special requests/allergens at point of sale). A meat supply failure goes straight to step 3. Naming a backup butcher to the same specification and halal status is an outstanding Head Office action; until it exists, a meat supply failure is a menu reduction or a closure, and the business should size that risk accordingly.
3. Owner decides. If a critical ingredient still cannot be sourced, the Owner decides whether to reduce the menu or close temporarily.
4. Take items off sale everywhere. Any unavailable item is marked unavailable on every delivery platform and on the website immediately, and removed from sale at the till.
5. Tell customers. Customers are told by staff at the counter and through social media.
Emergency purchases
Salad items (lettuce, tomato, cucumber) may be bought from local retail as an emergency measure. Core items — chicken, burger and kebab meat, cheese, oil, batter and breading, marinades and sauces — must never be replaced with a product that does not meet the approved specification (Chapter 2.4 — Approved supplier list).
6. Development, Growth & Profit
6.1 Financial reporting & KPIs
Daily/weekly/monthly reporting templates
Introduction
This chapter defines the reporting cadences and template structures for financial and operational reporting at Imran’s Diner. Consistent reporting gives the Franchisee and Head Office visibility into unit performance, enables early identification of issues, and supports data-driven decision-making. Reporting is not bureaucracy — it is the mechanism through which the business stays in control.
Minimum Operating Standards
Daily report: completed by the Closing Lead or Unit Manager (currently Owner) at end of day. Contents: total revenue (cash and card split), transaction count, average transaction value, labour hours worked, any significant variances (cash discrepancy, waste event, customer complaint), and a brief operational note (e.g. equipment issue, staffing change). Submitted before the next trading day to the shared reporting location specified by Head Office — one folder per unit, one file per period, using the Head Office template. Email is a fallback when the shared location is unavailable, not the standard: reports that arrive as email attachments cannot be aggregated across units, and the purpose of standardised templates is lost the moment the data lives in an inbox. [Tawheed to confirm: the shared reporting location, which should be the same system that holds the Head Office template library]
Weekly report: completed by the Unit Manager, submitted to the Franchisee by Monday 9am for the prior week, ahead of the weekly ops review. Contents: total revenue vs. budget/forecast, labour cost as % of revenue, food cost as % of revenue (once EPOS margin data is available — currently blocked until EPOS margin data is configured), waste cost as % of food cost, customer complaint summary, staffing changes (new starters, leavers, absences), and top 3 operational priorities for the coming week.
Monthly report: completed by the Unit Manager with Franchisee input, submitted to Head Office by the 5th of the following month, or the next working day where the 5th falls on a weekend or bank holiday. Contents: full P&L summary (revenue, COGS, labour, overheads, net profit), KPI dashboard (see Chapter 6.1 — Core KPI definitions), year-to-date performance vs. budget, marketing activity summary, training and compliance status, maintenance and capex summary, and a narrative section covering key achievements, challenges, and plans.
All reports must use the standardised templates provided by Head Office to ensure consistency across franchise locations.
Reports must be retained for 6 years. The binding requirement is VAT record-keeping, which is 6 years; Companies Act 2006 s.388(4) requires only 3 years for a private company, so the VAT period governs. This matches the cash handling record retention in Chapter 5.1.
Roles & Responsibilities
Closing Lead: Completes the daily report.
Unit Manager: Completes weekly and monthly reports; ensures daily reports are submitted.
Franchisee: Reviews weekly and monthly reports; takes action on variances; contributes to monthly narrative.
Head Office: Provides report templates; reviews monthly reports; benchmarks performance across locations; raises concerns.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) report submission rate (% submitted on time), (b) data quality (completeness and accuracy), (c) action items arising from report reviews and their completion rate. Late or incomplete reporting is an early warning sign of operational drift.
Tools & Resources
Daily report template. Weekly report template. Monthly report template (including P&L and KPI dashboard). Shared reporting location (one folder per unit, one file per period — specified by Head Office, see the daily report standard above).
Core KPI definitions
Introduction
This chapter defines the core Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for Imran’s Diner units. KPIs are the vital signs of the business — a small set of metrics that, tracked consistently, give a clear picture of financial health, operational quality, and customer satisfaction. Every KPI must have a clear definition, a data source, a target, and an owner.
Minimum Operating Standards
Financial KPIs: (a) Revenue — total sales (ex. VAT), tracked daily/weekly/monthly against budget. (b) Average Transaction Value (ATV) — revenue divided by transaction count; indicates upselling effectiveness and product mix. Target: not yet set. ATV is an output of menu mix and attachment rate, so it is set once Presto reporting provides a baseline and the attachment rate measure in Chapter 5.5 is running, rather than asserted ahead of them. [Tawheed to confirm: ATV target, once Presto reporting is configured] (c) Labour Cost % — total labour cost (gross wages + employer social costs) as a percentage of revenue. Target: 24% of revenue, fully loaded — including employer's NI, pension contributions and holiday pay accrual. The wages-only equivalent is derived from the labour model (Chapter 3.4 — Labour-to-sales ratio) and stated alongside it once that model is complete, so that a franchisee building a P&L works from the all-in figure rather than the wages line. The target is locked as written and reviewed once the labour modelling exercise establishes what is achievable against the trading pattern.4 — Labour-to-sales ratio). (d) Food Cost % — total cost of goods sold as a percentage of revenue. Target: 25% of revenue, equivalent to a 75% gross margin. Current performance is a 63% gross margin, so this is a stated improvement target and not a description of today. Closing that gap is worth roughly 12 percentage points of revenue, which makes it the single largest profit lever identified in this manual — it is a repricing and re-specification programme, not a housekeeping adjustment. It is approached through three levers — price, portion and specification, and menu mix — and delivered through the menu engineering exercise and the margin targets in Chapter 6.2, not by cutting quality. Measurement is currently blocked until Presto margin data is configured: the EPOS reports 100% margin because recipe costs have not been loaded. The target is locked as written and reviewed once menu engineering establishes what is achievable product by product.2 — COGS & recipe costing). (e) Net Profit Margin — net profit as a percentage of revenue after all costs. Target: not yet set. Net margin is an output of the food cost, labour and overhead targets above, so it is set once those are confirmed and a full year of actuals exists, rather than asserted ahead of them. [Tawheed to confirm: net profit margin target] (f) Marketing Spend % of Revenue — total marketing expenditure (LSM + any central marketing fund contribution) as a percentage of net revenue, tracked monthly. Target: 2%. Local marketing spend is measured against gross revenue for the year, not against monthly or net revenue — a quiet month does not reduce the obligation and a strong month does not discharge it early. The minimum local spend is to be built into the franchise agreement, so that it is a contractual obligation rather than a manual standard (see Chapter 4.2 — Local marketing toolkit). (g) Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) by Channel — where trackable via EPOS, loyalty data, or campaign-specific codes: marketing spend divided by new customers acquired, broken out by channel (social media, delivery apps, local activity). Track monthly where data permits; note limitations where attribution is approximate.
Operational KPIs: (a) Order Accuracy Rate — percentage of orders delivered correctly (see Chapter 5.5). Target: 98%. (b) Speed of Service — average time from order to the customer receiving the complete order, measured to driver handoff for delivery orders (see Chapter 5.5). Target: 10–12 minutes. (c) Waste % — cost of waste as a percentage of food purchases, judged on a rolling four-week average (see Chapter 5.3 — Waste tracking & reporting). The target is held in Chapter 5.3 and set from the four-week baseline; it is not restated here. (d) Cleaning/Compliance Audit Score — score from internal operational audits. Target: not yet set — the internal operational audit structure does not yet exist. The audit framework is a Head Office build; until it exists this KPI is not reported, and no score should be recorded against an audit with no defined scope. [Tawheed to confirm: audit score target, once the audit structure is built]
People KPIs: (a) Staff Turnover Rate — number of leavers in the period divided by average headcount, annualised. Target: below 70% annualised. Turnover is currently tracking well against the sector, and 70% is set as the ceiling rather than the expectation: a sustained rise towards it is an early-warning indicator in its own right (see Underperformance early-warning indicators). (b) Absence Rate — days lost to absence as a percentage of total working days. Target: 3% or below. Absence is low at present; 3% is set as a neutral working target against the ONS UK average of 2% of working hours lost (4.4 days per employee per year), allowing for the higher absence typical of hospitality shift work. Reviewed once the business has a full year of its own data. (c) Training Compliance — percentage of team members with current, completed training records. Target: 100%.
