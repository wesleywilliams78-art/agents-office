Measure Analyse Improve
Track monthly: (a) inventory holding period in days (stock value ÷ daily COGS), (b) average payables days (total payables ÷ daily purchases), (c) card settlement lag (days from transaction to bank receipt), (d) MVA liability vs. MVA reserve balance. The goal is a negative working capital cycle (customers pay before suppliers are due) — normal for a QSR. If the cycle turns positive (you're paying out before cash comes in), investigate immediately.

Underperformance early-warning indicators   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter defines the financial and operational indicators that signal a unit is underperforming and may require formal intervention. (2) A review meeting is scheduled within [Adnan to confirm: e.g. 10 working days] Right between the Franchise Owner, Unit Manager, and Head Office. Formal remediation process & timeline   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter defines the structured process for remediating underperformance at a Creamy unit once an early-warning indicator has been triggered and a review meeting has taken place. The remediation process is deliberately structured to mirror the readiness criteria in Chapter 6.4: just as expansion readiness is assessed against objective criteria with no partial credit, remediation is assessed against a formal improvement plan with defined milestones and a fixed timeline. The goal is always recovery — but the process must also create a clear, documented trail that supports escalation to franchise agreement default and termination if recovery does not materialise (see Chapter 6.7 — Franchise agreement default & termination conditions).
Minimum Operating Standards
Stage 1 — Improvement Plan (triggered by Underperformance Notice): Within [Adnan to confirm: e.g. 10 working days] Right of the review meeting, Head Office and the Franchise Owner must jointly agree a written Improvement Plan. The plan must specify: (a) the specific indicators that triggered the process, (b) root causes identified, (c) corrective actions (specific, measurable, with named owners), (d) interim milestones (e.g. monthly checkpoints), (e) success criteria (the indicator thresholds that must be met for the remediation to be considered successful), and (f) the review period (typically [Adnan to confirm: e.g. 3 months]).Right
Stage 2 — Monitored Improvement: During the review period, performance is tracked against the Improvement Plan milestones at the agreed frequency (typically monthly). Head Office provides support as agreed in the plan (additional training, operational visits, marketing support, etc.). The Franchise Owner must provide requested data and implement agreed actions within the stated timelines.
Stage 3 — Review: At the end of the review period, a formal assessment is conducted: (a) All indicators met — remediation successful; the unit exits the formal process. The case remains on file for [Adnan to confirm: e.g. 12 months] Right for monitoring purposes. (b) Partial improvement — indicators trending in the right direction but not yet met. Head Office may extend the review period by [Adnan to confirm: e.g. one further month] Right, maximum one extension. (c) No improvement or deterioration — escalation to Stage 4.
Stage 4 — Final Notice: Head Office issues a Final Notice specifying: (a) which improvement criteria were not met, (b) what further action the Franchise Owner must take, (c) a final review date (typically [Adnan to confirm: e.g. 30 days]) Right, and (d) the consequences of continued non-improvement (which may include franchise agreement default proceedings as defined in Chapter 6.7 — Franchise agreement default & termination conditions).
Documentation: every stage must be documented in writing, signed by both parties, and retained. This documentation is essential both for fairness (the franchisee must have clear notice of what is expected) and for legal enforceability if the process escalates to termination.
Support: the remediation process is not adversarial. Head Office must provide genuine, practical support during the improvement period — not just monitoring. If the Franchise Owner identifies a barrier that requires Head Office action (e.g. a supplier issue, a system deficiency, a brand-level marketing gap), Head Office must address it or acknowledge it as a mitigating factor.
Roles & Responsibilities
Head Office: Drafts the Improvement Plan (jointly with the Franchise Owner); provides support during the monitored period; conducts interim and final reviews; issues Final Notices; maintains documentation.
Franchise Owner / Operator: Participates in drafting the Improvement Plan; implements corrective actions; provides data and access for monitoring; escalates barriers to Head Office.
Unit Manager: Executes day-to-day corrective actions; provides operational data; participates in interim reviews.
Legal Advisor (if escalation to Stage 4): Reviews documentation trail; advises on franchise agreement implications; supports any formal dispute process.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of Improvement Plans initiated per year, (b) percentage resolved successfully at Stage 3 (target: majority — if most cases escalate to Stage 4, the support model may be inadequate), (c) average duration from Underperformance Notice to resolution, (d) number of cases escalated to Final Notice, (e) number of cases resulting in franchise agreement default proceedings. Review the remediation process itself annually — if it is not resulting in recoveries, the process or the support model needs adjustment.
Tools & Resources
Improvement Plan template (standardised format: indicators, root causes, corrective actions with owners and deadlines, milestones, success criteria, review dates). Interim review template (progress against each milestone). Final Notice template (formal letter). Case file (chronological documentation of all notices, meetings, plans, and reviews for each remediation case). Performance data (from EPOS, financial reports, audit scores, customer feedback — as per Chapter 6.1).
