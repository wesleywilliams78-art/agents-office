Holiday timing: there is no UK statutory main holiday window (unlike some other jurisdictions). Annual leave is not blocked outright for any period. Instead, the number of team members on leave at the same time is capped so that the minimum five positions remain covered on every shift, and leave during the unit's highest-volume periods requires the Unit Manager's approval at the point of request rather than automatic acceptance. The periods treated as high-volume are set per unit from its own trading data and listed in the pre-opening pack. The employer may refuse or require leave to be taken at specified times, provided notice at least twice the length of the leave requested is given (Working Time Regulations 1998, reg. 15).
Leave requests must be submitted via the rota/scheduling system at least 4 weeks in advance for periods of 1 week or more, and at least 1 week in advance for single days, so that requests land before the rota for that period is built. The Unit Manager (currently Owner) approves or declines requests based on operational requirements.
Sick leave: employees can self-certify sickness absence for up to 7 calendar days without needing evidence from a doctor. Absence of up to 7 calendar days is self-certified on the Imran's Diner self-certification form, completed on the employee's first day back and held in the personnel file. Head Office issues the form. Absence beyond 7 calendar days requires a fit note (formerly a 'sick note') from a GP or other authorised healthcare professional.
Statutory Sick Pay (SSP): since 6 April 2026, SSP is payable from the first day of sickness absence and there is no minimum earnings requirement (Employment Rights Act 2025). Eligible employees receive £123.25 a week or 80% of their normal weekly earnings, whichever is lower, paid by the employer (not the state) for up to 28 weeks. The rate changes each April — check gov.uk/statutory-sick-pay before relying on the figure. Imran’s Diner should confirm whether it will offer any company sick pay above the statutory minimum.
Absence reporting: team members must notify the Unit Manager of any unplanned absence as early as possible and in any event before the shift starts, by telephone call to the unit. A voicemail, text message or message passed through a colleague is not acceptable notification unless a call has been attempted first. The Unit Manager records the absence in the rota/scheduling system on the day it occurs. Because Statutory Sick Pay is payable from the first day of absence, the reporting record is also the pay record and must be accurate.
Other statutory leave types (for reference): maternity leave (up to 52 weeks, with Statutory Maternity Pay for up to 39 weeks), paternity leave (up to 2 weeks), shared parental leave, unpaid parental leave (up to 18 weeks per child up to their 18th birthday), time off for dependants (a reasonable, unpaid amount to deal with an emergency involving a dependant), and bereavement leave. Imran's Diner provides 3 days' paid bereavement leave on the death of a close family member, with additional unpaid leave at the Unit Manager's discretion. Statutory Parental Bereavement Leave of 2 weeks applies where a child dies. The Employment Rights Act 2025 extends statutory bereavement leave more broadly; Head Office will confirm the entitlement and update this chapter when the regulations are made.

3.6 Food safety/H&S
Allergen management
Introduction
This chapter defines Imran’s Diner's allergen management procedures. Allergen management is a critical food safety obligation and a legal requirement in the UK under the Food Information Regulations 2014 (implementing retained EU Regulation 1169/2011 on food information to consumers) and Natasha's Law (Food Information (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2019, full ingredient/allergen labelling for pre-packed for direct sale, or PPDS, food). Failure to manage allergens correctly can cause serious illness or death and exposes the business to criminal liability.
Minimum Operating Standards
The 14 mandatory allergens that must be declared are: (1) cereals containing gluten, (2) crustaceans, (3) eggs, (4) fish, (5) peanuts, (6) soybeans, (7) milk (including lactose), (8) nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, cashews, pecans, Brazil nuts, pistachios, macadamias), (9) celery, (10) mustard, (11) sesame seeds, (12) sulphur dioxide/sulphites (>10mg/kg), (13) lupin, (14) molluscs.
Every menu item must have a documented allergen profile listing which of the 14 allergens are present. This information must be kept current and updated whenever a recipe or ingredient changes.
Allergen information must be available to customers at the point of sale. For non-pre-packed food (which covers most QSR takeaway service), allergen information must be provided orally on request, but a written reference (allergen matrix or menu annotation) must be available to support staff in providing accurate information. Any item sold as 'pre-packed for direct sale' (PPDS) — e.g. wrapped or boxed ahead of the counter for collection or delivery — must instead carry full ingredient labelling with allergens emphasised, per Natasha's Law.
Cross-contamination prevention: separate utensils, preparation surfaces, and storage must be used where practicable for allergen-free preparation. Where full separation is not possible (common in a small QSR kitchen), the risk must be documented and communicated to the customer ('may contain' warnings).
All team members must receive allergen awareness training during onboarding and refresher training at least annually (see Chapter 3.3).
When a customer declares an allergy: the order must be flagged (verbally and on the EPOS ticket), the team member must check the allergen matrix, the manager or a trained senior team member must verify the order before it is served, and if there is any doubt, the customer must be informed that the allergen cannot be guaranteed absent.
Ingredient supplier specifications must be kept on file for all products. Any supplier change or ingredient reformulation must trigger a review of the allergen matrix.
Allergen-related incidents (customer reaction, near-miss, or complaint) must be reported immediately to the Unit Manager (currently Owner) and logged as a food safety incident (see Chapter 3.6 — H&S incident reporting).
Roles & Responsibilities
Unit Manager: Maintains the allergen matrix; ensures all team members are trained; investigates allergen incidents.
Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader): Verifies allergen-flagged orders before service; manages allergen queries during service.
Team Members: Follow allergen procedures; ask customers about allergies; flag orders correctly.
Head Office: Provides allergen matrix templates and training materials; updates the matrix when central recipes change.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) allergen-related customer complaints (target: zero), (b) allergen training completion rates, (c) allergen matrix currency (last review date). Conduct allergen knowledge spot-checks during audits — ask team members to identify allergens in specific products.
Tools & Resources
Allergen matrix (product x allergen grid, displayed in kitchen and available at point of sale). Allergen awareness training materials. Allergen incident report form. Supplier specification files.
HACCP/food safety procedures
Introduction
This chapter covers Imran’s Diner's HACCP-based food safety procedures. Every food business in the UK must have a documented HACCP-based system in place (Regulation (EC) 852/2004, retained in UK law), most commonly implemented via the Food Standards Agency's 'Safer Food Better Business' (SFBB) pack. The local authority's Environmental Health Officers will inspect for compliance and award a Food Hygiene Rating.
Minimum Operating Standards
Imran’s Diner's food safety system is based on the seven HACCP principles: (1) Conduct a hazard analysis, (2) Determine Critical Control Points (CCPs), (3) Establish critical limits, (4) Establish monitoring procedures, (5) Establish corrective actions, (6) Establish verification procedures, (7) Establish documentation and record-keeping.
Critical Control Points for a QSR takeaway operation typically include: (a) Goods receiving — check delivery temperatures (chilled goods must arrive at 0-4°C, frozen at -18°C or below), check use-by dates, check packaging integrity; (b) Cold storage — fridges must operate at 0-4°C, freezers at -18°C or below, temperatures checked and logged at least twice daily; (c) Cooking — core temperature must reach at least 75°C (or an equivalent time/temperature combination); (d) Hot holding — food must be held at 63°C or above; (e) Allergen control (see separate chapter); (f) Cleaning and sanitisation; (g) Delivery/collection packaging — hot and cold items packaged and labelled correctly to maintain safe temperature in transit. These are the standard starting points for a QSR takeaway. Each unit's HACCP plan must be completed against its own menu, equipment and layout, and reviewed whenever the menu, equipment or supplier base changes. Head Office issues the HACCP plan template and the completed Sheffield plan as the reference; the franchisee remains responsible for the plan being accurate for their own unit.
Temperature logs: fridge and freezer temperatures must be recorded at least twice daily (morning and afternoon). Any reading outside the critical limit must trigger immediate corrective action (check probe accuracy, adjust thermostat, relocate stock if necessary) and be documented.
Cleaning schedules: documented daily, weekly, and deep-clean schedules must be in place and signed off (see Chapter 5.6 — Cleaning & maintenance).
Personal hygiene: all food handlers must wash hands before handling food, after touching raw ingredients, after using the toilet, after handling waste, and after sneezing/coughing. Hand sanitiser is not a substitute for handwashing. Clean uniform policy applies at all times.
Traceability: under Regulation (EC) 178/2002 (retained in UK law as 'assimilated law' post-Brexit), every food business must be able to trace ingredients one step back (supplier) and one step forward (customer, where applicable). Delivery records and batch information must be retained for a minimum of 12 months. Temperature, cooking and cleaning records are retained for 12 months beyond the shelf life of the food they relate to. Allergen documentation — the matrix, supplier specifications and any allergen incident record — is retained for 5 years, because allergen claims surface long after the event. Where records are held digitally, a copy must remain available on site for Environmental Health inspection.
Pest control: documented pest control arrangements must be in place (see Chapter 5.6 — Pest control protocol).
SFBB/HACCP documentation must be kept on-site and available for Environmental Health inspection at all times. It must include: HACCP plan, temperature logs, cleaning records, training records, pest control records, supplier specifications, allergen matrix, and any corrective action records.
Roles & Responsibilities
Unit Manager (currently Owner): Designated as the food safety lead for the unit; maintains the SFBB/HACCP system; ensures daily compliance; handles Environmental Health inspections.
Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader): Conducts temperature checks; signs off cleaning schedules; monitors food handling practices during shifts.
Team Members: Follow food safety SOPs; report any concerns or breaches immediately.
Head Office: Provides the HACCP plan template and SFBB framework; conducts internal food safety audits; updates procedures when regulations change.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) temperature log compliance (percentage of checks completed on time), (b) cleaning schedule sign-off completion, (c) Environmental Health inspection results / Food Hygiene Rating, (d) food safety incidents and corrective actions. Conduct internal food safety audits at least quarterly.
Tools & Resources
HACCP plan document. Temperature log sheets (fridge, freezer, delivery). Cleaning schedule templates. SFBB folder (physical or digital, containing all required documentation). Corrective action log. Supplier specification files.
H&S incident reporting
Introduction
This chapter sets out the health and safety incident reporting procedures for Imran’s Diner. UK law requires employers to maintain a safe working environment (Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974) and to report certain incidents to the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) under RIDDOR (the Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013). Under-reporting of incidents is both a legal risk and a missed opportunity to prevent recurrence.
Minimum Operating Standards
All incidents must be reported, no matter how minor. An 'incident' includes: any injury to a team member or customer (however slight), any near-miss (an event that could have caused injury but did not), any property damage, any fire or fire alarm activation, any food safety event (customer illness, allergen reaction, contamination), and any criminal activity on the premises.
Immediate actions: (1) ensure the safety of all persons, (2) administer first aid if trained and safe to do so, (3) call emergency services if required (999, or 112), (4) preserve the scene where relevant (do not clean up until the cause is understood), (5) notify the Unit Manager (currently Owner) immediately. At least one trained first aider must be on site during all trading hours; a kitchen with fryers, grills and knives is not a low-hazard workplace and an appointed person who can only summon help is not adequate cover. Head Office specifies the required qualification level in the pre-opening pack.
Reporting under RIDDOR: work-related deaths and 'specified injuries' (fractures other than to fingers/thumbs/toes, amputations, loss of sight, serious burns, and others listed in RIDDOR) must be reported to the HSE without delay, and within 10 days in writing; injuries causing incapacitation for more than 7 consecutive days must be reported within 15 days; certain dangerous occurrences (defined near-misses) must be reported regardless of injury. Reports are made via the HSE's online RIDDOR system. Imran's Diner consults employees directly on health and safety, as required by the Health and Safety (Consultation with Employees) Regulations 1996. Where a trade union is recognised at a unit, safety representatives are appointed under the Safety Representatives and Safety Committees Regulations 1977 instead. Consultation covers changes affecting health and safety, the information employees receive, and the planning of health and safety training. A written health and safety policy is mandatory once a business has 5 or more employees; every Imran's Diner unit exceeds that threshold from opening, because minimum staffing alone is five positions. Head Office issues the policy template; the Franchisee, as employer, signs and maintains it.
Internal reporting: all incidents must be recorded on the Imran’s Diner incident report form within 24 hours. The form captures: date, time, location, persons involved, description of what happened, immediate actions taken, root cause (preliminary), and follow-up actions required.
Investigation: all incidents must be investigated proportionally. Minor incidents require a brief root-cause note. Serious incidents require a full investigation including witness statements, timeline reconstruction, and identification of systemic causes.
Follow-up: corrective actions must be assigned, given a deadline, and tracked to completion. The Unit Manager must review all open actions weekly.
Incident data must be reviewed monthly to identify trends (e.g. recurring slip hazards, equipment-related injuries) and inform preventive action.
Roles & Responsibilities
All Staff: Report all incidents immediately; cooperate with investigations.
Unit Manager: Completes incident report forms; conducts investigations; reports to the HSE under RIDDOR where required; tracks corrective actions.
Safety representative (where appointed): Participates in investigations; advocates for employee safety concerns.
Franchisee: Ensures the reporting system is in place; reviews incident trends; ensures RIDDOR reporting obligations are met.
Head Office: Reviews incident data across franchise locations; identifies systemic issues; updates procedures as needed.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) total number of incidents reported per month (low numbers may indicate under-reporting, not safety), (b) time from incident to report completion, (c) corrective action completion rate and timeliness, (d) incident trends by type, location, and time. Review monthly at unit level and quarterly at franchise level.
Tools & Resources
Incident report form (paper and/or digital). Investigation template (for serious incidents). Corrective action tracker. First aid kit check log. HSE RIDDOR reporting portal (riddor.hse.gov.uk). Emergency contact poster (displayed prominently).

Review/reputation management
Introduction
This chapter covers the management of online reviews and the unit's digital reputation. Online reviews on platforms like Google, TripAdvisor, Facebook, and delivery apps directly influence customer acquisition. A proactive, professional approach to reviews — both positive and negative — is essential.
Minimum Operating Standards
Google Business Profile is the priority platform and is monitored daily. Every delivery platform the unit trades on also carries its own rating, and that rating directly affects the unit's ranking in the app — a rating drop costs order volume before it costs reputation, so delivery platform reviews are monitored daily on the same cycle. The unit's current live platform list is recorded on the unit channel record (see Chapter 4.3 — Social media posting standards) rather than fixed here, because it changes when delivery contracts change. TripAdvisor is monitored but not actively worked: it drives negligible traffic for a takeaway and the effort belongs on Google and the delivery apps.
All reviews must receive a response within 48 hours. One- and two-star reviews are responded to within 24 hours — a negative review left unanswered is the one prospective customers read, and the response is what the next reader judges, not the complaint. Positive reviews: a brief, genuine thank-you acknowledging the specific feedback. Negative reviews: acknowledge the concern, apologise where appropriate, offer to resolve the issue offline (provide a contact email or phone number), and never argue or become defensive publicly.
Response tone: consistent with Imran’s Diner's brand voice — warm, professional, and solution-oriented. Do not use copy-paste template responses for every review; personalise the response to the specific feedback.
Encouraging reviews: team members may politely encourage satisfied customers to leave a review, but must never offer incentives for reviews (this violates most platform terms and can breach the CAP Code if the incentive is not disclosed). A discreet counter card or receipt prompt is acceptable.
Negative review escalation: any review that (a) alleges a food safety issue, (b) alleges discrimination or harassment, (c) threatens legal action, or (d) goes viral / attracts media attention, must be escalated to the Franchisee and Head Office immediately before responding.
Review data should be analysed monthly to identify recurring themes (positive and negative) and inform operational improvements.
Fake or malicious reviews: do not respond publicly. Report to the platform for removal using the platform's dispute process. Document the report.
Roles & Responsibilities
Unit Manager (currently Owner): Monitors and responds to reviews daily; escalates serious reviews; reports monthly trends.
Franchisee: Handles escalated reviews; approves responses to high-sensitivity reviews; acts on recurring feedback themes.
Head Office: Provides response guidelines and templates; monitors brand reputation across all franchise locations; manages any PR/media issues arising from reviews.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track monthly: (a) average rating on each platform, (b) total number of reviews (growth trend), (c) response rate and average response time, (d) sentiment analysis (ratio of positive to negative reviews), (e) recurring themes from negative reviews and actions taken. Target: a minimum of 4.3/5 average on Google, with 4.5/5 as the standard of excellence, and the one-star share of all reviews below 8%. The one-star share is the controlling metric: a site does not move up the scale by collecting more five-star reviews, it moves up by removing the causes of one-star reviews. Recurring one-star themes are mapped back to the complaint register (Chapter 5.7 — Complaint resolution process) and treated as an operational problem, not a reputation problem.
Tools & Resources
Review monitoring is manual at a single site: Google Business Profile and each delivery platform's merchant dashboard checked daily, both of which push notifications for new reviews at no cost. A paid aggregation tool is only justified from roughly four sites, where checking eight to twelve dashboards daily stops being viable. That threshold is a Head Office decision, not a unit one. Response template library (starting points, not copy-paste). Monthly review summary template. Escalation log.
4.4 Promotions & loyalty
Approved promotion types
Where Imran’s Diner is today
Promotions are not run as a regular activity. The only promotions currently used are offers co-funded by delivery platforms.
Approved promotion types
Delivery-platform co-funded offers. Where the platform shares the cost of the offer.
Combo deals. Used to build trade in quiet periods (12pm–4pm, see Chapter 3.4 — Peak/off-peak staffing rules).
Student discount. 10% off, available 12pm to 4pm Monday to Thursday only, on collection and dine-in orders, on presentation of a valid student ID or recognised student discount card. Not available on delivery platform orders, where platform commission already takes a substantial share of the order value and a further discount can take the order below its contribution floor. Not available at peak (from 5pm) or at weekends: the purpose is to fill capacity that is already staffed and already paid for, not to discount demand the unit would have taken at full price. This is the distinction between a targeted quiet-period offer and the discounting barred under Not permitted below and under the no-discount pricing stance in Chapter 1.1.
To be explored
Seasonal specials. Not currently run. Where they are introduced, the windows with genuine demand for this brand and this trading pattern are Ramadan and Eid (strong late-evening and catering demand, aligned with the unit's existing late-night trading pattern), and the December party and office-catering season. A seasonal special is a product and a reason to visit, not a price cut — it is approved on the product specification and the margin it carries, not on the discount it offers. Head Office approval is required before any seasonal special runs.
Not permitted
Daily deal and voucher sites (e.g. Groupon); deep discounting; price wars; price matching or comparing prices with neighbouring businesses.
Rules
Head Office approval. Every promotion, including delivery-platform offers, needs Head Office approval before it runs.
Discount limits. No promotion may take any product below the minimum acceptable gross margin agreed in the pricing workshop (Chapter 6.2 — Margin targets by product), calculated for the channel the promotion runs on — delivery platform orders are assessed after platform commission, not before. Until the pricing workshop has set that minimum, no new promotion is approved beyond those already running. These limits must match the discount threshold that triggers a flag on the till (see Chapter 5.4 — Data governance & systems integration).
Loyalty programme mechanics
Where Imran’s Diner is today
A loyalty function supplied by the EPOS provider is in place but is not fit for purpose. There is no usable customer marketing base. Customer names and phone numbers are captured through caller ID on the tills, and email addresses are captured through the website only (see Chapter 5.4 — Data governance & systems integration).
Decision
Imran’s Diner will run a loyalty programme that captures customer details so customers can be marketed to directly, rewarded, and offered birthday meals. YSF Advisory will present options to choose from. Priority: the system is chosen before Site #2, because it feeds the Repeat Customer Rate KPI (Chapter 6.1) and is the main retention lever. The EPOS provider is Presto. The first action is a full feature audit of what Presto already provides before any new system is considered: Presto's published product set includes a digital loyalty and stamp system with a customer database, and branded online ordering with loyalty built in. Configuring a module already being paid for costs materially less than adding a second system alongside it, and a second system creates a second customer database that will diverge from the first. The mechanic will be visit-based (a stamp or equivalent) rather than points-based: a reward earned after a set number of full-price purchases, with a separate automated birthday reward. A visit-based reward costs a known amount — the food cost of one product, earned only after a defined number of full-price transactions — and can therefore be set before product-level margin data exists, whereas a points earn rate is a margin decision and cannot be set until the Chapter 6.2 margin targets are in place. The purpose of the scheme is the marketable customer base it builds, not the reward itself. Requirements for the chosen system
EPOS integration. Works with the EPOS so rewards are earned and redeemed at the till and in online orders without manual entry. The EPOS is Presto. Because Presto's published product set already includes a loyalty and stamp module, a customer database and a branded online ordering platform, the first question is not which loyalty system to buy but what the existing system already does and what it can do once configured. The same question applies to the margin reporting currently blocked in Chapter 6.2: Presto sells inventory and reporting modules, and the EPOS shows 100% margin because recipe costs have not been loaded, which is a configuration gap rather than a capability gap. Head Office is to complete a full Presto feature audit covering loyalty, customer database, online ordering, reporting, inventory, driver management and third-party integration capability, and only then decide whether to configure, upgrade or replace. If the Presto loyalty module cannot meet the requirements below, the choice becomes a second system alongside Presto — which creates a second customer database — or replacement, and that is a Head Office decision, not a unit one.
Repeat customer data. Records visits and spend by customer, so the Repeat Customer Rate can be reported by site (Chapter 6.1).
Marketing consent. Captures clear consent to email or SMS marketing at sign-up. Under the UK electronic marketing rules (PECR), marketing messages need consent unless the “soft opt-in” applies — details collected during a sale, marketing only for similar products, and a simple opt-out offered at sign-up and in every message. Customer contact and birthday details must be handled under UK GDPR, including a privacy notice at sign-up. Names and numbers captured through caller ID must not be used for marketing unless this consent is in place.
Birthday rewards. Supports automated birthday offers.
Data ownership. Customer data is owned by the franchisor, can be exported, and can be viewed across all sites. Franchisees must not run their own separate loyalty schemes.
