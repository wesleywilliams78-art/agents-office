1. Introduction
Creamy's service model is built around self-ordering kiosks as the primary ordering channel, with bar-side ordering as the secondary option for customers who prefer it. Regardless of how the customer orders, the in-person interaction — the greeting, the handover, the goodbye — is where the brand comes to life. These moments are short, but they're the ones customers remember.
This chapter sets out the minimum service standards and scripted touchpoints that every team member must deliver consistently. The scripts are not rigid word-for-word mandates — they're the baseline. Team members can personalise the delivery, but every element must be present every time.

2. Minimum Operating Standards
2.1 The Greeting
Every customer who enters the location must be acknowledged within seconds of walking through the door. The standard greeting is:
"Hi, welcome to Creamy."
Said with a smile, with eye contact. Not shouted across the room, not mumbled while looking at a screen. If the team member is mid-task, they still acknowledge — a smile and a "Hi, welcome" takes two seconds.
2.2 Self-Ordering Kiosks
Most customers will use the self-ordering kiosks. The team's role here is to be available, not to hover. If a customer looks uncertain or is hesitating at the kiosk, a team member should approach and offer help naturally — "Can I help you with anything?" — without making them feel they're doing it wrong.
2.3 Bar-Side Ordering
When a customer comes to the bar to order, the interaction follows this sequence:
Step 1 — Allergy Check Before taking the order, ask:
"Do you have any allergies we should know about?"
This is non-negotiable. It must happen before the customer starts choosing, not after. If the customer declares an allergy, the team member must know which menu items are affected — refer to the allergen matrix (Chapter 4.X — Allergen Management) and, if unsure, check with the Shift Leader before confirming the order. Never guess.
Step 2 — Take the Order Ask what they'd like. If the customer is unsure or browsing, guide them with a simple preference question:
"Would you like something really sweet, or something more with fresh fruits — a bit less sweet?"
This narrows the menu quickly without overwhelming the customer, and it shows the team member knows the product. It's a recommendation, not a sales pitch.
Step 3 — Upsell (Natural, Not Pushy) Once the main item is chosen, offer additions:
"Would you like a drink with that?"
And where relevant to the dessert:
"Would you like to add ice cream or cream?"
These are standard prompts, not hard sells. The tone is helpful — "would you like" — never pressured. If the customer says no, move on immediately.
Step 4 — Confirm & Process Repeat the order back to confirm, process payment, and let the customer know approximately how long it will take if there's a wait.
2.4 The Handover
When the order is ready and served to the customer — whether at the counter or brought to the table — the standard is:
"Here you go."
Said with a smile. Simple. Make eye contact. If it's a specific or elaborate dessert, it's fine to add a brief personal touch — "This one's really good, enjoy!" — but the minimum is always delivered.
2.5 The Goodbye
When a customer leaves, every team member within earshot should acknowledge them:
"Goodbye, have a nice day!"
This is the last impression. A customer who feels seen on the way out is more likely to come back than one who leaves unnoticed. It doesn't matter how busy the team is — the goodbye takes three seconds.
2.6 What the Standards Are Not
They are not a script to be recited robotically. The words above are the baseline — the team member's personality and warmth should come through.
They are not optional when it's busy. Peak hours are when service standards matter most, because that's when the most customers are forming their impression of the brand.
They are not only for new customers. Regulars get the same greeting, the same smile, the same goodbye. Familiarity is no excuse for dropping the standard.

Introduction

Level 5 — Head Office involvement: any complaint with potential brand-wide implications, any media enquiry about a complaint, any legal claim or threat of legal proceedings, any incident requiring public communication, and any regulatory enforcement action.

Equipment failure protocol (fridge/freezer/oven down)   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter defines what to do when a critical piece of equipment fails during trading hours or is discovered as failed at opening. Equipment failures in a food service environment are not just inconveniences — a fridge or freezer failure is a food safety emergency that can result in total product loss, a Mattilsynet enforcement action, or customer illness. A clear protocol ensures the team acts immediately to protect product, customers, and the business.
Minimum Operating Standards
Critical equipment is defined as any equipment whose failure directly affects food safety or the ability to trade: refrigeration (fridge, freezer, display chiller, waffle machine, crepe machine, fridge, freezer, milkshake blender, chocolate warmer, coffee machine.), cooking equipment (oven, hob, fryer, grill — as applicable to menu), and hot/cold holding units.
Refrigeration failure — immediate actions: (1) Do not open the unit door unless absolutely necessary. A closed fridge maintains safe temperature for approximately 4 hours; a closed chest freezer for approximately 24 hours (these are guidelines — actual times vary). (2) Check whether the failure is the unit itself or the power supply (tripped breaker, power cut). Reset breakers if safe to do so. (3) Begin temperature monitoring — record the internal temperature every 30 minutes using a calibrated probe thermometer. (4) If internal temperature rises above 5°C (fridge) or -15°C (freezer), begin emergency product transfer to alternative cold storage if available. (5) If no alternative storage is available and temperatures exceed safe limits (above 8°C for fridge, above -12°C for freezer), all affected perishable product must be disposed of. Record all waste on the waste log with 'equipment failure' as the reason.
Cooking equipment failure — immediate actions: (1) Assess whether a reduced menu can be offered using remaining working equipment. (2) If the failed equipment is essential to the core menu and no workaround exists, the Unit Manager must decide whether to continue trading with a reduced offering or to close temporarily. (3) Notify the Franchise Owner immediately if closure is being considered.
In all cases: (1) Contact the equipment maintenance provider or manufacturer's service line immediately. Record the call reference number. (2) Log the failure in the equipment maintenance log (see Chapter 5.6 — Equipment maintenance log) with: equipment name, time of failure, symptoms, actions taken, and service provider response. (3) If the failure affects food safety, complete a HACCP corrective action record (see Chapter 3.6).
Temporary closure: if the unit cannot safely trade due to equipment failure, the Unit Manager must (1) inform all scheduled staff, (2) post a notice at the entrance and update any active online ordering channels, (3) notify the Franchise Owner and Head Office, and (4) document the closure period and estimated reopening time.
Product loss: all product disposed of due to equipment failure must be recorded with quantities and estimated cost. This feeds into insurance claims (business interruption / stock loss) and the monthly waste report.
Post-repair: before returning the equipment to service, verify that it reaches the correct operating temperature and holds it for at least 1 hour before restocking with product. Record the verification in the equipment maintenance log.
Roles & Responsibilities
Shift Supervisor / Opening Lead: First responder — executes the immediate protocol, begins temperature monitoring, contacts the service provider.
Unit Manager: Decides on reduced menu or temporary closure; notifies the Franchise Owner and Head Office; manages the insurance/waste documentation.
Franchise Owner / Operator: Authorises expenditure for emergency repair or replacement; liaises with insurers; reviews the incident for prevention.
Head Office: Maintains the approved equipment service provider list; supports insurance claims; reviews failure patterns across the network.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of equipment failures per quarter by type, (b) response time from failure to service provider attendance, (c) product loss value per incident, (d) trading hours lost to equipment-related closure. Review equipment failure frequency during monthly business reviews — recurring failures on the same equipment may justify early replacement rather than continued repair.
Tools & Resources
Equipment maintenance log (see Chapter 5.6). HACCP corrective action record (see Chapter 3.6). Waste log (equipment failure entries). Emergency contact list — equipment service providers, with out-of-hours numbers. Temperature monitoring log (30-minute interval during failure). Insurance claim template / insurer contact details.

Systems outage procedure (EPOS/Tidsbanken/SmartMate down)   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter defines fallback procedures when one or more of Creamy's operational systems — EPOS, Tidsbanken, or SmartMate — become unavailable during trading. A systems outage does not mean the unit stops trading. It means the unit switches to manual processes until the system is restored. The key risk during an outage is not lost sales — it is lost data: transactions not recorded, hours not logged, and reconciliation gaps that take days to unpick. This chapter should be read alongside Chapter 5.4 (EPOS & systems use) for normal operating procedures.
Minimum Operating Standards
EPOS outage — fallback procedure: (1) Switch to manual order-taking using a pre-printed duplicate order pad (kept in back-of-house at all times). Each order slip must record: order number (sequential), items ordered, price, payment method (cash/card), and time. (2) If card payments are unavailable, switch to cash-only and display a notice at the counter and entrance. If a standalone card terminal is available as backup, use it — record transaction references on the manual order slip. (3) All manual order slips must be entered into the EPOS retrospectively once the system is restored. The Unit Manager is responsible for ensuring this is completed by end of the same trading day (or the following morning if the outage extends to close). (4) Reconcile: compare manual order slip total against the till cash count and any standalone card terminal records. Document any discrepancy.
Tidsbanken outage — fallback procedure: (1) Record all clock-in and clock-out times on a paper sign-in sheet (kept at the staff entrance). Each entry: employee name, date, clock-in time, clock-out time, break duration. (2) The Unit Manager must enter all manual time records into Tidsbanken once the system is restored, within  24 hours. (3) Any discrepancies between the paper record and the restored system log must be investigated and resolved before payroll is processed.
SmartMate outage — fallback procedure: The principle is the same: capture the data manually, on paper, re-enter it when the system is restored, and reconcile.
General outage rules: (1) Attempt a restart before escalating — close and reopen the application, restart the device, check the internet connection. (2) If the issue persists after restart, contact the system provider's support line. Record the support ticket number. (3) Notify the Unit Manager (if not on shift) and the Franchise Owner if the outage exceeds 1 hour. (4) Do not attempt any technical fixes beyond restart unless trained to do so — escalate to the system provider.
Manual-process supplies: each unit must maintain a minimum stock of pre-printed order pads, paper sign-in sheets, and a calculator in back-of-house at all times. These supplies must be checked monthly as part of the equipment/stock audit.
Post-outage: once the system is restored, the Unit Manager must (1) re-enter all manual records, (2) reconcile manual totals against system records, (3) document the outage in the equipment/systems log (date, time, duration, system affected, cause if known, data re-entered, discrepancies), and (4) report the outage to Head Office if it exceeded 1 hour or affected data integrity.
Roles & Responsibilities
Shift Supervisor: Executes the immediate fallback procedure; ensures manual records are kept accurately during the outage.
Unit Manager: Re-enters manual data once the system is restored; reconciles; reports to the Franchise Owner and Head Office.
Franchise Owner / Operator: Monitors outage frequency; escalates persistent issues to the system provider at a commercial level; authorises any workaround expenditure (e.g. standalone card terminal).
Head Office: Maintains relationships with system providers; monitors outage reports across the network; negotiates SLAs with providers.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number and duration of outages per system per quarter, (b) data re-entry accuracy (discrepancies found during reconciliation), (c) time from outage start to system restoration, (d) availability of manual-process supplies (checked monthly). Persistent outages on one system should trigger a formal review with the provider, including SLA compliance.
Tools & Resources
Pre-printed manual order pads (duplicate — one copy for kitchen, one retained). Paper sign-in sheets (Tidsbanken fallback). System provider support contact list (with ticket number log). Outage log (date, system, duration, cause, data reconciliation status). Standalone card terminal (backup — if available). See Chapter 5.4 for normal EPOS/Tidsbanken/SmartMate operating procedures.
