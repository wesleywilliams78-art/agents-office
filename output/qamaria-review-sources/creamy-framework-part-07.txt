Approved promotion types   Reviewed
1. Introduction
Promotions drive footfall, but the wrong promotion damages the brand. Creamy is a premium dessert bar — every promotional mechanic must reinforce that positioning, not undermine it. This chapter defines which promotion types are approved, which are explicitly prohibited, and the principle that governs all promotional decision-making: quality costs money, and Creamy will never compete on price.
A franchise partner does not need to invent their own promotional strategy. Head Office has tested what works and what doesn't across existing locations. This chapter captures that knowledge so every new location starts with proven tactics and avoids costly mistakes.

2. Minimum Operating Standards
2.1 Approved Promotion Types
The following mechanics are proven to drive revenue without eroding Creamy's brand positioning:
Combo Deals Bundling products at a combined price (e.g. a dessert plus a drink). Effective because they increase average transaction value while giving the customer a sense of added value — the brand isn't discounting a single product, it's encouraging a bigger order.
All combo pricing must maintain an acceptable margin. The franchise partner should calculate the combined cost of goods before setting the combo price and confirm with Head Office if unsure.
Combo deals must be presented as a curated experience ("The Creamy Combo"), not a discount.
Happy Hour A defined time window — typically during off-peak hours — with a promotional offer. Effective because it shifts demand into quieter periods without cheapening the peak experience.
Happy hour must not overlap with peak trading (18:00 onwards). The purpose is to build off-peak traffic, not discount the busiest period.
The offer can be a reduced price on selected items, a smaller free add-on, or a combo-style bundle. It should not be a blanket percentage off the menu.
Student Discounts A percentage or fixed-amount discount on presentation of a valid student ID. Effective because the 15–35 target demographic includes a large student population, and students who discover Creamy during their studies become long-term customers.
The discount rate should be set by Head Office to ensure consistency across locations. [Adnan to confirm: standard student discount rate.]
Valid only on presentation of a recognised student ID — not self-declared.
Buy One Get One Free (BOGOF) A tactical mechanic for specific campaigns — typically time-limited and tied to a launch, a seasonal push, or a social media activation. Effective when used sparingly to generate buzz and trial.
BOGOF must be pre-approved by Head Office on a campaign-by-campaign basis. It is not a standing offer.
The franchise partner must not run BOGOF independently without Head Office sign-off — overuse turns a tactical tool into a margin drain.
2.2 Prohibited Promotion Types
Groupon (and equivalent deal platforms) Explicitly does not work for Creamy. Deal platforms attract price-driven one-time visitors who rarely return at full price. They train customers to expect deep discounts, attract the wrong demographic, and damage perceived brand value. No franchise partner may list Creamy on Groupon or any equivalent discount deal platform.
Price Wars Creamy will never enter a price war with a competitor. If a nearby business undercuts on price, the response is not to match or beat — the response is to hold the line. The operating phrase across the network is: quality costs money. Any franchise partner who feels pressure to compete on price should contact Head Office for guidance rather than acting independently.
2.3 The Governing Principle
Every promotional decision passes through one filter: does this reinforce or erode Creamy's positioning as a premium dessert bar?
If a promotion makes the brand feel cheaper → don't do it.
If a promotion makes the customer feel they're getting something special → consider it.
If in doubt → ask Head Office. They can always guide the franchise partner on what to do and what to avoid. That guidance line exists precisely so franchise partners don't have to guess.

Opening Checklist
1. Introduction
This chapter provides the standard opening checklist for Creamy locations. A disciplined opening routine ensures the unit is clean, safe, fully stocked, and ready to serve before the first customer walks in. Every shift that starts poorly cascades through the day — missed prep, late temperature checks, and a rushed team set the tone for everything that follows.
The opening lead arrives alone (refer to Chapter 3.2 — Peak & Off-Peak Staffing for lone-opening time allowances) and must use that window to complete every item on this checklist methodically, without shortcuts.

2. Minimum Operating Standards
The opening team member (Shift Leader or Operations Manager) must arrive at least 30 minutes before the unit's published opening time.
The checklist must be completed in the following sequence:
Step 1 — Secure Entry Check the exterior for any signs of break-in, damage, or anything unusual before entering. If anything is suspicious, do not enter — contact the Operations Manager / franchise partner immediately and wait for guidance.
Step 2 — Alarm & Clock-In Deactivate the alarm system. Change into uniform, then log in to clock yourself in for the shift.
Step 3 — Lights & HVAC Turn on all lighting, heating/cooling, and ventilation/extraction systems. Allow extraction to run before firing up any cooking equipment.
Step 4 — Temperature Checks Record fridge and freezer temperatures on the daily log:
Fridges: 0–4°C
Freezers: −18°C or below
If any reading is outside the critical limit, report immediately to the Operations Manager and follow the HACCP corrective action procedure (refer to Chapter 4.X — HACCP & Food Safety). Do not use product from a unit that has breached temperature limits until the corrective action has been completed and documented.
Step 5 — Equipment Power-On Switch on all equipment in the following order:
EPOS system
Coffee machine
Bain-marie — fill with water before switching on (running a bain-marie dry will damage the unit)
Waffle machines
Crepe machines
Blenders
Display fridges / ice cream display
Any other equipment per the location's specific equipment list
Allow equipment to reach operating temperature before use.
Step 6 — Visual Cleanliness Check Walk through the entire front of house: customer seating, counter, menu boards, windows, entrance, and any outdoor seating. Everything must be clean and presentable before the doors open. If the closing team has left anything below standard, address it now and log it for the Operations Manager.
Step 7 — Restock Check and replenish all front-of-house and kitchen supplies: napkins, cups, lids, straws, retail display items, and any consumables that run low during service. The aim is zero interruptions during the first trading period.
Step 8 — Prep Complete the following daily prep tasks:
Melt chocolate in the bain-marie
Make the waffle mix and crepe mix
Cut strawberries for the day's use
Roll cutlery (fork and knife) in napkins
Any additional prep required for the day's menu or expected volume should follow the location's prep schedule. If prep cannot be completed before opening, prioritise the items needed for the first hour of trading and continue prep alongside early service.
Step 9 — Cash Float Verify the till float matches the expected amount. Count it, log the count on the daily cash sheet, and sign. If the float is short or over, document the discrepancy and notify the Operations Manager before opening. Do not adjust the float without authorisation.
Step 10 — Staff Check Confirm all scheduled team members have arrived and are in correct uniform. If anyone is missing or late, contact them and notify the Operations Manager if the shift is at risk of falling below minimum staffing levels (refer to Chapter 3.2).
Step 11 — Final Walk-Through The opening lead walks the entire unit, front to back, one final time. This is the quality gate — if anything from Steps 1–10 has been missed or isn't right, this is the last chance to catch it before customers arrive.
Step 12 — Open Doors Open at the published time. Not early, not late.

Sanitise all preparation surfaces

Sweep and mop the kitchen floor
Empty the bain-marie water — do not leave water sitting overnight

Secure dry stores. Any stock that has been left out at ambient temperature beyond safe limits must be discarded and logged (refer to Chapter 4.X — HACCP & Food Safety).

Reconcile the count to the EPOS end-of-day figures

Prepare banking if applicable
Secure all cash in the safe

Cash handling & till reconciliation   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter defines Creamy's cash handling and till reconciliation procedures. Rigorous cash handling protects the business from theft, fraud, and accounting errors, and ensures accurate financial reporting. Norway is increasingly cashless, but many QSR operations still handle meaningful volumes of cash. Note: Norwegian tax law (bokforingsloven and kassasystemforskriften) requires that EPOS/cash register systems meet specific technical requirements and that daily sales data is retained.
Minimum Operating Standards
The till float (vekslepenger) must be a fixed, agreed amount: NOK 2,000]. The float must be counted and verified at the start of every trading day (opening checklist) and at the end of every trading day (closing checklist).
All sales must be rung through the EPOS system. No sales may be conducted off-register. Under kassasystemforskriften, every sale must produce a customer receipt (kvittering).
Voids and refunds on the EPOS must be authorised by the Shift Supervisor or Unit Manager and logged with a reason. Excessive voids or refunds must be investigated.
Cash drops: if the till accumulates cash above a threshold of [Adnan to confirm: e.g. NOK 5,000], excess cash must be removed and placed in the safe (a 'cash drop'). Cash drops must be recorded on the daily cash sheet.
End-of-day reconciliation: count the physical cash in the till, subtract the opening float, and compare the result to the EPOS cash sales total. Any variance (over or short) must be recorded. Variances above NOK 50] must be investigated and reported to the Franchisee.
Banking: cash must be banked at least twice per week. Banking should be varied in timing and route for security. Two people should handle banking where possible.
No personal money (IOUs, personal change) may be taken from or added to the till. No staff purchases may be processed outside normal EPOS procedures.
Card payment reconciliation: card terminal settlement reports must be reconciled against EPOS card sales daily.
All cash handling records (daily cash sheets, banking records, variance logs) must be retained for 5 years for accounting documentation.
Roles & Responsibilities
Opening Lead: Counts and verifies the opening float.
Shift Supervisor: Authorises voids and refunds; conducts cash drops; monitors till activity.
Closing Lead: Conducts end-of-day reconciliation; secures cash; completes daily cash sheet.
Unit Manager / Franchisee: Reviews daily cash sheets; investigates variances; arranges banking.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) daily cash variance (over/short), (b) number and value of voids/refunds, (c) banking frequency vs. target. Persistent variances or unusual void patterns must trigger an investigation. Review weekly.
Tools & Resources
Daily cash sheet (template). EPOS end-of-day cash report. Void/refund log. Banking log. Safe log (recording cash in/out of the safe).

Stock rotation (FIFO)   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter defines Creamy's stock rotation procedures based on the FIFO (First In, First Out) principle. Proper stock rotation is a fundamental food safety requirement, minimises waste, and ensures customers always receive product made with the freshest ingredients. FIFO compliance is a standard Mattilsynet inspection point.
Minimum Operating Standards
All stock must be rotated on a FIFO basis: the oldest stock (earliest delivery date or earliest production/prep date) must always be used first.
When new deliveries are received, existing stock must be moved to the front/top and new stock placed at the back/bottom of shelves and storage areas.
All items in cold storage, dry storage, and prep areas must be clearly date-labelled. For items transferred from original packaging, the label must include: product name, date of transfer/opening, and use-by date.
Use-by dates (holdbarhetsdato) must never be extended or altered. 'Best before' (best for) dates are advisory but Creamy policy is to follow them unless Head Office guidance states otherwise for specific products.
Daily checks: as part of the opening procedure, a designated team member must check all cold and dry storage areas for items past their use-by date. Expired items must be removed, logged on the waste sheet, and disposed of.
Deliveries: upon goods receiving, check that delivered items have sufficient remaining shelf life for expected usage. Reject any delivery where items are damaged, at incorrect temperature (chilled goods above 4°C, frozen goods above -18°C), or have insufficient shelf life [Adnan to confirm: minimum acceptable remaining shelf life, e.g. at least 3 days for fresh items].
Prep items (anything made in-house): must be labelled with the prep date and a use-within period defined by the HACCP plan [Adnan to confirm: standard use-within periods for Creamy prep items]. Prep items past their use-within date must be discarded.
Roles & Responsibilities
All Team Members: Apply FIFO when accessing and restocking products; date-label items correctly; report any stock concerns.
Shift Supervisor: Conducts daily date checks; manages deliveries and goods receiving; ensures FIFO compliance during the shift.
Unit Manager: Monitors waste logs; investigates FIFO failures; includes FIFO compliance in training and performance management.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) waste due to expired stock (weight or cost per week), (b) FIFO compliance observed during audits (spot-check: are oldest items at the front?), (c) delivery rejections due to short shelf life or temperature issues. Excessive waste may indicate over-ordering or poor rotation discipline.
Tools & Resources
Date labels and label gun (or adhesive labels). Waste log/waste sheet. Delivery goods-receiving checklist. Temperature probe (for delivery checks).

Waste tracking & reporting   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter defines Creamy's waste tracking and reporting procedures. Food waste is a direct cost to the business, an environmental concern, and a potential indicator of operational issues (over-ordering, poor stock rotation, inaccurate prep planning). Tracking waste systematically enables data-driven decisions to reduce it.
Minimum Operating Standards
All waste must be recorded on the daily waste log at the point of disposal. The log must capture: date, item, quantity (weight or units), reason for waste (expired, over-production, dropped/damaged, customer return, quality issue), and the team member recording it.
Waste categories: (1) Date-related waste (expired or past use-within), (2) Prep waste (over-production, trimmings, prep errors), (3) Service waste (customer returns, incorrect orders), (4) Quality waste (items not meeting standard on inspection), (5) Damaged/spillage.
Waste cost must be calculated and reported weekly, expressed as a percentage of food purchases (cost of waste / cost of food purchased). Target waste percentage: [Adnan to confirm: e.g. below 3-5% of food cost].
Weekly waste review: the Unit Manager must review the waste log weekly to identify patterns and take corrective action. Common patterns include: specific products consistently wasted (adjust order quantities or prep volumes), waste concentrated at specific times (adjust prep schedules), and waste concentrated around specific team members (training issue).
