Sign-off & approval process
Introduction
This chapter defines the formal sign-off and approval process for new Imran’s Diner site openings. No new unit may proceed to fit-out, lease signing, or trading without completing the approval stages set out here. This process protects both the franchisee and the brand by ensuring every new site meets Imran’s Diner's commercial, operational, and regulatory requirements before capital is committed.
Minimum Operating Standards
The approval process has four stages: (1) Site Evaluation — completed checklist submitted to Head Office, (2) Commercial Review — Head Office assesses the site against the five-gate site decision rule (Chapter 2.1 — Demographic & footfall requirements), including the occupancy cost test, projected revenue and fit-out costs, (3) Conditional Approval — Head Office issues conditional approval subject to lease terms, planning, and licensing, (4) Final Approval — issued once the lease is agreed, licences are confirmed, and the fit-out plan is signed off.
Each stage must be formally documented. Head Office must issue a written decision (approval, conditional approval, or rejection with reasons) within 10 working days of receiving a complete submission.
No fit-out works may commence before Final Approval is issued.
The franchisee must not enter into any binding lease, contractor, or supplier commitment before receiving at least Conditional Approval from Head Office.
If the franchisee proceeds without approval, this constitutes a breach of the franchise agreement and may result in termination of the agreement.
All approval documentation must be retained by both Head Office and the franchisee for the duration of the franchise agreement plus 6 years, matching the contract limitation period in England and Wales.

Legal Advisor: Reviews lease and franchise agreement implications.

Front-of-house layout standards
Design intent
The front of house at Imran’s Diner is designed on purpose, using customer psychology most takeaways ignore. It is not a generic QSR layout with the Imran’s Diner name on it. Every unit applies the six design principles below. A shopfitter or store designer translates them into a layout for each site, and Head Office approves that layout before fit-out starts (Chapter 2.1 — Sign-off & approval process).
The six design principles (mandatory)
1. A landing zone between door and counter. Customers get a moment to arrive before they have to decide. The counter is not placed directly inside the door. The minimum clear distance from the entrance door to the front of the counter is 2 metres, measured along the customer's natural path, with a queue area that does not block the door when three or more customers are waiting. Head Office confirms the landing zone against the measured Sheffield unit during the menu engineering walkaround; where a site cannot achieve it, the layout requires written Head Office approval before fit-out. 
2. Staff face the door. The counter is positioned so staff face the entrance and acknowledge every customer within seconds of arrival. Being seen changes how long a wait feels more than the actual wait time does.
3. Order and collection are separate, with a visible queue system. Customers order at one point and collect at another, and can see where their order is. Uncertain or seemingly unfair waits feel much longer than ones people can see clearly. Separate order and collection points are mandatory in every unit from fit-out. Order numbers are issued at the point of order and called at collection. An order-status screen is the network standard and is specified by Head Office as part of the EPOS rollout; until it is in place, units operate the number system manually, and no unit installs its own screen or queue system without Head Office approval.
4. A menu board that is easy to decide from. The menu board shows a few clear groups, not a wall of options, so decisions at the counter are faster.
5. Food visible from the door. Food preparation or hot display is visible from the entrance, so the food sells on sight and smell before anyone reads the menu.
6. Lighting zoned for pace and comfort. Brighter lighting at the counter keeps service moving; warmer lighting in any seating area makes it feel like a diner, not a processing station. The counter and order zone are lit to 500 lux at a colour temperature of 4000K, which reads as bright and clean without feeling clinical. Any seating area is lit to 200-300 lux at 2700-3000K, which reads as warm. Kitchen and prep areas follow food-safety lighting requirements, not these standards. All lighting is dimmable where practical, and the full specification is issued by Head Office to the shopfitter as part of the fit-out pack.
Where Sheffield is today
The Sheffield unit was built before these principles were written, and does not yet meet all six. Head Office records which principles the unit currently meets and which are outstanding, and holds a dated plan to bring it to the full standard. New units are built to all six from opening. The standard applies to Head Office's own unit as it does to every franchisee's.

2.3 Pre-opening checklist
Regulatory/licensing requirements (UK-specific)
Introduction
This chapter sets out the regulatory and licensing requirements that must be satisfied before a new Imran’s Diner unit can legally commence trading in the UK. The UK has specific requirements for food businesses covering food safety registration, planning/use class, and (for late-opening units) licensing. Failure to obtain the correct registrations and permissions before opening is an offence and will result in enforcement action from the local authority (Environmental Health) or the Food Standards Agency.
Minimum Operating Standards
Food business registration: Every food business in the UK must be registered with the local authority (Environmental Health) before commencing operations, via the free online form at register.food.gov.uk. Registration must be submitted at least 28 days before the planned opening date (the statutory minimum notice period). This is a registration, not an approval — the council does not issue a 'permit' but will inspect and rate the premises under the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme.
Food safety management system: A documented, HACCP-based food safety management system must be established before opening. This is a legal requirement under Regulation (EC) 852/2004 (retained in UK law), most commonly met using the Food Standards Agency's 'Safer Food Better Business' (SFBB) pack. The system must cover HACCP principles, allergen management, cleaning routines, temperature control, traceability, and staff training. See Chapter 3.6 for detailed HACCP procedures.
Late Night Refreshment authorisation: Required under the Licensing Act 2003 only if the unit will supply hot food or hot drink to the public between 11pm and 5am. Applied for from the relevant local authority licensing team, either as a standalone authorisation or combined with a premises licence. Not required for units trading solely within standard daytime/evening hours.
Food hygiene training: There is no UK statutory exam gating trading (unlike some other jurisdictions), but the person responsible for daily operations, and all food handlers, must be 'trained, instructed, or supervised' to a level commensurate with their role (Regulation (EC) 852/2004, Annex II). Company policy: the Unit Manager (currently Owner) must hold Level 3 Food Safety and Hygiene for Catering, and every food handler must hold Level 2 (or equivalent), completed before unsupervised duties begin — see Staffing & training lead time chapter.
Business registration: The franchise entity must be registered with Companies House (if trading as a limited company) or registered for Self Assessment with HMRC (if a sole trader/partnership), and must register for VAT with HMRC once taxable turnover exceeds the registration threshold (currently £90,000).
Health & Safety Executive (HSE): If employing staff, the employer must comply with the general duties under the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974, register as an employer with HMRC for PAYE, and hold Employers' Liability insurance (a legal requirement — see below). No separate HSE licence is needed for a standard QSR takeaway, but a written health and safety policy is mandatory once the business has 5 or more employees, and RIDDOR-reportable incidents must be reported to the HSE (see H&S incident reporting chapter).
Fire safety: A fire risk assessment must be completed and documented before opening, and the unit must comply with the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005. Contact the local fire and rescue authority if a pre-opening inspection is required.
Insurance: Appropriate business insurance must be in place before trading, covering at a minimum: public liability, Employers' Liability (a legal requirement in the UK under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969, minimum £5 million cover), property/contents, and business interruption.
Roles & Responsibilities
Franchisee: Responsible for obtaining all registrations and permissions before opening; ensuring the Unit Manager holds the required food hygiene qualification; establishing the food safety management system.
Head Office: Provides guidance and templates (e.g. SFBB/HACCP plan template); verifies that all requirements are met before issuing Final Approval.
Legal / Compliance Advisor: Supports with applications and regulatory interpretation where needed.
Unit Manager: Must hold Level 3 Food Safety and Hygiene for Catering; is the named food safety lead for the unit.
Measure Analyse Improve

Maintain a licence register for each unit listing: registration/permission type, date obtained, renewal date (if applicable), and responsible person. Flag: food business registration confirmation, Late Night Refreshment authorisation (if applicable), food hygiene certificates, SFBB/HACCP documentation, fire risk assessment, and insurance certificates must all be on file before opening day.

Soft-launch protocol
Introduction
This chapter defines the soft-launch protocol for new Imran’s Diner units. A soft launch is a controlled, limited opening designed to stress-test operations, identify issues, and build team confidence before the full public launch. Every new Imran’s Diner unit must complete a soft-launch period before proceeding to grand opening.
Minimum Operating Standards
The soft-launch period must be a minimum of 3 trading days before the grand opening. The soft launch must test the unit at real trading pace, not simply open the doors: live ticket times against target, kitchen-to-pass handover, allergen handling, and till and card operation. The Unit Manager must debrief the soft launch with Head Office before the grand opening, and any issue identified must be closed out or carry an agreed action with a named owner and date.
During soft launch, the unit operates with reduced hours and/or a limited menu to control volume and allow the team to focus on execution quality.
Soft-launch invitations should be limited to: friends and family of the franchisee and team, local business neighbours, and a controlled number of walk-ins. No public marketing or social media promotion should occur during this period.
A daily soft-launch debrief must be conducted at the end of each day, covering: what went well, what went wrong, customer feedback, equipment or system issues, and staffing observations.
Critical issues identified during soft launch must be resolved before grand opening. If issues cannot be resolved in time, the grand opening date must be postponed — Head Office must approve any postponement.
Head Office (or a designated representative) should be present for at least one day of the soft launch to observe and provide feedback.
At the end of the soft-launch period, the Franchisee and Unit Manager (currently Owner) must complete a 'Launch Readiness Declaration' confirming that all identified issues have been addressed and the unit is ready for full public trading.
Roles & Responsibilities
Franchisee: Organises the soft-launch guest list; participates in daily debriefs; signs the Launch Readiness Declaration.
Unit Manager: Runs day-to-day soft-launch operations; leads debriefs; documents issues and resolutions.
Team Members: Operate as they would during normal trading; flag issues in real time.
Head Office: Observes, provides feedback, and co-signs the Launch Readiness Declaration.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of issues identified during soft launch and percentage resolved before grand opening, (b) customer feedback scores (even informal), (c) average ticket time and order accuracy during soft launch vs. targets. Use soft-launch data to set realistic opening-week targets.
Tools & Resources
Soft-launch debrief template (daily log). Launch Readiness Declaration form. Issue tracker (simple spreadsheet: issue, owner, status, resolution date).

A newly incorporated franchisee company has no trading history, so new units should expect to trade on pro-forma or cash-on-delivery terms until a payment history is established. All ingredient and packaging orders must be placed with suppliers on the Imran’s Diner approved supplier list (see Chapter 2.4 — Approved supplier list). Franchisees must not source from unapproved suppliers without written Head Office approval.
The first order (opening stock) should be calculated based on the projected first two weeks of trading volume, with a 20% buffer to account for demand uncertainty.
Each supplier account must be set up with: the correct legal entity name, delivery address, billing address, agreed payment terms, and a nominated order contact at the unit.
Delivery schedules must be confirmed with each supplier and aligned with the unit's storage capacity and operating hours.
A master order list (all SKUs, supplier, unit cost, minimum order quantity, lead time) must be maintained and kept current. Head Office provides the template; the franchisee maintains it locally.
Payment terms: Franchisees must not agree to terms outside the standard without Head Office approval. Payment terms must be followed in line with supplier terms set out per supplier.
Roles & Responsibilities
Franchisee: Sets up supplier accounts; places initial orders; maintains the local master order list.
Unit Manager (currently Owner): Manages day-to-day ordering once the unit is operational; monitors stock levels against par levels (see Chapter 5.3).
Head Office: Maintains the approved supplier list; negotiates central pricing as the supply base develops; provides the master order list template.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) all supplier accounts confirmed and active before the target date, (b) first delivery received on schedule, (c) any stock-outs during the first two weeks of trading. Review supplier performance (delivery reliability, quality, pricing) quarterly.
Tools & Resources
Master order list template (Head Office issued). Supplier account setup checklist. Delivery schedule planner.

Grand opening protocol
Introduction
This chapter defines the protocol for the grand opening of a new Imran’s Diner unit. The grand opening is the first full public trading day and is a significant marketing and operational event. It must be executed with precision — the team has one chance to make a first impression on the local market.
Minimum Operating Standards
The grand opening may only proceed after the soft-launch period is complete and the Launch Readiness Declaration has been signed by the Franchisee, Unit Manager (currently Owner), and Head Office (see Soft-launch protocol chapter).
The grand opening date must be confirmed with Head Office at least 4 weeks in advance, so that pre-opening awareness activity can run ahead of the date. Detailed marketing coordination with Head Office takes place in the final 2 weeks before opening.
Staffing across the grand opening weekend must be at 150% of the level the labour model sets for normal trading, to handle expected higher footfall. This cover comes from the unit's own team, not from other units: the opening team is recruited to 120% of the establishment the labour model requires, which provides the headroom to roster up for the opening weekend and absorbs early leavers. The 150% level is a short burst for the opening weekend only and is not a sustainable standing rota.
A grand opening marketing plan must be agreed with Head Office covering: social media announcements, local press outreach, any opening-day promotions or giveaways, and signage/banners. [Tawheed to confirm: grand opening marketing budget, to be set when Chapter 2.5 (Local launch plan) is written. It forms part of the franchisee's opening cash requirement, so it cannot be left open indefinitely.]
The unit must be fully stocked with at least 3 days' worth of inventory before grand opening day to avoid stock-outs during peak demand.
Operating hours on grand opening day: Normal operating hours, where different this should be agreed with Head Office in advance.
A designated Head Office representative should be present on grand opening day.
A post-opening review meeting must be held within 5 days of the grand opening covering: sales performance vs. projection, customer feedback, operational issues, and lessons learned.
Roles & Responsibilities
Franchisee: Owns the grand opening event; ensures readiness across staffing, stock, and marketing; hosts any VIP/press attendees.
Unit Manager: Manages operations on the day; ensures team is briefed and positioned; handles real-time issues.
Head Office: Provides marketing support; sends a representative; participates in the post-opening review.
Team Members: Execute at the highest standard; every team member must be briefed on grand opening expectations before the day.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) grand opening day sales vs. projection, (b) customer complaints or issues on the day, (c) social media reach and engagement from opening-day content, (d) staff feedback on preparedness. Use grand opening data to refine the protocol for future openings.
Tools & Resources
Grand opening checklist (day-before and day-of). Marketing plan template (coordinated with Head Office). Post-opening review template. Social media content calendar for launch week.

3. People
3.1 Recruitment & onboarding
Job descriptions by role
Introduction
This chapter provides standardised job description templates for core roles within an Imran’s Diner unit. Clear job descriptions support the written statement of employment particulars required under the Employment Rights Act 1996 (which must specify the employee's job title or a brief description of the work), and they are essential for setting expectations, supporting performance management, and ensuring consistency across franchise locations.
Minimum Operating Standards
Every role within a Imran’s Diner unit must have a written job description approved by Head Office. Job descriptions must be issued to employees as part of their employment contract or as an attached schedule.
Core roles (minimum): Unit Manager (currently Owner), Shift Supervisor (currently Team Leader), Team Member (front of house/counter), Team Member (kitchen/prep).
Each job description must include: job title, reporting line, key responsibilities (no more than 10), required qualifications or certifications, working hours/pattern, and physical requirements.
Job descriptions must comply with the Equality Act 2010 — they must not include requirements that directly or indirectly discriminate on a protected characteristic.
Job descriptions must be reviewed and updated at least annually, or whenever a role changes materially.
All job descriptions must be available in English. Translated versions may be maintained in parallel where relevant for non-English-speaking staff, but the English version is the reference document.
Roles & Responsibilities
Head Office: Develops and maintains master job description templates; approves role changes.
Franchisee: Ensures all staff have received their job description; adapts templates for local needs within approved parameters.
Unit Manager: Uses job descriptions for recruitment, onboarding, and performance conversations.
Measure Analyse Improve
Audit annually that every employee has a current, signed job description on file. Track any role-related disputes or grievances that may indicate unclear job descriptions. Review job descriptions as part of the annual manual review cycle.
Tools & Resources
Job description templates (by role, issued by Head Office). Employment contract template (see Chapter 3.5). Role matrix (summary table showing all roles, reporting lines, and key differentiators).
Recruitment process & channels
Introduction
This chapter defines the standard recruitment process and approved channels for hiring staff into Imran’s Diner units. A consistent recruitment process ensures the brand attracts and selects team members who align with Imran’s Diner's values and service standards, while complying with UK employment law.
Minimum Operating Standards
All vacancies must be advertised using approved channels. Recommended channels for the UK market include: Indeed, the DWP's Find a Job service (free job postings via gov.uk/find-a-job), social media (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok), local community boards, and word-of-mouth/employee referrals.
Job advertisements must comply with the Equality Act 2010 — no discriminatory language or requirements. Advertisements must state the role title, location, approximate hours, and application method.
The recruitment process follows six stages: (1) Advertise vacancy, (2) Screen applications (CV and cover letter), (3) Interview (at least one structured interview using standardised questions), (4) Reference check (minimum one reference from a previous employer), (5) Right to work check, completed and evidenced before the start date, (6) Offer and contract.
Interviews must be conducted using a standardised question set to ensure fairness and consistency. Head Office provides interview guides by role.
Under UK law, employers should not ask about pregnancy, family planning, religion, political views, or trade union membership during interviews, as doing so risks a discrimination claim under the Equality Act 2010.
All applicants must receive a response (offer or rejection) within 10 working days of their interview. Rejected candidates should receive a respectful, professional notification.
