Creamy operates a lean three-tier structure designed for a single-unit or small multi-unit dessert bar environment. 2.1 Operations Manager
The Operations Manager is the senior on-site leader responsible for the overall functioning of the location. Their authority covers any decision necessary to maintain day-to-day operations — they act, then inform. For longer-term or structural decisions (new processes, staffing changes, supplier issues, capital expenditure), they consult with the franchise owner / Head Office before implementation.
Core responsibilities:
Ensuring all operational systems (EPOS, scheduling, delivery platforms) are functioning correctly, and contacting the relevant provider directly if they are not
Ensuring all equipment and machinery is in working order — identifying faults, arranging repairs, and escalating to Head Office where replacement or capital spend is required
Conducting weekly checks that all operational routines (cleaning schedules, stock rotation, opening/closing procedures) are being followed consistently
Presenting and explaining any new menu items, processes, or operational changes to the team — the Operations Manager is the bridge between Head Office decisions and frontline execution
Overseeing shift leaders and holding them accountable for shift-level performance
Decision authority: Full autonomy on immediate operational decisions (e.g. remaking a customer order, adjusting a shift rota for the day, resolving an equipment issue). Escalates to franchise owner / Head Office on matters affecting long-term cost, staffing structure, supplier relationships, or brand standards.
2.2 Shift Leader
The Shift Leader is responsible for execution during their shift. Their job is to ensure everything runs according to plan — the standards are set by the Operations Manager; the Shift Leader enforces them in real time.
Core responsibilities:
Making sure every team member on shift is following established rules, routines, and service standards
Reporting everything of note to the Operations Manager — issues, incidents, customer complaints, stock shortages, staff performance concerns
Seeking guidance from the Operations Manager when something goes wrong or falls outside normal operating procedure — the Shift Leader does not improvise solutions to problems they haven't been trained to handle
Decision authority: Autonomy within the boundaries of the shift plan (e.g. managing break rotations, handling straightforward customer requests, directing team members between tasks). Escalates to the Operations Manager for anything unplanned, unusual, or potentially costly.
2.3 Team Members
Team Members are the frontline of service and production. The standard is simple: if it's in the plan, execute it; if it's not, ask before acting.
Core responsibilities:
Executing assigned tasks to the standard demonstrated during training — food preparation, service, cleaning, restocking
Following all hygiene, safety, and brand standards without exception
Reporting and asking before taking any action that deviates from the established routine — team members do not make independent operational decisions
Decision authority: None beyond executing trained tasks. All exceptions, deviations, or uncertainties are referred to the Shift Leader on duty.
