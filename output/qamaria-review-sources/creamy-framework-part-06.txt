Introduction

Minimum Operating Standards
Disciplinary process — stages: (1) Informal conversation (minor first-time issues — documented in a file note), (2) Formal verbal warning (documented, employee informed it is a warning), (3) Written warning (formal letter setting out the issue, expected improvement, timeline, and consequences of failure to improve), (4) Final written warning, (5) Dismissal (oppsigelse, §15-7 — requires saklig grunn, i.e. justifiable cause) or summary dismissal (avskjed, §15-14 — only for gross misconduct).

Dismissal notices must be in writing, delivered personally or by registered post, and must inform the employee of their right to request negotiation (forhandlinger, §17-3) and their right to take legal action (§17-4). The statutory notice period is 1 month during the first 5 years of employment (§15-3), though the contract may specify a longer period.
Grievance process: any employee may raise a grievance about their working conditions, treatment, or any other workplace concern. Grievances should be raised with the Unit Manager in the first instance; if the grievance concerns the Unit Manager, it should be raised with the Franchisee. The grievance must be acknowledged within 3 working days and a response provided within 10 working days. Right
All disciplinary and grievance records must be retained confidentially in the employee's personnel file. Access is restricted to the Franchisee and, where relevant, Head Office HR support.
Legal note: Norwegian employees have strong statutory protections. If in any doubt about the lawfulness of a proposed dismissal, seek legal advice before proceeding. The cost of an unfair dismissal finding (usaklig oppsigelse) can be substantial, including reinstatement and compensation.

2. Minimum Operating Standards
2.1 The Five Pillars
Happy Creamy exists to make people's day better. The tone is upbeat, warm, and genuinely positive — never forced, never sarcastic, never passive-aggressive. Customers chose to treat themselves; the brand's voice should reflect that this is a good moment.
Sounds like: "We're so glad you came in!" · "Can't wait for you to try this one."
Does not sound like: Over-the-top enthusiasm that feels fake · Emoji overload · Ignoring a complaint with relentless cheerfulness
Modern Creamy speaks like a brand that belongs in 2024, not 2004. The language is current, clean, and natural — the way you'd talk to a friend, not the way a corporate handbook reads. Visual identity, social content, and in-store communication should all feel fresh and relevant.
Sounds like: Direct, conversational language · Short sentences · Current references where appropriate
Does not sound like: Slang that dates quickly · Trying too hard to be trendy · Corporate stiffness ("We at Creamy would like to inform valued customers that…")
Premium Creamy is a quality-first brand. The tone reflects that — it's confident about the product without being arrogant, and it never undersells. The brand doesn't compete on price; it doesn't talk like it does either. No "cheap," no "bargain," no apologetic discounting language.
Sounds like: "Made with real Belgian chocolate." · "We don't cut corners on ingredients."
Does not sound like: Luxury-brand pretension · Exclusionary language · Price-led messaging ("Only 99kr!")
Welcoming Everyone is welcome at Creamy — families, students, couples, groups of friends, solo visitors. The tone is inclusive, approachable, and never intimidating. A first-time customer should feel as comfortable as a regular.
Sounds like: "Come as you are." · "There's always room for one more."
Does not sound like: Exclusive or cliquey · Assuming the customer already knows the menu · Language that makes anyone feel like they don't belong
Confident Creamy knows what it is and doesn't apologise for it. The brand leads its category in Norway — the tone should reflect that quiet confidence. State what the brand does well without hedging, but never cross into arrogance or putting competitors down.
Sounds like: "Norway's dessert bar." · "We set the standard."
Does not sound like: Naming or disparaging competitors · Boastful claims that can't be backed up · Defensive or reactive language
2.2 Applying the Pillars
The five pillars work together — no single pillar should dominate at the expense of the others. A post that's happy but not premium feels cheap. A response that's confident but not welcoming feels cold. The skill is in blending all five into a single, recognisable voice.
Channel | Priority Emphasis | Watch Out For
Social media posts | Happy, Modern | Don't sacrifice Premium for trend-chasing
Customer complaint responses | Welcoming, Confident | Don't let Happy override genuine acknowledgment of the issue
Menu descriptions / in-store signage | Premium, Modern | Don't over-describe — keep it clean and appetising
Recruitment posts | Welcoming, Happy | Don't lose Confident — Creamy is a brand people should want to work for
Review responses (positive) | Happy, Welcoming | Keep it brief and genuine — don't copy-paste the same reply
Review responses (negative) | Welcoming, Confident | Acknowledge, resolve, move offline. Never defensive, never dismissive
2.3 What Creamy Never Sounds Like
Apologetic about pricing
Aggressive or confrontational
Corporate or bureaucratic
Desperate for attention
Dismissive of feedback
Imitative of another brand's voice

Introduction
This chapter provides guidance on community engagement for Creamy units. Building genuine relationships within the local community drives long-term loyalty, positive word-of-mouth, and brand affinity. Community engagement is not just marketing — it is how Creamy embeds itself as a valued part of the neighbourhood.
Minimum Operating Standards
Each unit should identify and engage with at least 3-5 local community organisations, groups, or institutions per year. Examples include: schools, sports clubs, religious or cultural organisations, local charities, and business associations (næringsforening). Right
Approved engagement activities include: sponsorship of local events or teams (within approved budget), hosting community events at the unit (e.g. charity evenings, school fundraisers), offering venue space for local group meetings (where space permits), providing product donations for local fundraising events, and participating in local business networks or events.
All community engagement must be consistent with Creamy's brand values. The unit should not engage with organisations or causes that are politically divisive or that conflict with the brand's positioning.
Community engagement activities should be documented and shared with Head Office for inclusion in any central communications or social media content.
Budget: community engagement spend should come from the LSM budget (see Local store marketing playbook chapter). Significant sponsorship commitments (above NOK 5,000) require Head Office approval. Right
Roles & Responsibilities
Franchisee / Unit Manager: Identifies community engagement opportunities; manages relationships; represents Creamy at local events.
Team Members: Participate in community events as brand ambassadors when asked.
Head Office: Provides guidance on brand-appropriate engagements; amplifies local stories through central channels.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of community engagements per quarter, (b) estimated reach/impact of each engagement, (c) any resulting media coverage or social media mentions. Community engagement is difficult to tie directly to sales, so measure it through brand awareness indicators and customer feedback rather than pure ROI.
Tools & Resources
Community engagement log (activity, date, organisation, estimated reach, cost). Event planning checklist. Brand ambassador guidelines for team members attending events.
