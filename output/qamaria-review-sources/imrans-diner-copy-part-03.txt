Probation review
Introduction
This chapter sets out the process for managing and reviewing employee probation periods at Imran’s Diner. There is no statutory maximum probation period in UK law (unlike some other jurisdictions); the length of the probationary period, and any shortened notice period during probation (e.g. 1 week), are set by the contract rather than by statute. Imran's Diner sets both deliberately short, for the reason given in the standards below. Probation is a two-way evaluation: the employer assesses suitability and the employee assesses whether the role meets their expectations.
Minimum Operating Standards
All new employees are subject to a probation period as stated in their employment contract. The standard Imran’s Diner probation period is 4 months. This is deliberately set below six months: from 1 January 2027 the qualifying period for an ordinary unfair dismissal claim falls from two years to six months, so a six-month probation would end at the exact point statutory protection begins.
Probation reviews must be conducted at: (a) the midpoint of the probation period, and (b) a final review held with enough time for any notice to expire before the employee reaches six months' continuous service. Where notice is paid in lieu rather than worked, statutory notice is added to the termination date when continuous service is calculated, so the final review is held earlier rather than later. On a 4-month probation period the final review takes place at month 3.
Reviews must assess: attendance and punctuality, adherence to food safety and hygiene standards, customer service quality, teamwork and attitude, competency progression against the training plan, and any conduct issues.
The review must be documented using the standard probation review form and signed by both the employee and the reviewing manager.
Outcomes: (a) Pass — confirmed in role, (b) Extend — probation extended with specific improvement targets and timeline (note: extending beyond the contractual probation end date requires the employee's agreement and a contract amendment), (c) Fail — employment terminated during probation with the contractual notice period. Termination during probation should still be based on objective, documented grounds (performance, conduct, or suitability for the role) — from 1 January 2027 an employee can bring an ordinary unfair dismissal claim after six months' continuous service, reduced from two years, so the protection now sits close behind the probation period and a fair, documented process is essential rather than advisable.
If termination during probation is being considered, a short meeting to discuss the decision should be held before it is finalised. Best practice (and the ACAS Code, where the process is treated as disciplinary in nature) gives the employee the right to be accompanied by a trade union representative or colleague.
All probation documentation must be retained in the employee's personnel file.
Roles & Responsibilities
Unit Manager (currently Owner): Conducts probation reviews; documents performance; recommends outcome.
Franchisee: Approves probation outcomes (especially extensions or terminations); attends the probation-outcome meeting if required.
Employee: Participates in reviews; raises any concerns about the role or working conditions.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) probation pass rate, (b) number of probation extensions and outcomes, (c) any probation-related disputes or claims. A consistently low pass rate may indicate recruitment or training issues rather than employee quality.
Tools & Resources
Probation review form (midpoint and end-of-probation versions). Probation-outcome meeting invitation template. Probation outcome letter templates (pass, extend, fail). Personnel file checklist.

3.4 Rotas & labour model
Demand forecasting & capacity assessment
Introduction
This chapter defines how Imran’s Diner forecasts customer demand and assesses the physical capacity of each unit. The labour model (see Chapter 3.4 — Labour-to-sales model) converts a sales forecast into staffing requirements, but that forecast has to come from somewhere. Without a disciplined demand forecast, the labour model either over-staffs (destroying margin) or under-staffs (destroying service). Capacity assessment answers a different but related question: can the kitchen and front-of-house physically handle the forecasted volume? This chapter also feeds directly into the expansion readiness criteria in Chapter 6.4 — if Site #1 is consistently capacity-constrained, that supports the case for Site #2; if it is demand-constrained, the answer is marketing or pricing, not a second unit.
Minimum Operating Standards
A weekly sales forecast must be prepared by the Unit Manager (currently Owner) each Thursday for the following week. The forecast must be broken down by day and by trading period (e.g. lunch, afternoon, evening) using the trading-period definitions established for the unit.
The forecast must be built from: (a) historical sales data from EPOS (same week prior year, same week prior month, trailing 4-week average), (b) known events or factors (local events, school holidays, weather forecast, planned promotions), and (c) trend adjustment (growth or decline trend over the trailing 12 weeks).
Until sufficient EPOS history is available (minimum 12 weeks of trading data), forecasts should be built using the initial business plan projections, adjusted weekly based on actual performance. The transition from projection-based to data-based forecasting must be documented. During those first 12 weeks the forecast is reviewed against actual trading every week by the Unit Manager with Head Office, and the plan-based projection is re-based whenever cumulative variance exceeds 15%. Soft-launch and opening-week actuals form the first data points.
Forecast accuracy must be measured weekly: actual revenue vs. forecast, expressed as a percentage variance. Target accuracy: within ±10% at daily level and ±5% at weekly level. Daily variance is expected to be wider than weekly, because day-level swings partly offset across the week; a unit that cannot hold ±5% weekly has a forecasting problem rather than a trading problem. Persistent over- or under-forecasting must be investigated and the forecasting method recalibrated.
Capacity assessment: each unit must have a documented maximum throughput capacity covering (a) kitchen output — maximum orders per hour the kitchen can produce without quality degradation, (b) front-of-house — maximum covers or counter transactions per hour based on available space, and (c) equipment bottlenecks — any single piece of equipment that constrains total output. Each unit's throughput capacity is documented by Head Office before opening and reviewed quarterly. The reference figures are measured at the Sheffield unit and issued as the baseline every new unit is assessed against.
When the demand forecast exceeds 85% of documented capacity for any trading period, the Unit Manager must flag this to the Franchise Owner and review mitigation options (adjusted prep schedule, additional staffing, menu simplification for peak, or extended trading hours to redistribute demand). 85% is a flag threshold, not an operating target: peaks within a trading period run materially above the period average, so a period forecast at 85% means the busiest part of it is at or beyond capacity. A unit that regularly forecasts above 80% for the same trading period has a structural capacity issue rather than a staffing one, and that belongs in the expansion assessment at Chapter 6.4.
Seasonal trading patterns must be documented once a full year of data is available. The seasonal profile feeds into annual budgeting (Chapter 6.3 — Annual planning process), recruitment planning, and stock ordering.
Roles & Responsibilities
Unit Manager: Prepares the weekly demand forecast; monitors forecast accuracy; documents capacity constraints; flags capacity threshold breaches.
Franchise Owner / Operator: Reviews weekly forecasts; approves mitigation actions when capacity thresholds are breached; uses forecast data for strategic planning and expansion assessment.
Head Office: Provides forecasting templates and methodology guidance; benchmarks forecast accuracy across units; uses aggregate demand data for network-level planning.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track weekly: (a) forecast accuracy — actual vs. predicted revenue by day and by week, (b) number of trading periods where demand exceeded the capacity threshold, (c) correlation between forecast and actual labour deployment (are rotas being built from the forecast or from habit?). Monthly: review forecast accuracy trend — improving accuracy indicates the model is learning; persistent inaccuracy indicates a methodology problem or an external factor not being captured. Quarterly: reassess documented capacity limits based on any changes to equipment, layout, or menu.
Tools & Resources
Weekly demand forecast template (spreadsheet — day × trading period matrix with prior-year, trailing-average, and adjustment columns). EPOS sales reports (historical data by day/hour). Capacity assessment worksheet (kitchen throughput, front-of-house limits, equipment constraints). Forecast accuracy tracker (weekly variance log).

3.5 HR policies & conduct
Code of conduct
Introduction
This chapter sets out the Imran’s Diner Code of Conduct, which defines the behavioural expectations for all team members, supervisors, managers, and franchise owners. The Code of Conduct underpins Imran’s Diner's culture and brand values, and provides the basis for the disciplinary process (see Chapter 3.5 — Disciplinary & grievance process).
Minimum Operating Standards
All Imran’s Diner team members are expected to: treat customers, colleagues, and suppliers with respect and courtesy; maintain high standards of personal hygiene and appearance (clean uniform, hair tied back, no excessive jewellery in food handling areas per food safety requirements); arrive on time for scheduled shifts and be ready to work at the start of the shift; follow all SOPs set out in this manual; handle company property, equipment, and stock with care; maintain confidentiality regarding business information, recipes, financial data, and customer information.
The following are examples of misconduct that may result in disciplinary action: repeated lateness or unauthorised absence, failure to follow food safety or hygiene procedures, rudeness or poor attitude towards customers or colleagues, use of mobile phones during working hours except during designated breaks, unauthorised use or removal of company property or stock, breach of data protection obligations (UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018).
The following are examples of gross misconduct that may result in summary dismissal without notice: theft or fraud, deliberate damage to property, being under the influence of alcohol or illegal substances at work, violent or threatening behaviour, serious breach of food safety procedures putting customers at risk, harassment or discrimination (in breach of the Equality Act 2010).
All team members must sign an acknowledgement that they have read and understood the Code of Conduct as part of their onboarding.
The Code of Conduct applies equally to all staff regardless of position or tenure.
Roles & Responsibilities
All Staff: Adhere to the Code of Conduct at all times while on Imran’s Diner premises or representing the brand.
Unit Manager (currently Owner): Models expected behaviour; addresses breaches promptly and consistently; ensures all team members have signed the acknowledgement.
Franchisee: Ensures the Code is enforced fairly; handles escalated conduct issues.
Head Office: Maintains and updates the Code; provides guidance on interpretation.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of Code of Conduct breaches logged per quarter, (b) nature and severity of breaches, (c) consistency of enforcement (same breach type should receive the same response regardless of who commits it). Review the Code annually and update as needed.

Disciplinary & grievance process
Introduction
This chapter defines the disciplinary and grievance procedures for Imran’s Diner. These processes must comply with the ACAS Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures and the Employment Rights Act 1996, and provide a fair, documented framework for addressing both employer-initiated concerns (disciplinary) and employee-initiated concerns (grievance). Getting this wrong exposes the business to unfair dismissal and constructive dismissal claims at Employment Tribunal, and a failure to follow the ACAS Code can increase any award by up to 25%.
Minimum Operating Standards
Disciplinary process — stages (following the ACAS Code): (1) Informal conversation (minor first-time issues — documented in a file note), (2) Formal verbal warning (documented, employee informed it is a warning), (3) Written warning (formal letter setting out the issue, expected improvement, timeline, and consequences of failure to improve), (4) Final written warning, (5) Dismissal (must be for a fair reason under s.98 Employment Rights Act 1996 and follow a fair process) or summary dismissal (without notice — only for gross misconduct).
Before any dismissal, a formal disciplinary hearing must be held. The employee must be given reasonable notice of the meeting, informed in writing of the matters to be discussed and the evidence, and offered the statutory right to be accompanied by a trade union representative or colleague (Employment Relations Act 1999, s.10). The meeting must be a genuine hearing, not a rubber stamp.
Dismissal notices must be in writing, delivered personally or by recorded post, and must inform the employee of their right to appeal and, where applicable, their right to bring an Employment Tribunal claim. Since 1 October 2026 the time limit for most Employment Tribunal claims has been 6 months, increased from 3 months by the Employment Rights Act 2025. The statutory minimum notice period is 1 week per complete year of service, up to 12 weeks (Employment Rights Act 1996, s.86), though the contract may specify a longer period.
Grievance process: any employee may raise a grievance about their working conditions, treatment, or any other workplace concern. Grievances should be raised with the Unit Manager (currently Owner) in the first instance; if the grievance concerns the Unit Manager, it should be raised with the Franchisee. The grievance must be acknowledged in writing within 5 working days, a grievance meeting held within 10 working days of receipt, and a written outcome provided within 5 working days of that meeting. Where any of these cannot be met, the employee must be told in writing before the deadline passes, with the reason and a revised date. A missed internal deadline is itself evidence of an unfair process.
All disciplinary and grievance records must be retained confidentially in the employee's personnel file. Access is restricted to the Franchisee and anyone the Franchisee has appointed to advise on the case (e.g. a retained HR advisory company).
Legal note: the Franchisee is the employer and is responsible for taking their own independent HR advice. Head Office can offer general guidance but does not provide accredited HR or employment-law advice. The first port of call is always Acas (acas.org.uk; helpline 0300 123 1100). Franchisees with persistent or complex HR issues should consider keeping an HR advisory company on retainer. UK employees build up increasing statutory protections with length of service, most notably unfair dismissal protection. The probation period is set at 4 months for this reason (see Chapter 3.4 — Probation review). If in any doubt about the lawfulness of a proposed dismissal, take legal advice before proceeding. The cost of an unfair dismissal finding can be substantial, including reinstatement, re-engagement, and compensation. Head Office must be informed of any formal disciplinary, grievance, dismissal or Employment Tribunal matter. From 1 January 2027, employees can claim unfair dismissal after 6 months’ service, reduced from 2 years (Employment Rights Act 2025).
Roles & Responsibilities
Unit Manager: Handles informal conversations and Stage 1-2 disciplinary matters; investigates and responds to grievances at unit level.
Franchisee: Handles Stage 3+ disciplinary matters and dismissals; chairs the disciplinary hearing; handles grievances escalated beyond Unit Manager.
Employee: Engages with the process in good faith; may be accompanied by a trade union representative or colleague at formal meetings.
Head Office: Is informed of formal disciplinary, grievance, dismissal and Employment Tribunal matters; offers general, non-accredited guidance and promotes consistency across franchise locations. Independent HR advice (Acas first, or a retained HR advisory company) is the Franchisee's responsibility.
Measure Analyse Improve
Track: (a) number of disciplinary cases by stage, (b) number of grievances raised and outcomes, (c) time to resolution, (d) any cases that result in legal claims. A high volume of disciplinary cases in one unit may indicate a management or culture issue rather than an employee issue.
Tools & Resources
Disciplinary letter templates (verbal warning, written warning, final written warning, dismissal). Disciplinary/grievance hearing invitation template. Grievance form. Investigation record template. Personnel file checklist. ACAS Code of Practice (reference copy). Acas helpline and website (acas.org.uk, 0300 123 1100).
Leave & absence policy
Introduction
This chapter sets out Imran’s Diner's leave and absence policies, covering annual leave, sick leave, and other statutory and discretionary leave types. These policies must comply with UK law, primarily the Working Time Regulations 1998 and the Employment Rights Act 1996.
Minimum Operating Standards
Annual leave: all employees are entitled to a statutory minimum of 5.6 weeks' paid annual leave per year (28 days for a standard 5-day week, capped at 28 days by statute) under the Working Time Regulations 1998; this can include bank holidays. The statutory minimum is the network standard. A franchisee may offer more than the statutory minimum at their own cost, but may not offer less. For staff with irregular hours or working part of the year, rolled-up holiday pay of 12.07% of pay may be used for leave years starting on or after 1 April 2024; staff with fixed regular hours should accrue leave in the standard way.
