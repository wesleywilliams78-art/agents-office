Status: Excluded — with one defined exception pathway.
Creamy does not operate in standard shopping centre units. This exclusion is based on the founder's direct experience (a shopping centre unit opened in 2018 was sold at a loss within approximately one year — see Chapter 1.1) and on structural factors that apply across the Norwegian market.
Reasons for exclusion:
(a) High rents and additional costs. Shopping centre leases in Norway typically carry higher base rents than equivalent high street positions, plus additional charges: common area maintenance (felleskostnader), marketing levies, and in many cases turnover-linked rent that penalises strong trading. For a dessert bar with Creamy's product mix and average transaction value, this cost structure erodes unit-level profitability to an unacceptable degree.
(b) No control over opening hours. Shopping centre tenants are required to operate during the centre's standard opening hours, which in Norway are typically short by international standards — often closing at 20:00 on weekdays and 18:00 on Saturdays. Critically, most Norwegian shopping centres are closed on Sundays (with limited exceptions around the Christmas trading period under helligdagsfredloven). Creamy's peak trading is evenings and weekends. A location that is closed when Creamy's customers are most likely to visit is fundamentally misaligned with the business model.
(c) Brand positioning conflict. Creamy's positioning as a dessert destination — a place customers deliberately choose to visit (see Chapter 1.3 — Positioning vs. Category) — is undermined by a shopping centre context where the visit is incidental to a broader shopping trip. The brand experience is different when a customer has come specifically to Creamy versus when they pass it on the way to H&M.
Exception — independent street entrance:
A shopping centre location may be considered if, and only if, all of the following conditions are met:
The unit has its own independent entrance directly from the street, separate from the shopping centre's internal access
The lease terms permit Creamy to set its own opening hours using the independent entrance, including evening hours beyond the centre's closing time and Sunday trading
The independent entrance provides adequate visibility and frontage (minimum two large windows — see Chapter 2.2)
The rent and cost structure, including all centre charges, delivers acceptable unit economics as assessed by Head Office
If all four conditions are confirmed in writing (in the lease heads of terms, not verbally), the site may proceed to the standard evaluation process. If any condition is not met, the site is excluded — there is no partial compliance pathway for this exception.

EXCLUSION 2 — FOOD COURTS
Status: Not excluded, but requires a bespoke operating model.
Food courts are not categorically excluded from Creamy's franchise model. However, a food court unit operates so differently from a standard Creamy unit that it cannot simply adopt the standard operating model and menu. A food court location requires Head Office to develop and approve a modified operating framework before the franchise agreement is signed.
Why food courts are different:
(a) Service format. Food court customers expect counter service with takeaway packaging. Creamy's standard model is a sit-down dessert bar experience with plated service. A food court unit must serve in disposable boxes and containers, which changes product presentation, portion logistics, and the customer's perception of the brand.
(b) Menu scope. The full Creamy menu may not be operationally viable in a food court format — limited kitchen space, different equipment configuration, and faster expected service times may require a reduced or adapted menu. Head Office must define which products are included in the food court menu and how they are adapted for the format.
(c) Brand experience. The Creamy "happy place" experience (see Chapter 1.4 — Core Values) is harder to deliver in a shared food court environment where Creamy does not control the seating, music, lighting, or overall atmosphere. The brand expression must shift from environment to product and direct customer interaction at the counter.
(d) Operating economics. Food court units typically have lower average transaction values than sit-down units but may compensate through higher volume. The business case for a food court unit must be modelled separately — standard Creamy P&L assumptions do not apply.
What Head Office must develop before a food court unit is approved:
Adapted menu for food court format (reduced menu, food court-suitable products, disposable packaging specifications)
Modified product presentation standards (how Creamy's quality and generosity values translate to disposable boxes rather than plated service)
Food court-specific operational procedures (prep workflow, service speed targets, packaging and labelling for takeaway)
Food court-specific unit economics model (revenue, cost, and margin assumptions distinct from the standard model)
Brand guidelines for food court units (signage, counter design, uniform — how the Creamy brand is expressed in a shared environment)
Franchise agreement addendum covering the food court-specific terms and operating model
Until these materials are developed and approved by Head Office, no food court franchise agreement should be signed. A prospective franchisee who proposes a food court location should be informed that the format is possible in principle, but that Head Office must complete the food court adaptation work before the site can proceed.

Regulatory/licensing requirements (Norway-specific)   [YSF ADVISORY — DRAFTED, PENDING REVIEW]
Introduction
This chapter sets out the regulatory and licensing requirements that must be satisfied before a new Creamy unit can legally commence trading in Norway. Norway has specific requirements for food service businesses covering food safety registration, establishment licences, and operator qualifications. Failure to obtain the correct registrations and licences before opening is a criminal offence and will result in enforcement action from Mattilsynet (the Norwegian Food Safety Authority) or the relevant municipality.
Minimum Operating Standards
Mattilsynet registration: Every food business in Norway must be registered with Mattilsynet before commencing operations (Matloven §§6-7). Registration is done via the Mattilsynet online portal (skjema.mattilsynet.no) and must be submitted at least 4 weeks before the planned opening date. This is a registration, not an approval — Mattilsynet does not issue a 'permit' but must be notified and may inspect. Right - Done - WW
IK-mat (internkontrollforskriften for næringsmidler): An internal control system for food safety must be established and documented before opening. This is a legal requirement under the IK-mat regulations (forskrift om internkontroll for å oppfylle næringsmiddellovgivningen). The system must cover HACCP principles, allergen management, cleaning routines, temperature control, traceability, and staff training. See Chapter 3.6 for detailed HACCP procedures. Right - done WW
Serveringsbevilling (food service licence): Required for any establishment that serves food or drink to the public (serveringsloven §3). Applied for from the relevant kommune (municipality). The applicant (or a designated daglig leder — daily manager) must have passed the etablererprøven (establishment test) before the licence can be granted. Right - Done WW
Etablererprøven: The establishment test covers food hygiene regulations, basic business law, and relevant sections of serveringsloven. It is administered by the kommune and must be passed by the person responsible for daily operations. This is non-negotiable — the kommune will not issue a serveringsbevilling without it. Right - Done WW
Næringsregisteret (business registration): The franchise entity must be registered in the Brønnøysund Register Centre (Brønnøysundregistrene) as a Norwegian business entity, with a valid organisasjonsnummer. Right - Done WW
Arbeidstilsynet (Labour Inspection Authority): If employing staff, the employer must comply with registration and reporting obligations under the Working Environment Act (arbeidsmiljøloven). No separate licence is needed, but the employer must have documented HMS (helse, miljø og sikkerhet — health, environment and safety) internkontroll in place. Right - Done WW
Fire safety: A fire risk assessment must be completed and documented before opening, and the unit must comply with forskrift om brannforebygging (fire prevention regulations). Contact the local brannvesen (fire service) if a pre-opening inspection is required. Right - Done WW
Insurance: Appropriate business insurance must be in place before trading, covering at a minimum: public liability, employer's liability (yrkesskadeforsikring — mandatory in Norway), property/contents, and business interruption. Right - Done WW

Introduction

Minimum Operating Standards
MANDATORY INSURANCE POLICIES
Every Creamy franchise unit must hold the following insurance policies, all placed through Tryg Forsikring via the Creamy group arrangement:

1. Yrkesskadeforsikring (Occupational Injury Insurance)
Status: Legally mandatory.
Under yrkesskadeforsikringsloven (the Occupational Injury Insurance Act), every Norwegian employer is legally required to hold occupational injury insurance covering all employees. This is not discretionary — operating without yrkesskadeforsikring is a criminal offence.
This insurance covers employees who suffer injury or illness as a result of an occupational accident or harmful exposure during work. It provides compensation for medical expenses, loss of income, and permanent disability arising from workplace incidents.
Key requirements:
Coverage must be in place from the first day any employee starts work — there is no grace period
All employees must be covered, including part-time and temporary staff
The employer (franchisee) bears the full cost of the premium — it cannot be deducted from employee wages
Arbeidstilsynet (the Labour Inspection Authority) can request proof of coverage during inspections
Failure to hold yrkesskadeforsikring means the franchisee is personally liable for all compensation that the insurance would have covered, in addition to facing regulatory sanctions. This is a non-negotiable compliance requirement (see Chapter 2.3 — Non-Negotiable Operating Standards).

2. Alminnelig ansvarsforsikring (General Liability Insurance)
Status: Not legally mandatory, but required by Creamy.
General liability insurance covers the business against claims from third parties (primarily customers) for bodily injury or property damage arising from the business's operations. In a food service environment, the most common scenarios include: a customer slipping on a wet floor, an allergic reaction to an undisclosed ingredient, injury from faulty furniture or equipment, or damage to a customer's property.
Key requirements:
Coverage must be sufficient to cover realistic claim scenarios in a customer-facing food service environment — [Adnan to confirm: whether a minimum sum insured is specified, e.g. NOK 10 million per claim]
The policy must cover the premises, products served, and any events or activities conducted by the unit (e.g. community events, sampling — see Chapter 4.1 and 4.2)
Product liability should be included or confirmed as part of this policy — if a customer becomes ill from a Creamy product, the claim falls under liability insurance

3. Fritidsulykkesforsikring (Leisure/Off-Duty Accident Insurance)
Status: Not legally mandatory, but required by Creamy.
This insurance covers employees for accidental injury that occurs outside of working hours — during their leisure time. Yrkesskadeforsikring only covers injuries at work; fritidsulykkesforsikring extends accident coverage to the employee's private life.
This is an employee benefit, not a legal requirement. Creamy requires it for two reasons: it is a meaningful staff benefit that supports recruitment and retention (particularly important in the competitive Norwegian hospitality labour market), and it demonstrates that Creamy takes a genuine interest in employee wellbeing beyond the minimum legal obligation — consistent with the "Create Happiness" value (see Chapter 1.4), which applies to the team as well as customers.
Key requirements:
All employees must be covered, including part-time staff
The employer (franchisee) bears the cost
Coverage terms to be as per the Tryg Forsikring group arrangement

4. Næringsforsikring (Business/Commercial Insurance)
Status: Not legally mandatory in full, but required by Creamy.
Næringsforsikring is a bundled commercial insurance product that typically covers a range of business risks under a single policy. Through Tryg, the Creamy næringsforsikring package should include (as a minimum):
Brannforsikring (fire insurance): Coverage for fire, explosion, and related damage to the premises, equipment, and stock. If the franchisee leases the premises, the lease will almost certainly require proof of fire insurance — and the landlord's building insurance does not cover the tenant's fit-out, equipment, or stock.
Vannskade (water damage): Coverage for flooding, pipe bursts, and water damage to equipment and stock.
Innbrudd og tyveri (burglary and theft): Coverage for break-in damage, theft of equipment, stock, and cash.
Driftsavbrudd (business interruption): Coverage for lost revenue during a period when the unit cannot trade due to an insured event (fire, flood, major equipment damage). This is critical — a unit that suffers a fire and has no business interruption cover faces a double loss: the physical damage plus weeks or months of zero revenue while repairs are completed.
Maskinforsikring (machinery/equipment insurance): Coverage for breakdown, damage, or failure of commercial kitchen equipment. Given the cost of commercial equipment (see Chapter 2.5 — Kitchen Equipment Requirements), an uninsured equipment loss can be financially devastating for a single-unit franchisee.

INSURANCE ADMINISTRATION
(a) Placement through Tryg Forsikring. All required insurance must be placed through Tryg Forsikring using the Creamy group arrangement. This ensures consistent coverage terms across the network, access to the negotiated group discount, and allows Head Office to verify compliance centrally. Franchisees must not arrange their own insurance independently without Head Office approval — an independently arranged policy may have different terms, exclusions, or limits that create coverage gaps.
(b) Proof of insurance. Franchisees must provide Head Office with proof of insurance (policy schedule or certificate of insurance) before the unit opens and annually upon renewal. A unit must not open or continue to trade without valid insurance in place.
(c) Policy review. Insurance coverage should be reviewed annually at renewal to ensure: sums insured remain adequate (particularly equipment value, which depreciates, and stock value, which may increase with menu expansion), the business description on the policy accurately reflects current operations, and any changes to the business (new activities, additional premises, increased headcount) are notified to Tryg.
(d) Claims reporting. Any incident that may give rise to an insurance claim must be reported to Tryg promptly and to Head Office simultaneously. Late reporting can jeopardise coverage. The Unit Manager should document the incident immediately (photographs, witness details, written account) even before a claim decision is made.

Roles & Responsibilities
Creamy Head Office: Maintains the Tryg Forsikring group arrangement. Provides franchisees with contact details and instructions for placing insurance during onboarding. Verifies proof of insurance before unit opening and at annual renewal. Reviews the insurance requirements annually and updates this chapter as needed.
Franchise Owner / Operator: Bears the cost of all required insurance. Arranges insurance through Tryg using the Creamy group arrangement. Provides proof of insurance to Head Office. Ensures coverage is maintained without lapse. Reports incidents that may give rise to claims. Reviews coverage annually at renewal.
Unit Manager: Ensures the unit's certificate of insurance is available on-site. Reports any incident that could become a claim (customer injury, equipment damage, break-in, fire, flood) to the franchisee and Head Office immediately. Maintains an incident log.
Team Members: Report any incident (customer injury, property damage, theft, equipment failure, slip, fall) to the Unit Manager immediately. Do not admit liability or make promises to customers about compensation — direct them to the Unit Manager.

Measure, Analyse, Improve
(a) Insurance compliance rate. Track the percentage of franchise units with valid, verified insurance across all four required policies. Target: 100% at all times. Non-compliance is a serious risk to the franchisee and to the brand.
(b) Claims history. Track claims across the network by type (liability, property, equipment, business interruption). Claims data reveals operational risks — a high frequency of slip-and-fall claims may indicate a cleaning or floor maintenance issue; repeated equipment claims may indicate a specification or maintenance problem.
(c) Cost benchmarking. Monitor insurance premiums across franchisees annually. The group arrangement should deliver cost benefits — if premiums are rising significantly, review whether claims history is the driver and whether operational improvements could reduce the risk profile.
(d) Coverage adequacy review. At least annually, assess whether the required coverage levels remain adequate. As menu and equipment specifications evolve, the insured values should keep pace.
